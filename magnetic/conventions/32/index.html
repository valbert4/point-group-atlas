32 Corepresentations ¶

32.1 The case of an irrep ¶

A group 𝖬\mathsf{M} of magnetic type II or III has an antiunitary representative 𝘢\mathsfit{a}, named in its record. The representative is Θ\varTheta in magnetic type II, and in magnetic type III the antiunitary element over the element of least index of 𝖦∖𝖧\mathsf{G} \setminus \mathsf{H}. The field representative holds the index of that element. Its corepresentations [Wig59, chapter 26] are built from the irreps Λ\Lambda of 𝖧\mathsf{H}, with their matrices DΛ(𝘩)D^{\Lambda}(\mathsfit{h}) of section 9.

The conjugate irrep Λ𝘢\Lambda^{\mathsfit{a}} of Λ\Lambda has the matrices DΛ𝘢(𝘩)=DΛ(𝘢𝘩𝘢−1)∗D^{\Lambda^{\mathsfit{a}}}(\mathsfit{h}) = D^{\Lambda}(\mathsfit{a}\mathsfit{h}\mathsfit{a}^{-1})^{*}, where 𝘢𝘩𝘢−1\mathsfit{a}\mathsfit{h}\mathsfit{a}^{-1} is the product that applies 𝘢\mathsfit{a} first, read in the multiplication table of 𝖦\mathsf{G} (section 4.1). The indicator of Λ\Lambda is

(32.1)ι=1∣𝖧∣∑𝘨∈𝖦∖𝖧χΛ(𝘨2),\iota = \frac{1}{\lvert \mathsf{H}\rvert} \sum_{\mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{G} \setminus \mathsf{H}} \chi_{\Lambda}(\mathsfit{g}^2),

the sum over 𝖦\mathsf{G} in magnetic type II, where it is the Frobenius–Schur indicator νFS\nu_{\mathrm{FS}} of section 7. It gives the case of Λ\Lambda [DW62a]:

  1. Case (a), ι=+1\iota = +1: Λ𝘢\Lambda^{\mathsfit{a}} is equivalent to Λ\Lambda, and Λ\Lambda alone forms a corepresentation of the same dimension.
  2. Case (b), ι=−1\iota = -1: Λ𝘢\Lambda^{\mathsfit{a}} is equivalent to Λ\Lambda, and two copies of Λ\Lambda form one corepresentation.
  3. Case (c), ι=0\iota = 0: Λ𝘢\Lambda^{\mathsfit{a}} is equivalent to another irrep of 𝖧\mathsf{H}, the partner of Λ\Lambda, and the two form one corepresentation.

The complex matrices that commute with every matrix of a corepresentation form a copy of ℝ\mathbb{R}, ℍ\mathbb{H} or ℂ\mathbb{C} in the cases (a), (b) and (c). In magnetic type II no irrep is pseudoreal (section 7), so a real irrep is of case (a) and a complex irrep is of case (c), with its complex-conjugate partner (section 6.4).

The field unitary_irreps names the irreps of 𝖧\mathsf{H}, and corepresentations lists the corepresentations in the irrep order of 𝖧\mathsf{H}, a pair of case (c) under its first irrep. Each entry has these fields:

  1. irrep: the id of its first irrep Λ\Lambda.
  2. case and, in case (c), partner: the case and the id of the partner.
  3. indicator (magnetic type III): ι\iota. In magnetic type II it is the field frobenius_schur of the irrep (section 7).
  4. N: the matrix NN (section 32.2) of the cases (a) and (b).

32.2 The matrices ¶

A corepresentation 𝒟\mathcal{D} is written in the order of operators: the product that applies 𝘨\mathsfit{g} first, then 𝘩\mathsfit{h}, is 𝒟(𝘩) 𝒟(𝘨)\mathcal{D}(\mathsfit{h})\, \mathcal{D}(\mathsfit{g}). A unitary element acts by the transposes DΛ(𝘩)⊤D^{\Lambda}(\mathsfit{h})^{\top} of the matrices of section 9, and the representative by a unitary matrix times the complex conjugation KK. In the cases (a) and (b) the matrix NN is unitary, with

(32.2)DΛ(𝘩)⊤N=N DΛ𝘢(𝘩)⊤for every 𝘩∈𝖧,NN∗=ι DΛ(𝘢2)⊤.D^{\Lambda}(\mathsfit{h})^{\top} N = N\, D^{\Lambda^{\mathsfit{a}}}(\mathsfit{h})^{\top} \quad \text{for every } \mathsfit{h} \in \mathsf{H}, \qquad N N^{*} = \iota\, D^{\Lambda}(\mathsfit{a}^2)^{\top}.

It is unique up to a phase. In magnetic type III the record holds it as N=ℰ/𝒩N = \mathcal{E}/\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}, with the phase and the scale of section 10.3, in the fields matrix (ℰ\mathcal{E}) and N (𝒩\mathcal{N}) of N. In magnetic type II it is d\sqrt{d} times the block of Λ⊗Λ\Lambda \otimes \Lambda into the trivial irrep (section 10.3), with row (μ1,μ2)(\mu_1, \mu_2) of the block as row μ1\mu_1 and column μ2\mu_2 of NN. This block is real, and the record names it by a pointer. The representative acts as follows in the three cases:

  1. Case (a): 𝒟(𝘩)=DΛ(𝘩)⊤\mathcal{D}(\mathsfit{h}) = D^{\Lambda}(\mathsfit{h})^{\top} and 𝒟(𝘢)=NK\mathcal{D}(\mathsfit{a}) = N K.
  2. Case (b): 𝒟(𝘩)=DΛ(𝘩)⊤⊕DΛ(𝘩)⊤\mathcal{D}(\mathsfit{h}) = D^{\Lambda}(\mathsfit{h})^{\top} \oplus D^{\Lambda}(\mathsfit{h})^{\top} and 𝒟(𝘢)=(0N−N0)K\mathcal{D}(\mathsfit{a}) = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & N \\ -N & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right) K.
  3. Case (c): 𝒟(𝘩)=DΛ(𝘩)⊤⊕DΛ𝘢(𝘩)⊤\mathcal{D}(\mathsfit{h}) = D^{\Lambda}(\mathsfit{h})^{\top} \oplus D^{\Lambda^{\mathsfit{a}}}(\mathsfit{h})^{\top} and 𝒟(𝘢)=(0DΛ(𝘢2)⊤10)K\mathcal{D}(\mathsfit{a}) = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & D^{\Lambda}(\mathsfit{a}^2)^{\top} \\ 1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right) K.

Every other antiunitary element is 𝘩𝘢\mathsfit{h}\mathsfit{a}, the product that applies 𝘩\mathsfit{h} first, with 𝒟(𝘩𝘢)=𝒟(𝘢) 𝒟(𝘩)\mathcal{D}(\mathsfit{h}\mathsfit{a}) = \mathcal{D}(\mathsfit{a})\, \mathcal{D}(\mathsfit{h}).

Where a function moves with the points (section 4.3), the corepresentation on the same kets is the complex conjugate of 𝒟\mathcal{D}: a unitary element 𝘩\mathsfit{h} acts by DΛ(𝘩)†D^{\Lambda}(\mathsfit{h})^{\dagger}, and N∗N^{*} replaces NN.

32.3 Labels ¶

A corepresentation is named by its irreps of 𝖧\mathsf{H}, with its case beside the name: Λ\Lambda in case (a), ΛΛ\Lambda\Lambda in case (b), and in case (c) Λ\Lambda followed by its partner, such as 1E 2E{}^1\mathrm{\scriptsize E}\,{}^2\mathrm{\scriptsize E}. In case (c) the first irrep in the order of the record of 𝖧\mathsf{H} comes first. The dimension of a corepresentation is the sum of those of its irreps.
