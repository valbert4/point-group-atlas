35 Tensors ¶

A tensor property odd under time reversal has a Jahn symbol with the letter a. The record reads the components of its tensor in spaces of cartesian_tensors (section 13.2), each at an irrep of 𝖦\mathsf{G}. That irrep is χ\chi where the spaces are those of the tensor. It is χ⊗det⁡\chi \otimes \det where they are those of the tensor without its axial factor e, since that factor multiplies each component by det⁡𝘨\det \mathsfit{g}. The components that 𝖬\mathsf{M} allows are those of the copies of that irrep in each space.

The field group.allowed_properties lists the properties that 𝖬\mathsf{M} allows, those whose spaces hold a copy of their irrep, each with jahn, spaces, irrep and properties. The field irrep is the id of χ\chi or of χ⊗det⁡\chi \otimes \det, and a space that holds two parts of the tensor is listed twice. 35.1 Invariants ¶

The field invariants holds the invariants of 𝖬\mathsf{M} in the vectors of the four kinds of section 31.2, alone and in one pair. It has these fields:

  1. rings: for each kind, the pointer to the field invariants (section 13.1) of the record of its image, or the null outside-the-catalogue. The frame of the image carries these invariants into the frame of the record of 𝖬\mathsf{M}.
  2. multipoles: one entry for each kind of multipole and each rank up to 4, with kind, J (the rank) and multiplicity, the pointer to its multiplicity in the field subduction_o3 of 𝖦\mathsf{G} (section 11).
  3. bases: the pointer to the symmetry-adapted bases of 𝖦\mathsf{G} (section 14), whose blocks give the forms of the multipoles, each a hermitian operator.
  4. two_vectors: the invariants in a polar vector even under time reversal and an axial vector odd under time reversal, such as an electric polarization and a magnetization, with kinds, counts and polynomials.

A multipole of rank JJ is a copy of an irrep of 𝖦\mathsf{G} in D(J,p)D^{(J,p)} (section 11). Its kind fixes the irrep and the parity pp:

  1. electric: the trivial irrep and p=(−1)Jp = (-1)^{J}.
  2. electric-toroidal: the trivial irrep and p=−(−1)Jp = -(-1)^{J}.
  3. magnetic-toroidal: χ\chi and p=(−1)Jp = (-1)^{J}.
  4. magnetic: χ\chi and p=−(−1)Jp = -(-1)^{J}.

A group of magnetic type II has the first two kinds only. The operators on a spin that 𝖬\mathsf{M} allows are the electric multipoles of even rank and the magnetic multipoles of odd rank.

The field kinds of two_vectors is ["polar_even", "axial_odd"], and 𝖬\mathsf{M} moves the two vectors as in section 31.2. A monomial key lists the exponents of the components xx, yy, zz of the polar vector, then of the axial vector, such as "1,0,0;0,0,1" for the product of the polar xx and the axial zz. Each entry of counts gives count, the number of independent invariants of degree a in the polar vector and b in the axial vector. The degrees a and b are at least 1 and their sum is at most 6. The field polynomials gives the basis of these invariants for a sum of degrees at most 4. It is the reduced row echelon basis in the monomials ordered by their keys descending, each vector scaled to coprime integer coordinates with a positive pivot, as in section 13.
