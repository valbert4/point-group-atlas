38 Products and Clebsch–Gordan coefficients ¶

The product 𝒟1⊗𝒟2\mathcal{D}_{1} \otimes \mathcal{D}_{2} of two corepresentations of 𝖬\mathsf{M} is a representation of 𝖬\mathsf{M}. A unitary element acts on it by the Kronecker product of the matrices of section 32.2, and the representative 𝘢\mathsfit{a} by U1⊗U2U_{1} \otimes U_{2} times KK, where 𝒟1(𝘢)=U1K\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathsfit{a}) = U_{1} K and 𝒟2(𝘢)=U2K\mathcal{D}_{2}(\mathsfit{a}) = U_{2} K. Its decomposition into corepresentations follows from the characters on 𝖧\mathsf{H} by the formula of section 34. The field fusion of a finite group has these fields:

  1. corepresentations: the ids of the corepresentations, those of their first irreps, in the order of corepresentations (section 32.1).
  2. products: one for each pair 𝒟1≤𝒟2\mathcal{D}_{1} \le \mathcal{D}_{2} in that order, with a and b, the ids of 𝒟1\mathcal{D}_{1} and 𝒟2\mathcal{D}_{2}, and terms. A term is a corepresentation and its multiplicity, which is never 0, and the terms follow the same order.

A copy of a corepresentation 𝒟\mathcal{D} in 𝒟1⊗𝒟2\mathcal{D}_{1} \otimes \mathcal{D}_{2} is a block CC of d1d2d_{1} d_{2} rows and dd columns, where d1d_{1}, d2d_{2} and dd are the dimensions of 𝒟1\mathcal{D}_{1}, 𝒟2\mathcal{D}_{2} and 𝒟\mathcal{D}. Its rows (μ1,μ2)(\mu_1, \mu_2) are in the Kronecker order of section 10.3, and the block satisfies

(38.1)(𝒟1(𝘩)⊤⊗𝒟2(𝘩)⊤) C=C 𝒟(𝘩)⊤for every 𝘩∈𝖧,(U1⊗U2) C=C∗U,(\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathsfit{h})^{\top} \otimes \mathcal{D}_{2}(\mathsfit{h})^{\top})\, C = C\, \mathcal{D}(\mathsfit{h})^{\top} \quad \text{for every } \mathsfit{h} \in \mathsf{H}, \qquad (U_{1} \otimes U_{2})\, C = C^{*} U,

where 𝒟(𝘢)=UK\mathcal{D}(\mathsfit{a}) = U K. The first equation is that of section 10.3, with the transposes of the matrices of section 32.2 in the place of those of section 9. The blocks of one product form a unitary matrix.

The first dΛd_{\Lambda} columns CΛC_{\Lambda} of a copy are those of the first irrep Λ\Lambda of 𝒟\mathcal{D}, and they form a copy of Λ\Lambda in the restriction of the product to 𝖧\mathsf{H}. The second equation gives the other columns from them. In case (b) the columns of the second Λ\Lambda are −((U1⊗U2) CΛN−1)∗-((U_{1} \otimes U_{2})\, C_{\Lambda} N^{-1})^{*}, and in case (c) those of Λ𝘢\Lambda^{\mathsfit{a}} are ((U1⊗U2) CΛ)∗((U_{1} \otimes U_{2})\, C_{\Lambda})^{*}.

38.1 The copies ¶

The copies of 𝒟\mathcal{D} in a product form one part, or two when 𝒟1=𝒟2\mathcal{D}_{1} = \mathcal{D}_{2}: the copies symmetric under the exchange of the two factors, C[(μ1,μ2),μ]=C[(μ2,μ1),μ]C[(\mu_1, \mu_2), \mu] = C[(\mu_2, \mu_1), \mu], then the antisymmetric ones. The first of these rules that applies selects the copies of a part:

  1. Inheritance. A part of two copies is inherited when every copy of Λ\Lambda in the restriction to 𝖧\mathsf{H} lies on the rows of one pair of irreps of 𝒟1\mathcal{D}_{1} and 𝒟2\mathcal{D}_{2}. The product of that pair must hold Λ\Lambda twice in the table of 𝖧\mathsf{H} (section 10.3). The copies are then the two blocks of that table, in its order and with the separations of section 10.4, each times its phase.
  2. Row order. Otherwise the projection rule selects the copies one by one.

The phase of a block of the table of 𝖧\mathsf{H} is 1 in case (c). In case (a) it is the phase of positive real part, or i\mathrm{i}, with which the block satisfies the second equation.

The projection rule takes the unit columns in row order. Column 1 of the next copy is the normalized projection of the first unit column with a nonzero projection on the allowed columns. These are the columns 1 of the blocks of the part that satisfy both equations and are orthogonal to the copies already selected. Orthogonal means over ℂ\mathbb{C} in case (c). In case (b) it means over ℍ\mathbb{H}: the allowed columns are also orthogonal to the column dΛ+1d_{\Lambda} + 1 of each earlier copy. In case (a) it means over ℝ\mathbb{R}, and i\mathrm{i} times each unit column follows it. The two equations give the other columns of the copy.

At multiplicity one a copy is unique up to a sign in case (a), a unit quaternion in case (b) and a phase in case (c). The projection rule fixes it by these forms:

  1. Case (a): the first nonzero entry of column 1 has a positive real part, or is i\mathrm{i} times a positive number.
  2. Case (b): column 1 is real and positive in its first nonzero row, and column dΛ+1d_{\Lambda} + 1 is 0 in that row.
  3. Case (c): the first nonzero entry of column 1 is real and positive, as in section 10.3.

A block is stored as ℰ/𝒩\mathcal{E}/\sqrt{\mathcal{N}} with the scale of section 10.3, ℰ\mathcal{E} a positive rational multiple of the block, so its phase is that of the copy. Every copy of case (a) has the form of its case at multiplicity one, and the phase of section 10.3 holds in the cases (b) and (c). The field separation of a copy takes these values:

  1. "unique": 𝒟\mathcal{D} occurs once in the product.
  2. "exchange-symmetric" and "exchange-antisymmetric": the one copy of its part, or an inherited copy as in section 10.4.
  3. "crossing-symmetric", "crossing-antisymmetric", "so3-parent-lowest" and "so3-parent-complement": an inherited copy, as in section 10.4.
  4. "row order": a copy of the projection rule in the one part of the product.
  5. "exchange-symmetric, row order": a copy of the projection rule in the symmetric part.

38.2 The field clebsch_gordan ¶

The field clebsch_gordan of a finite group (kind "table") has corepresentations, as in fusion, and products in the order of fusion.products. A product has a, b and couplings, one for each copy, by 𝒟\mathcal{D} in the order of the corepresentations and then by κ\kappa. A coupling has c, the id of 𝒟\mathcal{D}, kappa (κ=1,2,…\kappa = 1, 2, \dots) and separation, and then one of these pairs of fields:

  1. pointer and phase: a copy whose columns CΛC_{\Lambda} are a block of the table of 𝖧\mathsf{H} times 1 or i\mathrm{i}, on the rows of the first irreps of 𝒟1\mathcal{D}_{1} and 𝒟2\mathcal{D}_{2}. These columns are 0 on the other rows. The pointer names the coupling of that block in the record of 𝖧\mathsf{H} (section 30.3), and the phase is "1" or "i".
  2. N and partners: every other copy, stored as in section 10.3 with all its columns.

The second equation gives the other columns of a copy that a pointer names, as in section 38. A document sets the products in a table, each square with its antisymmetrized square in braces (section 34). It sets every block that its record stores, with κ\kappa and the separation where 𝒟\mathcal{D} occurs more than once. A copy that a pointer names is not set: the document names the section of the document of 𝖧\mathsf{H} that sets its block, and it lists the couplings of phase i\mathrm{i}.
