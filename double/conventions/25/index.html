25 Subduction ¶

25.1 Subduction from the parent ¶

The irrep D(J,p)D^{(J,p)} of 𝖯𝗂𝗇−(𝟥)\mathsf{Pin^{-}(3)}, of angular momentum J=0,12,1,…J = 0, \tfrac{1}{2}, 1, \dots and parity p=±1p = \pm 1, is DJ(U)D^{J}(U) on (U,ε)(U, \varepsilon), times ε\varepsilon when p=−1p = -1. At integer JJ it is the irrep of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} pulled back, whose subduction is that of 𝖦\mathsf{G} (integer_J, section 11). The field subduction_parent gives the multiplicities bΓ(J,p)b_{\Gamma}(J, p) of the spinor irreps at half-integer JJ with 2J≤412J \le 41, for p=+1p = +1 (g) and p=−1p = -1 (u): table[k] at J=k+12J = k + \tfrac{1}{2}, and

(25.1)bΓ(J,p)=residues[(J−12) mod period][Γ]+increment[Γ]⋅⌊(J−12)/period⌋.b_\Gamma(J, p) = \texttt{residues}[(J - \tfrac{1}{2}) \bmod \texttt{period}][\Gamma] + \texttt{increment}[\Gamma] \cdot \lfloor (J - \tfrac{1}{2})/\texttt{period} \rfloor.

25.2 Spin–orbit levels ¶

A term 2S+1Γ{}^{2S+1}\Gamma is an irrep Γ\Gamma of 𝖦\mathsf{G} with the spin SS (section 19), a spin even under the inversion. Under spin–orbit coupling it splits into the irreps of 𝟤𝖦\mathsf{2G} in (Γ⊗D(S,+1))↓𝟤𝖦(\Gamma \otimes D^{(S,+1)})\downarrow \mathsf{2G}: spinor irreps at half-integer SS and vector irreps at integer SS. The field spin_orbit_levels gives them for S=12,1,…,72S = \tfrac{1}{2}, 1, \dots, \tfrac{7}{2}, the nonzero spins of a d or f configuration (a singlet keeps its level), from the products of section 24 and the multiplicities of section 25.1. It has twoS_range and levels, one for each vector irrep Γ\Gamma (orbital, orbital_id) at each twoS, with the irreps of the restriction (terms: irrep, irrep_id, multiplicity).

25.3 jj configurations ¶

The field jj_configurations gives the configurations of electrons in a spinor level, the branching of section 19 along a level of one electron with its spin. By the rule of time reversal on spinors, a spinor level is a Kramers level: a pseudoreal spinor irrep alone, a real one twice, a complex-conjugate pair together (members, kind, dim). In a level of dimension dd, nn electrons fill its exterior power Λn\Lambda^{n}, n=0,…,dn = 0, \dots, d (powers: n and the multiplicities terms): vector irreps at even nn, spinor irreps at odd nn. Its character follows from the characters of the level on the powers of each element by Newton's identities, and Λd−n\Lambda^{d-n} is Λn⊗Λd\Lambda^{n} \otimes \Lambda^{d}.

Continuous groups. The continuous preimages give the levels of their instances up to 72\tfrac{7}{2}, and in SU2 the configurations are those of nn electrons in the subshells of s, p, d and f electrons.

25.4 Spin–orbitals ¶

The field spin_orbitals gives the states of one electron of orbital angular momentum l≤3l \le 3 (shells: s, p, d, f), the space D(l,(−1)l)⊗D(1/2,+1)D^{(l,(-1)^l)} \otimes D^{(1/2,+1)}. They are written in the real solid harmonics ff of section 13 (functions) times the spin kets u=∣ 1/21/2⟩u = \bigl\lvert{}^{1/2}_{\,1/2}\bigr\rangle and v=∣ −1/21/2⟩v = \bigl\lvert{}^{1/2}_{\,-1/2}\bigr\rangle. A vector lists the nonzero coefficients cc of ∑c f s\sum c\, f\, s over the functions ff and the spin kets ss (function, twoMs, c), up to a positive factor. They are coprime Gaussian integers when they are Gaussian rationals up to one positive factor, and otherwise divided by their positive rational content. With the Fischer product ⟨f,f⟩\langle f, f\rangle of section 13.1, the amplitude of the normalized ff times ss is c⟨f,f⟩/totc\sqrt{\langle f, f\rangle/\mathrm{tot}}, tot=∑∣c∣2⟨f,f⟩\mathrm{tot} = \sum \lvert c\rvert^2 \langle f, f\rangle.

A finite preimage gives the LS form (form "LS") for each orbital level, the isotypic component of a vector irrep Γ\Gamma of 𝖦\mathsf{G} among the functions of degree ll (orbital, orbital_id, multiplicity). It gives the copies κ\kappa of the spinor irreps in that component times the spin, built as the blocks of section 26.3. Here (𝘨,b)(\mathsfit{g}, b) acts by 𝘨\mathsfit{g} on the functions (section 4.1) and by section 26.1 on the spin. The coordinates are in the order of the functions, uu before vv for each, and each copy's partners μ\mu follow the partner law with the record's matrices.

Time reversal conjugates the coefficients and sends fuf u to fvf v and fvf v to −fu-f u, each ff being real. In the level of a real irrep Γ\Gamma the copies are adapted as the blocks of section 26.1. In case (a) they come from the real form of section 14.2, and in case (b) they come in pairs. The first copy of a pair is the next copy of the reduced row echelon basis less its projections on the pairs before it, in the phase form. Time reversal takes the level of the first irrep Γ\Gamma of a complex pair onto the level of its partner. The copies in the first level have the phase form. The copy v(2)v^{(2)} of the second level is the image of the copy v(1)v^{(1)} of the first with the same κ\kappa. It has Θv(1)=v(2)N\varTheta v^{(1)} = v^{(2)} N in case (a) and Θv(1)=−v(2)N\varTheta v^{(1)} = -v^{(2)} N in case (b). In case (c) it is Θv(1)\varTheta v^{(1)}, a copy of the partner of Λ\Lambda.

Continuous groups. The axial continuous preimages 𝟤𝖢∞\mathsf{2C_{\infty}}, …, 𝟤𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{2D_{\infty h}} give the LS form of 𝟤𝖢𝟣𝟤\mathsf{2C_{12}}, 𝟤𝖢𝟣𝟤𝗁\mathsf{2C_{12h}}, 𝟤𝖢𝟣𝟤𝗏\mathsf{2C_{12v}}, 𝟤𝖣𝟣𝟤\mathsf{2D_{12}}, 𝟤𝖣𝟣𝟤𝗁\mathsf{2D_{12h}} respectively, whose irreps of index at most 72\tfrac{7}{2} are the restrictions of the instances, with their matrices (section 29.2). The record SU2 gives the jj form (form "jj"): the subshells j=l∓12j = l \mp \tfrac{1}{2} (subshells, twoj =2j= 2j), and for each twoM the state ∣l j m⟩=∑⟨l ml 12 ms∣j m⟩∣l ml⟩∣12 ms⟩\lvert l\,j\,m\rangle = \sum \langle l\, m_l\, \tfrac{1}{2}\, m_s \vert j\, m\rangle \lvert l\, m_l\rangle \lvert \tfrac{1}{2}\, m_s\rangle. The sum uses the kets ∣l ml⟩\lvert l\, m_l\rangle of section 4 and the coefficients of section 29.3 in this order of the factors (its tables put the spin first, and ⟨l ml 12 ms∣j m⟩=(−1)l+1/2−j⟨12 ms l ml∣j m⟩\langle l\, m_l\, \tfrac{1}{2}\, m_s \vert j\, m\rangle = (-1)^{l+1/2-j} \langle \tfrac{1}{2}\, m_s\, l\, m_l \vert j\, m\rangle). As Θ∣l j m⟩=(−1)l(−1)j−m∣l j −m⟩\varTheta \lvert l\, j\, m\rangle = (-1)^{l} (-1)^{j-m} \lvert l\, j\, -m\rangle, a state of odd ll is multiplied by −i-\mathrm{i}, so that every state is adapted to time reversal (section 26.1). The state m=jm = j of each subshell, its partner 1, then has the phase form of section 14. The record Pinminus3 names these states with the parity (−1)l(-1)^l (parity_rule).
