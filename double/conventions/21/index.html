21.1 Lifts ¶

The lift of the rotation 𝘙𝐯(ω)\mathsfit{R}_{\mathbf{v}}(\omega) to 𝖲𝖴(𝟤)\mathsf{SU(2)} is R~𝐯(ω)=cos⁡ω2−isin⁡ω2 𝐯^⋅σ⃗\tilde{R}_{\mathbf{v}}(\omega) = \cos\tfrac{\omega}{2} - \mathrm{i}\sin\tfrac{\omega}{2}\,\hat{\mathbf{v}}\cdot\vec{\sigma} with 0≤ω<4π0 \le \omega < 4\pi, and R~𝐯(ω+2π)=𝘌ˉ R~𝐯(ω)\tilde{R}_{\mathbf{v}}(\omega + 2\pi) = \bar{\mathsfit{E}}\,\tilde{R}_{\mathbf{v}}(\omega). The canonical lift U(𝘨)U(\mathsfit{g}) of an element 𝘨\mathsfit{g} of a point group is the lift of its rotation part (det⁡𝘨) 𝘨(\det \mathsfit{g})\,\mathsfit{g} with nonnegative scalar part. At scalar part 0 (a half turn), the element that names its class (section 5.2) takes R~𝐯(π)\tilde{R}_{\mathbf{v}}(\pi) about its pole. The other elements of the class take the lift conjugate to it, so the canonical lifts of a class lie in one class of the preimage. Then U(𝘩) U(𝘨)=(−1)c(𝘨,𝘩) U(𝘨𝘩)U(\mathsfit{h})\,U(\mathsfit{g}) = (-1)^{c(\mathsfit{g},\mathsfit{h})}\,U(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{h}), in the order of section 4.1.

21.2 The elements of a preimage ¶

The element (𝘨,b)(\mathsfit{g}, b) of 𝟤𝖦\mathsf{2G}, b=0b = 0 or 1, is ((−1)b U(𝘨),det⁡𝘨)((-1)^{b}\,U(\mathsfit{g}), \det \mathsfit{g}). Its index is 2k+b2k + b for the element 𝘨\mathsfit{g} of index kk of the record of 𝖦\mathsf{G} (base, bar), and 𝘌ˉ\bar{\mathsfit{E}} has index 1 (ebar_index). The product is

(21.1)(𝘨,a)(𝘩,b)=(𝘨𝘩, a+b+c(𝘨,𝘩) mod 2).(\mathsfit{g}, a)(\mathsfit{h}, b) = \bigl(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{h},\ a + b + c(\mathsfit{g}, \mathsfit{h}) \bmod 2\bigr).

Each element has these fields:

  1. matrix: the transpose of the 𝖲𝖴(𝟤)\mathsf{SU(2)} part, so that the matrices compose like P(𝘨)P(\mathsfit{g}) (sections 4.1 and 4.3).
  2. parity: det⁡𝘨\det \mathsfit{g}.
  3. su2_turn: τ=ω/2π\tau = \omega/2\pi of the 𝖲𝖴(𝟤)\mathsf{SU(2)} part R~𝐯(ω)\tilde{R}_{\mathbf{v}}(\omega).
  4. euler_sign: the ss with (−1)b U(𝘨)=s e−iγσz/2 e−iβσy/2 e−iασz/2(-1)^{b}\,U(\mathsfit{g}) = s\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\gamma\sigma_z/2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta\sigma_y/2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha\sigma_z/2} for the Euler angles of 𝘨\mathsfit{g} (section 4.2).

The geometry of the element is that of the element base of 𝖦\mathsf{G}, and the records hold every element. The documents set the canonical lifts (𝘨,0)(\mathsfit{g}, 0) alone in the tables of the elements and of the spinor matrices. If MM is the matrix of a canonical lift, its barred partner has the matrix −M-M and, in a spinor irrep, −D(𝘨,0)-D(\mathsfit{g}, 0).

21.3 Fundamental domains ¶

A finite record stores no fundamental domain, and a continuous record names that of its image and stores its volumes (section 29.1). The documents set the fundamental domain of the orientations of objects whose symmetry is a closed subgroup of 𝖯𝗂𝗇−(𝟥)\mathsf{Pin^{-}(3)}, its cosets placed by the lifts 𝘘~\tilde{\mathsfit{Q}}, from that of its image 𝖦\mathsf{G} in section 17. Volumes are those of the metric that the covering map carries to that of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}: vol⁡𝖲𝖴(𝟤)=16π2\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{SU(2)} = 16\pi^2 and vol⁡𝖯𝗂𝗇−(𝟥)=32π2\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{Pin^{-}(3)} = 32\pi^2.

The preimage 𝟤𝖦\mathsf{2G} contains 𝘌ˉ\bar{\mathsfit{E}}, so its cosets 𝟤𝖦𝘘~\mathsf{2G}\tilde{\mathsfit{Q}} are the lifts of those of 𝖦\mathsf{G}: the cell of 𝖦\mathsf{G}, each rotation by its canonical lift (section 21.1), its fundamental domain and its space. The preimage has vol⁡𝟤𝖦=2vol⁡𝖦\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{2G} = 2\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{G}. A section over 𝖦\mathsf{G} does not contain 𝘌ˉ\bar{\mathsfit{E}}: its cosets cover those of 𝖦\mathsf{G} twice. The two lifts of the rotations of the cell of 𝖦\mathsf{G} form the section's cell, of twice the volume. Its space is 𝖲𝖴(𝟤)\mathsf{SU(2)} modulo its NN elements in 𝖲𝖴(𝟤)\mathsf{SU(2)}: the lens space 𝖫(N,1)\mathsf{L}(N, 1) in the orientation of section 17.6, or 𝖲𝖴(𝟤)\mathsf{SU(2)} itself for N=1N = 1. Either way, the volume of the group times vol⁡ℱ\operatorname{vol}\mathcal{F} is 32π232\pi^2.

A preimage and a section act on the directions through the covering map, so a finite record names the orbifold of 𝖦\mathsf{G} (orbifold, section 17.6). A preimage also names the harmonics of 𝖦\mathsf{G} (harmonics, section 17.5). As 𝘌ˉ\bar{\mathsfit{E}} acts on D(J,p)D^{(J,p)} by (−1)2J(-1)^{2J}, the harmonics on 𝟤𝖦\𝖯𝗂𝗇−(𝟥)\mathsf{2G}\backslash\mathsf{Pin^{-}(3)} have integer JJ, and they are those on 𝖦\𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G}\backslash\mathsf{O(3)}. A continuous record names its orbit space and its harmonics in the same way (section 29.1). The documents name these values at the end of their section Fundamental domain.
