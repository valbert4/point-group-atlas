23 Spinor irreps ¶

23.1 Characters ¶

An irrep Γ\Gamma of a preimage is a vector irrep, χΓ(𝘌ˉ)=d\chi_{\Gamma}(\bar{\mathsfit{E}}) = d, or a spinor irrep, χΓ(𝘌ˉ)=−d\chi_{\Gamma}(\bar{\mathsfit{E}}) = -d. The vector irreps are those of 𝖦\mathsf{G} with χΓ(𝘨,b)=χΓ(𝘨)\chi_{\Gamma}(\mathsfit{g}, b) = \chi_{\Gamma}(\mathsfit{g}) (vector_irreps), and a vector irrep's image is that of the irrep of 𝖦\mathsf{G}. The field irreps lists the spinor irreps, with the fields of section 6.2 and these values:

  1. spinor: true.
  2. kernel: a node of the record's own lattice (section 28.1).

  3. image: the group formed by its matrices, isomorphic to 𝟤𝖦/ker⁡Γ\mathsf{2G}/\ker\Gamma:

    1. the cyclic group 𝖢n\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{n}}} of its characters, n=∣𝟤𝖦∣/∣ker⁡Γ∣n = |\mathsf{2G}| / |\ker\Gamma|, for dimension 1.
    2. the preimage 𝟤𝖧\mathsf{2H} in 𝖲𝖴(𝟤)\mathsf{SU(2)}, identified by its order and the largest order of its elements, for dimension 2 with every matrix in 𝖲𝖴(𝟤)\mathsf{SU(2)}.
    3. otherwise, the group of this catalogue isomorphic to it: the record's own when Γ\Gamma is faithful, its su2_subgroup when the kernel is a lift of the inversion.

23.2 Labels and ids ¶

A spinor irrep is labeled by its dimension: A (1), E (2), G (4) or I (6). A complex one is labeled by the dimension of its complex-conjugate pair, as in section 6.1 (E for dimension 1, G for dimension 2). When 𝖦\mathsf{G} contains the inversion, the label takes the tag g or u of the character ±d\pm d on the lift (1,−1)(1, -1) of the inversion. Otherwise, when the two lifts of σ𝘩\mathsfit{\sigma_{h}} are not conjugate, it takes the tag ' or '' of the character ±i d\pm\mathrm{i}\,d on the canonical lift of σ𝘩\mathsfit{\sigma_{h}}. The subscript is the least half-integer JJ at which the irrep occurs in D(J,p)D^{(J,p)} (section 25.1). Here pp is the parity of its tag (+1+1 for g, −1-1 for u) when 𝖦\mathsf{G} contains the inversion, and p=+1p = +1 otherwise.

Two complex irreps that would share a label carry the prefixes 1 and 2, 1 with the character of smaller argument in [0,2π)[0, 2\pi) (then of smaller modulus) on the first class with unequal characters. A complex pair is one irrep over ℝ\mathbb{R}, named by both labels (pair_label). The id writes J=n/2J = n/2 as nh (E1h, 1E3h, G3hg). The spinor irreps follow the tags (g or none before u, ' before ''), then the letter, JJ and the prefix.

23.3 Matrices ¶

The matrices of a spinor irrep obey section 9 in the element order of section 21.2, by the first applicable construction:

  1. linear: the character, for dimension 1.
  2. conjugate: the complex conjugates of the matrices of the first irrep from of a complex pair, for its second irrep in dimension above 1. These irreps are 2G3/2{}^2\mathrm{\scriptsize G}_{3/2} of 𝟤𝖳\mathsf{2T} and its two parities in 𝟤𝖳𝗁\mathsf{2T_{h}}.
  3. wigner: the action of section 26.1 at the 2J2J of twoJ and the first parity parity, +1+1 before −1-1, at which the kets of twoM carry the irrep. These kets are ∣ ∓JJ⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,\mp J}\bigr\rangle for dimension 2 of an axial group and all the kets for dimension 2J+12J + 1.
  4. twist: the matrices of the spinor irrep from times the one-dimensional irrep by.
  5. galois: the matrices of from with the automorphism ζℳ↦ζℳsigma\zeta_{\mathcal{M}} \mapsto \zeta_{\mathcal{M}}^{\texttt{sigma}} applied entrywise, ℳ=\mathcal{M} = conductor. Here sigma is the least exponent whose matrices carry the irrep, first among those that fix i\mathrm{i} and ζ3\zeta_3 (when the field of the entries contains them), then among all.

The second irrep of every complex pair thus has the conjugate matrices of its first irrep, in dimension 1 its character. Time reversal then takes a block of the first irrep to a block of the second (section 26.1).

A document writes the matrices of an irrep, or those of section 21.2, over a common rational factor when doing so makes the widest of them at least a fifth narrower. Each entry is then an integer times a square root times a sum of integer multiples of 1, φ\varphi, i\mathrm{i} and iφ\mathrm{i}\varphi (5\sqrt{5} for 2φ−12\varphi - 1).

23.4 bethe and bilbao ¶

The spinor irreps of the 36 preimages of the groups of section 6.5 also carry bethe and bilbao, and their vector irreps carry those of 𝖦\mathsf{G} (vector_irreps). Every other spinor irrep has null not-in-source. The spinor irreps come after the vector irreps in both numberings, bethe writes the sign of the inversion as in section 6.5, and bilbao writes a bar and no sign, "bar Gamma_5" for Γˉ5\bar{\Gamma}_{5}.

The tables of bethe [KDWS63] give a spinor character on the canonical lift (𝘨,0)(\mathsfit{g}, 0) of section 21.1. Their unbarred operation is the rotation through an angle in (−π,π](-\pi, \pi] about the axis that they name, or 𝘪\mathsfit{i} times one, the inversion leaving spinors unchanged.

The double point groups of bilbao [EBW+17] are these preimages, with the same elements. The 2×22 \times 2 matrix of an operation is a lift of its rotation part, the identity for the inversion (the lift (1,−1)(1, -1) of section 20.1), and the operation marked d is the other lift. On its hexagonal tables the matrices are those of [BC72]. Their rows and columns run over m=−12,12m = -\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2}: in the usual order the matrices are lifts in the frame with xx along [120], yy along [100] and zz against the hexagonal axis c. Thus 2_001 and m_001 are barred partners, and 2_1-10 and m_1-10 are the lifts about their named axes.

In 𝖣𝟥𝖽\mathsf{D_{3d}} the binary rotations fix the frame of both works, [KDWS63] and [EBW+17]. Thus their mirrors σ𝘥=𝘪 𝘊𝟤′\mathsfit{\sigma_{d}} = \mathsfit{i}\,\mathsfit{C_{2}'} are the record's (σ𝘥,1)(\mathsfit{\sigma_{d}}, 1): there the canonical lifts of 𝘪\mathsfit{i} and of a binary rotation multiply to a barred mirror. Read with the opposite orientation of a work's frame, the complex pairs of spinor irreps of 2C2, 2C1h, 2C2h, 2C1v, 2D1, 2D1d, 2C4, 2S4, 2C4h, 2C3, 2S6, 2C6, 2C3h, 2C6h, 2D3, 2C3v, 2D3d, 2T and 2Th would exchange their symbols. The rule of section 6.5 fixes them.
