\section{Class space}\label{s:class_space}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.16}{16}, \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.21}{21} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.29.1}{29.1}.
\leavevmode\label{n:head.component}A row is a component of the class space of $\grp{SU(2)}$, an arc of classes. \label{n:head.coset}\label{n:head.element}A row gives whether its elements cover proper or improper elements of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$} (coset) and the element of $\grp{Pin^{-}(3)}$ at its angle. \label{n:angle.omega}The element at each angle is the lift $\lift{R}_z(\omega) = \cos\tfrac{\omega}{2} - \iunit\sin\tfrac{\omega}{2}\,\pauli{z}$, $0 \le \omega < 4\pi$ (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.29.1}{29.1}). \label{n:matrix.element.pin}The matrix $M(g)$ of the element is the transpose of its $\grp{SU(2)}$ part, so that the matrices compose as the matrices $P(g)$ of the elements of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$} (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.21.2}{21.2}).
The constant $\iunit$ is the imaginary unit, and $\expe$ is the base of the natural logarithm.
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}\toprule
component & coset & element & $M(g)$\\\midrule
$\comp{\lift{C}(\omega)}$ & proper & $\elt{\lift{C}}(\omega)$ & $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\expe^{-\iunit\omega/2} & 0\\ 0 & \expe^{\iunit\omega/2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)$\\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
On an arc, $\elt{\lift{R}_z}(\omega)$ is conjugate to $\elt{\lift{R}_z}(4\pi - \omega)$, so the classes run over $0 \le \omega \le 2\pi$, from $\cls{E}$ through the half turns to $\cls{\barred{E}}$.
The distinguished points are $\cls{E}$ at $\omega = 0$, $\cls{C_2}$ at $\omega = \pi$ and $\cls{\barred{E}}$ at $\omega = 2\pi$.
\leavevmode\label{n:local.haar.h}The Haar measure, of total mass 1, is $\diff h = (1 - \cos\omega)\,\diff\omega/(2\pi)$ on $\comp{\lift{C}(\omega)}$, $\omega\in\bigl[0, 2\pi\bigr]$, of mass $1$.

\section{Fundamental domain}\label{s:fundamental_domain}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.17}{17} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.21.3}{21.3}.
An object with symmetry $\grp{SU(2)}$ placed by $\lift{Q} \in \grp{Pin^{-}(3)}$ is the same when placed by $g\lift{Q}$ with $g \in \grp{SU(2)}$. \label{n:operator.cosets}Thus its orientations are the cosets $\grp{SU(2)}\lift{Q} = \{g\lift{Q} : g \in \grp{SU(2)}\}$, the points of the space $\grp{SU(2)}\backslash\grp{Pin^{-}(3)}$. \label{n:operator.volume}Volumes are those of the metric that the covering map carries to that of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/O3.pdf}{$\grp{O(3)}$} (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.17}{17}), so that $\vol\grp{SU(2)} = 16\pi^2$ and $\vol\grp{Pin^{-}(3)} = 32\pi^2$, twice those of $\grp{SO(3)}$ and $\grp{O(3)}$.
As $\grp{SU(2)}$ contains $\elt{\barred{E}}$, a coset $\grp{SU(2)}\lift{Q}$ contains both lifts of every element of a coset of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$} in $\grp{O(3)}$, and $\grp{SU(2)}\backslash\grp{Pin^{-}(3)}$ is $\grp{SO(3)}\backslash\grp{O(3)}$, two points. A cell's rotations stand here for their canonical lifts (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.21.1}{21.1}). \label{n:set.cell}\label{n:set.fundamental}Thus the cell $\fcell$ and the fundamental domain $\fdomain$ are those of the document of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$}, in its section Fundamental domain. The group $\grp{SU(2)}$ covers \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$} twice. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{e:volume}\vol\fdomain = 2,\qquad \vol\grp{SU(2)}\cdot\vol\fdomain = 16\pi^2\cdot 2 = 32\pi^2 = \vol\grp{Pin^{-}(3)}.\end{equation}

\section{Families of irreps}\label{s:families}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.7}{7}, \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.16}{16}, \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.23}{23} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.29.2}{29.2}.
\leavevmode\label{n:head.family.irreps}\label{n:index.dimension}\label{n:index.frobenius}\label{n:head.index.family}A row is a family of irreps, by its label, with its dimension $d$, its Frobenius--Schur indicator $\fsind$ (real for $+1$ and pseudoreal for $-1$) and the range of the index of its irreps. \label{n:head.kernel}\label{n:function.character}The kernel $\ker\Gamma$ is the subgroup of the elements that an irrep $\Gamma$ of the family maps to the identity, and the character of $\Gamma$ at $g$ is $\irchar{\Gamma}(g)$. \label{n:head.image}The image is the group formed by the irrep's matrices, isomorphic to $\grp{SU(2)}/\ker\Gamma$. A row also gives the family's character on each component, in the angle $\omega$ of the lift. The vector families, on which $\elt{\barred{E}}$ is the identity, are those of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$} (\texttt{vector\symbol{95}families}), read on these classes through the covering map. A point $\omega$ of an arc covers the rotation angle $\phi = \omega$ modulo $2\pi$. The image and matrices of a vector family are those of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$}, and its kernel is the preimage of its kernel in that group. The spinor families, on which $\elt{\barred{E}}$ is $-1$, follow below the rule, with an instance for every half-integer $J = \tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{3}{2}, \dots$. The image of a spinor family is $\grp{SU(2)}$. The irreps are irreducible over $\mathbb{C}$. Over $\mathbb{R}$ (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.7}{7}), a real irrep is also an irrep in a basis with real matrices. Read as a real space of dimension $2d$, a pseudoreal irrep is one irrep over $\mathbb{R}$ of character $2\irchar{\Gamma}$ and keeps its own label. The determinant character of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$}, of value $\det g$ on the elements that cover $g$, is $\irrJ{0}$, the instance $J = 0$ of $\irrJ{J}$, and is trivial in a group of type I.

\subsection{Matrices of a general element}\label{s:general}
\leavevmode\label{n:angle.euler.alpha}\label{n:angle.euler.beta}\label{n:angle.euler.gamma}The Euler angles $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ are those of $U = \lift{R}_z(\alpha)\,\lift{R}_y(\beta)\,\lift{R}_z(\gamma)$, the lifts (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.21.1}{21.1}) multiplied as matrices of $\grp{SU(2)}$. \label{n:matrix.irrep.entry}In the basis $\jm{J}{m}$, rows $m'$ and columns $m = J, J-1, \dots, -J$, the matrix of $g$ is
\begin{equation}\label{e:euler}D_{m',m}(g) = \expe^{\iunit m\alpha}\,\wignerd{J}_{m,m'}(\beta)\,\expe^{\iunit m'\gamma} = (-1)^{m-m'}\,\expe^{\iunit m\alpha}\,\wignerd{J}_{m',m}(\beta)\,\expe^{\iunit m'\gamma},\end{equation}
\leavevmode\label{n:matrix.wigner}that is, $D(g) = \wignerD{J}(U)^\dagger$ with $\wignerD{J}_{m',m}(U) = \expe^{-\iunit m'\alpha}\,\wignerd{J}_{m',m}(\beta)\,\expe^{-\iunit m\gamma}$ (\texttt{general\symbol{95}element}). \label{n:matrix.wigner.small}\label{n:index.halfangle}\label{n:local.sum.wigner}The Wigner matrix [\href{../../../REFERENCES.pdf\#ref.VMK88}{VMK88}, section 4.3] is
\begin{equation}\label{e:wigner}\begin{aligned}\wignerd{J}_{m',m}(\beta) &= \sum_\sigma (-1)^{m'-m+\sigma}\,\frac{\sqrt{(J+m')!\,(J-m')!\,(J+m)!\,(J-m)!}}{(J+m-\sigma)!\,\sigma!\,(m'-m+\sigma)!\,(J-m'-\sigma)!}\,c_\beta^{2J+m-m'-2\sigma}s_\beta^{m'-m+2\sigma},\end{aligned}\end{equation}
where $c_\beta = \cos\tfrac{\beta}{2}$ and $s_\beta = \sin\tfrac{\beta}{2}$, and the sum runs over the $\sigma$ for which every factorial argument is $\ge 0$. \label{n:index.cayleyklein}\label{n:local.sum.cayleyklein}In the entries of $U = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} a & b\\ -\conj{b} & \conj{a}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, the matrix is
\begin{equation}\label{e:entries}\begin{aligned}\wignerD{J}_{m',m}(U) &= \sqrt{(J+m')!\,(J-m')!\,(J+m)!\,(J-m)!}\\ &\quad\times\sum_k\frac{a^{k}\,b^{J+m'-k}\,(-\conj{b})^{J+m-k}\,(\conj{a})^{k-m-m'}}{k!\,(J+m'-k)!\,(J+m-k)!\,(k-m-m')!},\end{aligned}\end{equation}
where the sum runs over the $k$ for which every factorial argument is $\ge 0$. This matrix is that of $U$ on the polynomials of the kets $\jm{J}{m}$ when $U$ sends $u$ to $au - \conj{b}v$ and $v$ to $bu + \conj{a}v$, so that $\wignerD{\nicefrac{1}{2}}(U) = U$. \label{n:vector.unitaxis}\label{n:vector.axis.components}For the lift $U = \lift{R}_{\vv}(\phi)$ by $\phi$ about the unit axis $\unitaxis = (\hat{v}_x, \hat{v}_y, \hat{v}_z)$, $a = \cos\tfrac{\phi}{2} - \iunit\hat{v}_z\sin\tfrac{\phi}{2}$ and $b = -(\hat{v}_y + \iunit\hat{v}_x)\sin\tfrac{\phi}{2}$.

Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.10.2}{10.2} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.29.3}{29.3}.
The record holds a rule for every pair of families with a spinor factor (\texttt{rules}), and these rules take one form. \label{n:index.J12}In this form $J_1$ and $J_2$ are the indices of the factors, an integer for a vector family and a half-integer for a spinor family. The square of a spinor irrep (\texttt{squares}) follows, with the antisymmetric part in braces. The products of two vector families are those of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$} (\texttt{vector\symbol{95}rules}).

\paragraph{Products.}
\begin{align}\irrJ{J_1}\irrprod \irrJ{J_2} &= \bigoplus_{J=\lvert J_1-J_2\rvert}^{J_1+J_2} \irrJ{J}.\end{align}

\paragraph{Squares.}
\begin{align}\irrJ{J}\irrprod \irrJ{J} &= \bigoplus_{K=1,3,\dots,2J} \irrJ{K}\oplus\Bigl\{\bigoplus_{K=0,2,\dots,2J-1} \irrJ{K}\Bigr\}.\end{align}

\section{Clebsch--Gordan coefficients}\label{s:clebsch_gordan}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.10.3}{10.3} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.29.3}{29.3}.
\leavevmode\label{n:function.cg}\label{n:index.m12}\label{n:index.projection.M}\label{n:matrix.cg.stored}\label{n:index.cg}\label{n:local.sum.racah}These coupling rules cover every pair of irreps:
\begin{romanlist}\raggedright
\item Every irrep in the product of two irreps (section~\ref{s:fusion}) occurs once: $\grp{SU(2)}$ and \href{Pinminus3.pdf}{$\grp{Pin^{-}(3)}$} are simply reducible.
\item $\cgc{J_1}{m_1}{J_2}{m_2}{J}{M}$ in the Condon--Shortley convention [\href{../../../REFERENCES.pdf\#ref.VMK88}{VMK88}, section 8.1.1]: real, zero unless $M = m_1 + m_2$, and $\cgc{J_1}{J_1}{J_2}{J-J_1}{J}{J} > 0$.
\item $C[(\intx_1,\intx_2),\intx] = \cgc{J_1}{m_1}{J_2}{m_2}{J}{M}$ with $\intx_1 = J_1 - m_1 + 1$, $\intx_2 = J_2 - m_2 + 1$, $\intx = J - M + 1$ ($m$ descending), on the kets $\jm{J}{m}$ of $\irrJp{J}{+1}$ (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.26.1}{26.1}), so the phase rule (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.10.3}{10.3}) is the Condon--Shortley condition.
\item The closed form of van der Waerden and Racah [\href{../../../REFERENCES.pdf\#ref.VMK88}{VMK88}, section 8.2.1, eq.~3] applies at integer and half-integer $J$ and sums over the $z$ for which every factorial argument is $\ge 0$: \begin{equation}\begin{aligned}&\cgc{J_1}{m_1}{J_2}{m_2}{J}{M} = \delta_{M,m_1+m_2}\sqrt{\frac{(2J+1)\,(J_1+J_2-J)!\,(J_1-J_2+J)!\,(-J_1+J_2+J)!}{(J_1+J_2+J+1)!}}\\ &\qquad\times\sqrt{(J_1+m_1)!\,(J_1-m_1)!\,(J_2+m_2)!\,(J_2-m_2)!\,(J+M)!\,(J-M)!}\\ &\qquad\times\sum_z\frac{(-1)^z}{z!\,(J_1+J_2-J-z)!\,(J_1-m_1-z)!\,(J_2+m_2-z)!\,(J-J_2+m_1+z)!\,(J-J_1-m_2+z)!}.\end{aligned}\end{equation}
\item Symmetries [\href{../../../REFERENCES.pdf\#ref.VMK88}{VMK88}, section 8.4]: $\cgc{J_1}{m_1}{J_2}{m_2}{J}{M} = (-1)^{J_1+J_2-J}\cgc{J_2}{m_2}{J_1}{m_1}{J}{M} = (-1)^{J_1+J_2-J}\cgc{J_1}{{-m_1}}{J_2}{{-m_2}}{J}{{-M}}$.
\item The products with $J_1 \le J_2 \le 10$ and $J_1$ or $J_2$ half-integer are in one file for each $2J_1$, \texttt{data/clebsch\symbol{95}gordan/SU2/<NN>.json} with \texttt{NN} $= 2J_1$ in two digits, 00 to 19 (\texttt{files}). The products of two integer $J$ have the coefficients of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$} (\texttt{vector\symbol{95}products}).
\end{romanlist}

\section{Spin--orbit levels}\label{s:spin_orbit}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.19}{19}, \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.25.2}{25.2} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.29.4}{29.4}.
\leavevmode\label{n:local.spin}\label{n:symbol.term}A term $\term{2S+1}{\Gamma}$ is the vector irrep $\Gamma$ with the spin $S$, a spin even under the inversion. Under spin--orbit coupling it splits into the irreps of $\grp{SU(2)}$ in
\begin{equation}\label{e:spinorbit}\restr{(\Gamma \irrprod \irrJp{S}{+1})}{\grp{SU(2)}},\end{equation}
and they are spinor irreps at half-integer $S$ and vector irreps at integer $S$. In an atom they are the levels $J = \lvert J_1 - S\rvert, \dots, J_1 + S$ of the term $\term{2S+1}{\Gamma}$ of an orbital level $\Gamma$ of angular momentum $J_1$, the products of section~\ref{s:fusion}. Row $\Gamma$, column $2S + 1$ of a table gives the levels of $\term{2S+1}{\Gamma}$ for the vector irreps of index at most 3 and $S \le \nicefrac{7}{2}$, and the record holds them (\texttt{spin\symbol{95}orbit\symbol{95}levels}). The section Electronic terms of the document of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$} gives the terms in the same way. Column $2S + 1 = 2$ gives the levels of one electron in a shell of section~\ref{s:spin_orbitals}, and that section gives their states.

\section{\texorpdfstring{$jj$}{jj} configurations}\label{s:jj}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.19}{19} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.25.3}{25.3}.
In $jj$ coupling, an electron of an atomic shell $\orbital{p}$, $\orbital{d}$ or $\orbital{f}$ has the angular momentum $J$ of one of its two subshells $\orbital{p}_{1/2}$, $\orbital{p}_{3/2}$, $\orbital{d}_{3/2}$, $\orbital{d}_{5/2}$, $\orbital{f}_{5/2}$ and $\orbital{f}_{7/2}$. In the subshell $J$, $n$ electrons fill the level $\irrJ{J}$, a Kramers level, and their levels are $\Lambda^{n}\irrJ{J}$, $n = 0, \dots, 2J + 1$. These levels are vector irreps at even $n$ and spinor irreps at odd $n$, and the configurations of $n$ and of $2J + 1 - n$ electrons have the same levels. A configuration in both subshells of $\orbital{p}$, $\orbital{d}$ or $\orbital{f}$ has the products of their powers (section~\ref{s:fusion}), and the electronic terms of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$} split into these products. The record holds every $n$ (\texttt{jj\symbol{95}configurations}).
