\leavevmode\label{n:element.inversion}\label{n:group.preimage}The group $\grp{2C_{4v}}$ is the preimage in $\grp{Pin^{-}(3)}$ of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/C4v.pdf}{$\grp{C_{4v}}$}, whose document sets the fields that this table does not repeat (\texttt{cover\symbol{95}of}): its type, family, geometry, crystal classes and the names of its \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/O3.pdf}{$\grp{O(3)}$}-conjugacy class. \section{The cover}\label{s:cover}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.20.1}{20.1}, \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.21.1}{21.1} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.21.2}{21.2}.
\leavevmode\label{n:group.pin.minus}\label{n:group.su2}\label{n:local.pin.sign}\label{n:matrix.covered}\label{n:matrix.lift.su2}\label{n:index.bar}The point group $\grp{C_{4v}}$ has the preimage $\grp{2C_{4v}}$ in $\grp{Pin^{-}(3)} = \grp{SU(2)} \dirprod \{+1, -1\}$, whose element $(U, \varepsilon)$ covers $\varepsilon R(U) \in \grp{O(3)}$, where $U$ covers the rotation $R(U)$. The preimage is the double group of $\grp{C_{4v}}$, with the $2 \cdot 8 = 16$ elements $(g, 0) = (U(g), \det g)$ and $(g, 1) = (-U(g), \det g)$, $g \in \grp{C_{4v}}$, where $U(g)$ is the canonical lift of $g$ to $\grp{SU(2)}$ (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.21.1}{21.1}). \label{n:function.cocycle}With $U(h)\,U(g) = (-1)^{c(g,h)}\,U(gh)$, $gh$ = first $g$, then $h$, the product is
\begin{equation}\label{e:product}(g, b)(h, b') = \bigl(gh,\ b + b' + c(g, h) \bmod 2\bigr).\end{equation}
\leavevmode\label{n:mark.bar}The covering map $(g, b) \mapsto g$ has kernel $\{\elt{E}, \elt{\barred{E}}\}$, where $\elt{\barred{E}} = (\elt{E}, 1)$ is central of order 2. \label{n:element.named}\label{n:mark.lift}The barred partner $\elt{\barred{X}}$ of an element $\elt{X}$ of $\grp{C_{4v}}$ is $(\elt{X}, 1)$, and the lift of the inversion, $\elt{\lift{i}} = (1, -1)$, is central of order 2.

\section{Symmetry operations}\label{s:elements}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.4}{4}, \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.5.1}{5.1} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.21}{21}.
\leavevmode\label{n:index.element}\label{n:element.g}Element $k$ of $\grp{2C_{4v}}$ is the pair $(g, b)$ of the element $g$ of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/C4v.pdf}{$\grp{C_{4v}}$} of index $\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ in its document and $b = k - 2\lfloor k/2\rfloor$. The pair $(g, 0)$ is the canonical lift of $g$, and $(g, 1)$ is its barred partner (section~\ref{s:cover}). \label{n:matrix.element.pin}\label{n:matrix.element}The matrix $M(k)$ of the element is the transpose of its $\grp{SU(2)}$ part $(-1)^{b}\,U(g)$, and the matrices compose as the matrices $P(g)$ of the elements of $\grp{C_{4v}}$:
\begin{equation}\label{e:mprod}M(k)\,M(k') = M(kk'),\qquad kk' = \text{first } k\text{, then } k'.\end{equation}

\section{Invariant polynomials}\label{s:invariants}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.26.4}{26.4}.
\leavevmode\label{n:local.binary.form}A binary form $f = \sum_i c_i\, u^i v^{n-i}$ of degree $n$ in the spin kets $u = \jm{1/2}{1/2}$ and $v = \jm{1/2}{-1/2}$ is invariant when $k f = f$ for every element $k$, the elements acting at the parity $+1$ (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.26.4}{26.4}). At the parity $-1$ the invariants of even degree are the same, and those of odd degree are the semi-invariants of $\det_{\grp{2C_{4v}}}$.
The spinor irreps of $\grp{2C_{4v}}$ have dimension 2, as those of the binary dihedral group of order 16, the group of its matrices on $u$, $v$. \label{n:function.hilbert}The invariant polynomials in the partners of one are those of the group of its matrices, counted in each degree by its Molien series $H_V(t)$ below. In dimension 2 the matrices form a binary dihedral group, whose invariants are the forms of [\href{../../../REFERENCES.pdf\#ref.Kle84}{Kle84}] of the degrees of its series. Among the finite groups of this catalogue, only $\grp{2O}$, $\grp{2T_{d}}$, $\grp{2O_{h}}$, $\grp{2I}$ and $\grp{2I_{h}}$ have spinor irreps of dimension above 2 [\href{../../../REFERENCES.pdf\#ref.McK80}{McK80}].

\section{\texorpdfstring{$jj$}{jj} configurations}\label{s:jj}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.19}{19} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.25.3}{25.3}.
A spinor level is a Kramers level: a pseudoreal spinor irrep alone, a real one twice or a complex-conjugate pair together. In a level of dimension $d$, $n$ electrons fill its exterior power $\Lambda^{n}$, $n = 0, \dots, d$: vector irreps at even $n$ and spinor irreps at odd $n$. Every level of $\grp{2C_{4v}}$ has dimension 2: $\Lambda^{1}$ is the level, and $\Lambda^{0}$ and $\Lambda^{2}$ are the trivial irrep (\texttt{jj\symbol{95}configurations}).
