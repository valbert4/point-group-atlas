\section{Fundamental domain}\label{s:fundamental_domain}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.17}{17} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.21.3}{21.3}.
\leavevmode\label{n:operator.cosets}An object with symmetry $\grp{2C_{\infty h}}$ placed by $\lift{Q} \in \grp{Pin^{-}(3)}$ is the same when placed by $g\lift{Q}$ with $g \in \grp{2C_{\infty h}}$: its orientations are the cosets $\grp{2C_{\infty h}}\lift{Q} = \{g\lift{Q} : g \in \grp{2C_{\infty h}}\}$, the points of the space $\grp{2C_{\infty h}}\backslash\grp{Pin^{-}(3)}$. \label{n:operator.volume}Volumes are those of the metric that the covering map carries to that of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/O3.pdf}{$\grp{O(3)}$} (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.17}{17}), so that $\vol\grp{SU(2)} = 16\pi^2$ and $\vol\grp{Pin^{-}(3)} = 32\pi^2$, twice those of $\grp{SO(3)}$ and $\grp{O(3)}$.
\leavevmode\label{n:set.cell}\label{n:set.fundamental}\label{n:set.sphere}As $\grp{2C_{\infty h}}$ holds $\elt{\barred{E}}$, a coset $\grp{2C_{\infty h}}\lift{Q}$ holds both lifts of every element of a coset of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/Cinfh.pdf}{$\grp{C_{\infty h}}$} in $\grp{O(3)}$, and $\grp{2C_{\infty h}}\backslash\grp{Pin^{-}(3)}$ is $\grp{C_{\infty h}}\backslash\grp{O(3)}$, $\grp{C_{\infty}}\backslash\grp{SO(3)}$, the sphere $\twosphere$: the cell $\fcell$, each of its rotations by its canonical lift (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.21.1}{21.1}), and the fundamental domain $\fdomain$ are those of the document of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/Cinfh.pdf}{$\grp{C_{\infty h}}$}, in its section Fundamental domain; $\grp{2C_{\infty h}}$ covers \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/Cinfh.pdf}{$\grp{C_{\infty h}}$} twice, the circles of its components being of length $4\pi$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{e:volume}\vol\fdomain = 4\pi,\qquad \vol\grp{2C_{\infty h}}\cdot\vol\fdomain = 8\pi\cdot 4\pi = 32\pi^2 = \vol\grp{Pin^{-}(3)}.\end{equation}

\partbox{Class space and representations}
\section{Class space}\label{s:class_space}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.16}{16}, \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.21}{21} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.29.1}{29.1}.
\leavevmode\label{n:angle.omega}\label{n:head.component}\label{n:head.coset}\label{n:head.element}A row is a component of the class space of $\grp{2C_{\infty h}}$, an arc of classes; coset tells whether its elements cover proper or improper elements of \href{../../../rotation/data/pdf/Cinfh.pdf}{$\grp{C_{\infty h}}$}, element gives the element of $\grp{Pin^{-}(3)}$ at its angle: the lift $\lift{R}_z(\omega) = \cos\tfrac{\omega}{2} - \iunit\sin\tfrac{\omega}{2}\,\pauli{z}$, $0 \le \omega < 4\pi$ and the lift of the inversion $\elt{\lift{i}}$ (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.29.1}{29.1}).
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}\toprule
component & coset & element\\\midrule
$\comp{\lift{C}(\omega)}$ & proper & $\elt{\lift{C}}(\omega)$\\
$\comp{\lift{i}\,\lift{C}(\omega)}$ & improper & $\elt{\lift{i}\,\lift{C}}(\omega)$\\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
$\grp{2C_{\infty h}}$ is abelian: every element is a class, and the arcs run over $0 \le \omega < 4\pi$, the two lifts $\omega$ and $\omega + 2\pi$ of a rotation apart.
The distinguished points are $\cls{E}$ at $\omega = 0$, $\cls{C_2}$ at $\omega = \pi$, $\cls{\barred{E}}$ at $\omega = 2\pi$, $\cls{\barred{C}_2}$ at $\omega = 3\pi$, $\cls{\lift{i}}$ at $\omega = 0$, $\cls{\sigma_h}$ at $\omega = \pi$, $\cls{\barred{\lift{i}}}$ at $\omega = 2\pi$, $\cls{\barred{\sigma}_h}$ at $\omega = 3\pi$.

\leavevmode\label{n:local.binary.form}A binary form $f = \sum_i c_i\, u^i v^{n-i}$ of degree $n$ in the spin kets $u = \jm{1/2}{1/2}$ and $v = \jm{1/2}{-1/2}$ is invariant when every element leaves it unchanged, the elements acting at the parity $+1$ (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.26.4}{26.4}); at the parity $-1$ the invariant forms are the same, all of even degree. \leavevmode\label{n:function.hilbert}\label{n:local.partner.x}Every spinor irrep $V$ of $\grp{2C_{\infty h}}$ has dimension 1, and its values on the lifts of the rotations about $z$ are all the complex numbers of modulus 1: the number of independent invariant polynomials of degree $n$ in its partners, the multiplicity of the trivial irrep in the $n$-th symmetric power of $V$, is the coefficient of $t^n$ in its Molien series $H_V(t)$ [\href{../../../REFERENCES.pdf\#ref.Mol97}{Mol97}], and no polynomial of positive degree in its partner $x$ is invariant: $H_V(t) = 1$. A level of section~\ref{s:jj}, a complex-conjugate pair of partners $x$ and $y$, has the polynomials in $xy$: $H_V(t) = 1/(1 - t^{2})$. The record stores none of these series.

\section{\texorpdfstring{$jj$}{jj} configurations}\label{s:jj}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../../rotation/CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.19}{19} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.25.3}{25.3}.
A spinor level is a Kramers level: a pseudoreal spinor irrep alone, a real one twice, a complex-conjugate pair together. $n$ electrons in a level of dimension $d$ fill its exterior power $\Lambda^{n}$, $n = 0, \dots, d$: vector irreps at even $n$, spinor irreps at odd $n$. Every level of $\grp{2C_{\infty h}}$ has dimension 2: $\Lambda^{1}$ is the level, and $\Lambda^{0}$ and $\Lambda^{2}$ are the trivial irrep (\texttt{jj\symbol{95}configurations}).
