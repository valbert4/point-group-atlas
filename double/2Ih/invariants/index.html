The group has no one-dimensional irrep but the trivial one that a form carries: it has no semi-invariants; the forms that carry an irrep of dimension 2 or more are its copies in the bases of section 4.3.

Molien series. The number of independent invariant polynomials of degree nn in the partners of a spinor irrep VV, or of a spinor level of section 6.2 that is not one irrep, the multiplicity of the trivial irrep in the nn-th symmetric power of VV as that section gives the exterior powers, is the coefficient of tnt^n in Molien’s series HV(t)=∣𝟤𝖨𝗁∣−1∑k1/det⁡(1−t DV(k))H_V(t) = \lvert\mathsf{2I_{h}}\rvert^{-1}\sum_k 1/\det\bigl(1 - t\,D^V(k)\bigr) [Mol97], written over the denominator of the rule of the levels (Conventions, sections 19 and 26.4); the table gives them.

Spinor irreps of dimension 4 and 6. The invariant polynomials in the partners of a spinor irrep VV of dimension 4 or 6, V=D(J,±1)V = D^{(J,\pm 1)} restricted, written in the kets Xi=uiv2J−iX_i = u^i v^{2J-i} (Conventions, section 26.4): a row gives the degrees of a minimal set of generators of degree at most 12, with their number in parentheses when it exceeds one, the degrees of the primary invariants, a homogeneous system of parameters over whose polynomials the ring is a free module (they need not be the factors of the denominator of HV(t)H_V(t) above), and whether those generators generate the ring. The matrices have determinant 1, so the ring is Gorenstein [Ben93, section 4.6] and the numerator of its HV(t)H_V(t) a palindrome. The forms are in the record (invariants.higher); an irrep that has the matrices of an irrep of 𝟤𝖨\mathsf{2I} has its invariants.
