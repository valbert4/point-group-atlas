\leavevmode\label{n:element.inversion}The field \texttt{type} is I when $\grp{D_{\infty}}$ contains only rotations, II when $\grp{D_{\infty}}$ contains the inversion $\elt{i}$ and III otherwise. The field \texttt{polar} is yes when $\grp{D_{\infty}}$ fixes a nonzero vector. Crystallographers call a group chiral, or enantiomorphic, when its type is I, and centrosymmetric when it contains $\elt{i}$. The field \texttt{laue\symbol{95}class} is the group generated by $\grp{D_{\infty}}$ and $\elt{i}$.
In \texttt{product\symbol{95}form}, after a semidirect product, ${\times}\infty$ marks infinitely many subgroups conjugate to its second factor. The braces contain the classes of $\grp{D_{\infty}}$ that the finite second factor meets: atoms of the class space (section~\ref{s:class_space}) and distinguished points of its arcs (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.16}{16}).

\section{Class space}\label{s:class_space}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.16}{16}.
\leavevmode\label{n:angle.phi}\label{n:mark.atom}\label{n:angle.psi}\label{n:head.component}A row is a component of the class space of $\grp{D_{\infty}}$, an arc of classes or an atom (a single class). \label{n:head.coset}\label{n:head.element}A row gives whether its elements are proper or improper (coset) and the element at its angle. The matrix $P$ of the element is in the row-vector convention (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.4}{4}). On column vectors its matrix is the transpose $P^{\top}$, and products compose in the reverse order.

component & coset & element & $P$\\\midrule
$\comp{C_{\infty}(\phi)}$ & proper & $\elt{C_{\infty}}(\phi)$ & $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\phi & \sin\phi & 0\\-\sin\phi & \cos\phi & 0\\0 & 0 & 1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$\\\addlinespace[6pt]

The atom $\comp{\infty C_2'}$ is the class of the binary rotations $\elt{C_2'}(\psi)$ about the horizontal axes at every azimuth $\psi$.
\leavevmode\label{n:local.haar.h}The Haar measure, of total mass 1, is $\diff h = \diff\phi/(2\pi)$ on $\comp{C_{\infty}(\phi)}$, $\phi\in\bigl[0, \pi\bigr]$, of mass $\tfrac{1}{2}$, and the atom $\comp{\infty C_2'}$ has mass $\tfrac{1}{2}$.

\section{Fundamental domain}\label{s:fundamental_domain}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.4.2}{4.2} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.17}{17}.
\leavevmode\label{n:element.placement}A molecule with symmetry $\grp{D_{\infty}}$ placed by $Q \in \grp{O(3)}$ is the same set of atoms when placed by $gQ$ with $g \in \grp{D_{\infty}}$, since $P(gQ) = P(g)\,P(Q)$. \label{n:operator.cosets}Thus its orientations are the cosets $\grp{D_{\infty}}Q = \{gQ : g \in \grp{D_{\infty}}\}$, the points of the space $\grp{D_{\infty}}\backslash\grp{O(3)}$. On column vectors a coset is the left coset of the transposes $P(Q)^{\top}P(g)^{\top}$, $g \in \grp{D_{\infty}}$, a point of $\grp{O(3)}/\grp{D_{\infty}}$.
\leavevmode\label{n:operator.volume}Volumes are those of the invariant metric of \href{O3.pdf}{$\grp{O(3)}$} in which the distance from $\elt{E}$ to a rotation by $\omega \le \pi$ is $\omega$, so that $\vol\grp{SO(3)} = 8\pi^2$ and $\vol\grp{O(3)} = 16\pi^2$. The group $\grp{D_{\infty}}$ is the union of the circles $\elt{C_{\infty}}(\phi)$ and $\elt{C_2'}(\psi)$, $\phi \in [0, 2\pi)$ and $\psi \in [0, \pi)$, each of length $2\pi$, so that $\vol\grp{D_{\infty}} = 4\pi$.

\leavevmode\label{n:angle.euler.alpha}\label{n:angle.euler.beta}\label{n:angle.euler.gamma}The Euler angles of the rotation part $(\det Q)\,Q$ are $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$: by $\alpha$ about $z$, then by $\beta$ about $y$, then by $\gamma$ about $z$ (fixed axes). In these angles the volume element of \href{SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$} is $\sin\beta\,\diff\alpha\,\diff\beta\,\diff\gamma$. As $\elt{C_{\infty}}(\phi)\,Q$ has the angles $(\alpha + \phi, \beta, \gamma)$, a coset of \href{Cinf.pdf}{$\grp{C_{\infty}}$} is a circle of $\alpha$, and $(\beta, \gamma)$ are the polar and azimuthal angles of the axis $\dirvec{0}{0}{1}P((\det Q)\,Q)$ of the molecule. Thus the cosets have the metric of the unit sphere of the axes, of area element $\sin\beta\,\diff\beta\,\diff\gamma$. The element $\elt{C_2'}(\psi)$ carries the rotation of the angles $(0, \beta, \gamma)$ to that of $(-2\psi, \pi - \beta, \gamma + \pi)$, of the opposite axis.
\leavevmode\label{n:set.cell}The cell $\fcell$ of the rotations with $\alpha = 0$, $\beta \in [0, \tfrac{\pi}{2}]$ and $\gamma \in (-\pi, \pi]$ meets every coset of $\grp{D_{\infty}} \cap \grp{SO(3)} = \grp{D_{\infty}}$ once, except on its boundary ($\beta = 0$ and $\beta = \tfrac{\pi}{2}$), and, in the metric of the cosets, $\vol\fcell = 2\pi$.

\leavevmode\label{n:set.fundamental}Every coset $\grp{D_{\infty}}Q$ lies in \href{SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$} or in $\elt{i}\,\grp{SO(3)}$, so that $\fdomain = \fcell \cup \elt{i}\,\fcell$ is a fundamental domain. It contains one element of every coset, more only on its boundary.
\leavevmode\label{n:set.projective}The space $\grp{D_{\infty}}\backslash\grp{O(3)}$ is two copies of $\grp{D_{\infty}}\backslash\grp{SO(3)}$, the projective plane $\projplane$, and
\begin{equation}\label{e:volume}\vol\fdomain = 2\vol\fcell = 4\pi,\qquad \vol\grp{D_{\infty}}\cdot\vol\fdomain = 4\pi\cdot 4\pi = 16\pi^2 = \vol\grp{O(3)}.\end{equation}
