\leavevmode\label{n:element.inversion}The field \texttt{type} is I when $\grp{S_{20}}$ contains only rotations, II when $\grp{S_{20}}$ contains the inversion $\elt{i}$ and III otherwise. The field \texttt{hk} gives \texttt{H}, the rotation group isomorphic to $\grp{S_{20}}$, and \texttt{K}${} = \grp{S_{20}} \cap \grp{SO(3)}$, with $\grp{S_{20}} = \texttt{K} \cup \elt{i}\,(\texttt{H} \setminus \texttt{K})$ as sets. The fields \texttt{center} and \texttt{commutator\symbol{95}subgroup} are classes of the table of section~\ref{s:subgroups}. The field \texttt{polar} is yes when $\grp{S_{20}}$ fixes a nonzero vector. Crystallographers call a group chiral, or enantiomorphic, when its type is I, and centrosymmetric when it contains $\elt{i}$. The field \texttt{laue\symbol{95}class} is the group generated by $\grp{S_{20}}$ and $\elt{i}$.

\section{Symmetry operations}\label{s:elements}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.4}{4} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.5.1}{5.1}.
\leavevmode\label{n:element.g}\label{n:matrix.element}\label{n:vector.point}\label{n:vector.coordinates}\label{n:element.h}An element $g$ is the matrix $P(g)$ that moves the point $\pointr = (x, y, z)$, a row, to $\pointr\,P(g)$, and products compose as
\begin{equation}\label{e:product}P(g)\,P(h) = P(gh),\qquad gh = \text{first } g\text{, then } h.\end{equation}
On column vectors its matrix is the transpose $P(g)^{\top}$, and products compose in the reverse order.
\leavevmode\label{n:element.rotation.axis}\label{n:angle.omega}\label{n:vector.axis}A proper element is the rotation $\elt{R_{\vv}}(\omega)$ by the angle $\omega$ about the axis $\vv$, counterclockwise as seen from the tip of $\vv$. \label{n:element.rotoreflection.axis}\label{n:element.mirror.axis}An improper element is the rotoreflection
\begin{equation}\label{e:axis}\elt{S_{\vv}}(\omega) = \elt{\sigma_{\vv}}\, \elt{R_{\vv}}(\omega),\end{equation}
where $\elt{\sigma_{\vv}}$ is the reflection in the plane normal to $\vv$ (so $\omega = 0$ is that mirror). \label{n:vector.pole}The axis $\vv$ is an unnormalized direction, and $\elt{E}$ and $\elt{i}$ have none (a dash). \label{n:angle.euler.alpha}\label{n:angle.euler.beta}\label{n:angle.euler.gamma}The Euler angles of the rotation part $(\det g)\,g$ are $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$: by $\alpha$ about $z$, then by $\beta$ about $y$, then by $\gamma$ about $z$ (fixed axes). \label{n:index.element}\label{n:index.element.inverse}\label{n:head.sym}\label{n:head.ord}A row gives the index $k$ of $g$, its symbol (sym), its order (ord) and the index $k^{-1}$ of $g^{-1}$. \label{n:head.class}The class of $g$ is set with its size before its symbol (section~\ref{s:classes}).
\leavevmode\label{n:constant.golden}\label{n:constant.root}\label{n:index.root.order}Exact values are rationals, square roots, cosines and sines of rational multiples of $\pi$, and the constants
\begin{equation}\label{e:constants}\goldenratio = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1+\sqrt{5}\bigr),\qquad \rootunity{n} = \expe^{2\pi\iunit/n}\quad(n = 5, 20),\end{equation}
where $\iunit$ is the imaginary unit and $\expe$ is the base of the natural logarithm.

Every class of subgroups $\grp{H}$ is the stabilizer of a figure in space, the subgroup of the elements that carry it into itself. \label{n:local.orbit.size}\label{n:element.coset.least}\label{n:index.coset}The group $\grp{S_{20}}$ permutes the $N = [\grp{S_{20}} : \grp{H}]$ copies of the figure as it permutes the right cosets $\grp{H}\xi_j$, $j = 0, 1, \dots, N - 1$, ordered by the indices of their least elements $\xi_j$ ($\xi_0 = \cls{E}$). The copy $j$ is the figure moved by $\xi_j$. The matrices $P(g)$ of the defining representation of $\grp{S_{20}}$ (section~\ref{s:elements}) generate the copies, and the root point $\pointr_0$ of the figure moves to the point $\pointr_j = \pointr_0\, P(\xi_j)$ of the copy $j$. \label{n:local.coset.image}The element $g$ moves the copy $j$ to the copy $j'$, where $\grp{H}\xi_j g = \grp{H}\xi_{j'}$. \label{n:local.matrix.permutation}Its permutation matrix $D(g)$ has the one nonzero entry of row $j$ in column $j'$,
\begin{equation}\label{e:perm.matrix}\pointr_j\, P(g) = \pointr_{j'}, \qquad D(g)_{j,j'} = 1,\end{equation}
and it obeys $D(g)\, D(h) = D(gh)$. The record holds the coordinates of the points of every copy, the images $j'$ of every copy under every element and the table of marks (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.12.5}{12.5} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.12.6}{12.6}).

\section{Symmetry-adapted bases}\label{s:bases}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.14.1}{14.1}.
\leavevmode\label{n:index.m}\label{n:vector.ket}The kets $\jm{J}{m}$ are the spherical harmonics (the inversion acts as $(-1)^J$). \label{n:index.ketcoefficient}\label{n:vector.basis.symmetry}\label{n:index.ketweight}\label{n:index.total}A vector is listed by its coefficients $e_m$:
\begin{equation}\label{e:ket}v = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{tot}}}\sum_m e_m\sqrt{F_m}\;\jm{J}{m},\qquad F_m = (J+m)!\,(J-m)!,\qquad \mathrm{tot} = \sum_m |e_m|^2F_m,\end{equation}
\leavevmode\label{n:index.pair.sign}so a single ket has amplitude $\pm1$, and from $J = 10$ on a pair $\jm{J}{m} + s\,\jm{J}{-m}$, $s = \pm1$, has amplitudes $\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}$ and $\tfrac{1}{2}s\sqrt{2}$, or these times $\iunit$. Each $e_m$ is $\pm1$ or $\pm\iunit$.
\leavevmode\label{n:group.A}\label{n:function.character.abelian}The subgroup $\grp{A} = \grp{S_{20}}\cap\grp{C_{\infty h}}$ acts diagonally on the kets, on $\jm{J}{m}$ by the character $\chiA(m)$, and $\grp{S_{20}} = \grp{A}$. For $m > 0$, when $\chiA(m) \ne \chiA(-m)$, the kets $\jm{J}{m}$ and $\jm{J}{-m}$ are the blocks of the two irreps of a complex pair: the block of the first irrep has $e = 1$, and that of its partner $e = (-1)^m$, so that time reversal takes the first to the second. Otherwise they give two one-dimensional blocks $\jm{J}{m} + s\,\jm{J}{-m}$, $s = \pm1$, the one with $s = -(-1)^m$ times $\iunit$. The factor $\iunit$ makes each block adapted to time reversal (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.14}{14}). Blocks are ordered by $J$, then $|m|$, $+m$ before $-m$, a pair with $s = +1$ first (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.14.1}{14.1}).

\section{Invariant polynomials}\label{s:invariant_polynomials}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.13.1}{13.1}.
The invariants are the polynomials in $x$, $y$, $z$ that take the same value at $\pointr$ and at $\pointr\,P(g)$ for every point $\pointr$ and every element $g$. They form a ring with the Hilbert series $H_{\irr{a}}(t)/(1-t^{2}) = (1 + 2t^{11} + t^{20})/((1-t^{2})(1-t^{2})(1-t^{20}))$, whose coefficient of a power of $t$ is the number of linearly independent invariants of that degree. \label{n:head.degree}\label{n:head.role}A row is an invariant of a minimal generating set of the ring (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.13.1}{13.1}), with its degree, the invariant (polynomial) and its role, primary or secondary. Every invariant is in one way a sum of 1 and the secondary invariants, each times a polynomial in the primary invariants.
