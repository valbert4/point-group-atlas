\section{Symmetry operations}\label{s:elements}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.4}{4} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.5.1}{5.1}.
\leavevmode\label{n:element.g}\label{n:matrix.element}\label{n:vector.point}\label{n:vector.coordinates}\label{n:element.h}An element $g$ is the matrix $P(g)$ that moves the point $\pointr = (x, y, z)$, a row, to $\pointr\,P(g)$, and products compose as
\begin{equation}\label{e:product}P(g)\,P(h) = P(gh),\qquad gh = \text{first } g\text{, then } h.\end{equation}
On column vectors its matrix is the transpose $P(g)^{\top}$, and products compose in the reverse order.
\leavevmode\label{n:element.rotation.axis}\label{n:angle.omega}\label{n:vector.axis}A proper element is the rotation $\elt{R_{\vv}}(\omega)$ by the angle $\omega$ about the axis $\vv$, counterclockwise as seen from the tip of $\vv$. \label{n:element.rotoreflection.axis}\label{n:element.mirror.axis}An improper element is the rotoreflection
\begin{equation}\label{e:axis}\elt{S_{\vv}}(\omega) = \elt{\sigma_{\vv}}\, \elt{R_{\vv}}(\omega),\end{equation}
where $\elt{\sigma_{\vv}}$ is the reflection in the plane normal to $\vv$ (so $\omega = 0$ is that mirror). \label{n:vector.pole}The axis $\vv$ is an unnormalized direction, and $\elt{E}$ and $\elt{i}$ have none (a dash). \label{n:angle.euler.alpha}\label{n:angle.euler.beta}\label{n:angle.euler.gamma}The Euler angles of the rotation part $(\det g)\,g$ are $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$: by $\alpha$ about $z$, then by $\beta$ about $y$, then by $\gamma$ about $z$ (fixed axes). \label{n:index.element}\label{n:index.element.inverse}\label{n:head.sym}\label{n:head.ord}A row gives the index $k$ of $g$, its symbol (sym), its order (ord) and the index $k^{-1}$ of $g^{-1}$. \label{n:head.class}The class of $g$ is set with its size before its symbol (section~\ref{s:classes}).

\leavevmode\label{n:element.placement}A molecule with symmetry $\grp{D_{7}}$ placed by $Q \in \grp{O(3)}$ is the same set of atoms when placed by $gQ$ with $g \in \grp{D_{7}}$, since $P(gQ) = P(g)\,P(Q)$. \label{n:operator.cosets}Thus its orientations are the cosets $\grp{D_{7}}Q = \{gQ : g \in \grp{D_{7}}\}$, the points of the space $\grp{D_{7}}\backslash\grp{O(3)}$. \leavevmode\label{n:operator.volume}Volumes are those of the invariant metric of \href{O3.pdf}{$\grp{O(3)}$} in which the distance from $\elt{E}$ to a rotation by $\omega \le \pi$ is $\omega$, so that $\vol\grp{SO(3)} = 8\pi^2$ and $\vol\grp{O(3)} = 16\pi^2$. \leavevmode\label{n:vector.rodrigues}\label{n:vector.unitaxis}A rotation $\elt{R_{\vv}}(\omega)$ with $0 \le \omega \le \pi$ has the Rodrigues vector $\rodrigues = \tan(\omega/2)\,\unitaxis$, where $\unitaxis = \vv/\lVert\vv\rVert$ is its unit axis. Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.12.4}{12.4} to \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.12.6}{12.6}.
Every class of subgroups $\grp{H}$ is the stabilizer of a figure in space, the subgroup of the elements that carry it into itself. \label{n:local.orbit.size}\label{n:element.coset.least}\label{n:index.coset}The group $\grp{D_{7}}$ permutes the $N = [\grp{D_{7}} : \grp{H}]$ copies of the figure as it permutes the right cosets $\grp{H}\xi_j$, $j = 0, 1, \dots, N - 1$, ordered by the indices of their least elements $\xi_j$ ($\xi_0 = \cls{E}$). The copy $j$ is the figure moved by $\xi_j$. The matrices $P(g)$ of the defining representation of $\grp{D_{7}}$ (section~\ref{s:elements}) generate the copies, and the root point $\pointr_0$ of the figure moves to the point $\pointr_j = \pointr_0\, P(\xi_j)$ of the copy $j$. \label{n:local.coset.image}The element $g$ moves the copy $j$ to the copy $j'$, where $\grp{H}\xi_j g = \grp{H}\xi_{j'}$. \label{n:local.matrix.permutation}Its permutation matrix $D(g)$ has the one nonzero entry of row $j$ in column $j'$,
\begin{equation}\label{e:perm.matrix}\pointr_j\, P(g) = \pointr_{j'}, \qquad D(g)_{j,j'} = 1,\end{equation}
and it obeys $D(g)\, D(h) = D(gh)$. The record holds the coordinates of the points of every copy, the images $j'$ of every copy under every element and the table of marks (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.12.5}{12.5} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.12.6}{12.6}).
\leavevmode\label{n:head.figure}The figure of a class is the first of these whose stabilizer is $\grp{H}$: the point $\pointr_0$, the line through the origin along $\pointr_0$, a spin, a spinning arrow and a helix. Otherwise the figure is the least orbit of $\pointr_0$ under $\grp{H}$ whose stabilizer is $\grp{H}$, a segment, a polygon or a polyhedron. A spin is the axial vector $\pointr_0$, a sense of rotation about its line. A spinning arrow is the arrow $\pointr_0$ with a sense of rotation about itself, and a helix is the line along $\pointr_0$ with a handedness. These two figures are tried only when $\grp{H}$ has no improper element and $\grp{D_{7}}$ has one. The table lists the classes of subgroups in the order and with the names of the table of section~\ref{s:subgroups}. \label{n:head.site}A row gives $N$, the figure by its shape or number of points, the stabilizer of the point $\pointr_0$ (site), which tells the symmetry elements of $\grp{D_{7}}$ that the figure lies on, and $\pointr_0$. \label{n:head.vertices}The column vertices gives what the points of all the copies form (the tips of the arrows and spins, both ends of the lines and helices), with ${\times}k$ when $k > 1$ copies pass through each. \label{n:head.rank}The rank counts the classes of pairs of copies under $\grp{D_{7}}$, including that of a copy with itself. \label{n:head.cos}For a point, line or spin of rank at most 5, the column cos gives the cosine of the angle between two copies (of the acute angle, for lines) for each remaining class of pairs. \label{n:head.hand}The column hand gives 2 when $\grp{H}$ has no improper element and $\grp{D_{7}}$ has one, so that the rotations of $\grp{D_{7}}$ leave the copies in two classes, mirror images of each other. These classes are the left- and the right-handed copies of a chiral figure.

The kernel of the permutation representation of a class of subgroups is the subgroup of the elements that leave every copy in place. The decomposition is that of $D$, whose character at $g$ is the number of copies that $g$ leaves in place. \leavevmode\label{n:index.m}\label{n:vector.ket}The kets $\jm{J}{m}$ are the spherical harmonics (the inversion acts as $(-1)^J$). \label{n:index.ketcoefficient}\label{n:vector.basis.symmetry}\label{n:index.ketweight}\label{n:index.total}A vector is listed by its coefficients $e_m$:
\begin{equation}\label{e:ket}v = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{tot}}}\sum_m e_m\sqrt{F_m}\;\jm{J}{m},\qquad F_m = (J+m)!\,(J-m)!,\qquad \mathrm{tot} = \sum_m |e_m|^2F_m,\end{equation}
\leavevmode\label{n:index.pair.sign}so a single ket has amplitude $\pm1$, and from $J = 7$ on a pair $\jm{J}{m} + s\,\jm{J}{-m}$, $s = \pm1$, has amplitudes $\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}$ and $\tfrac{1}{2}s\sqrt{2}$. \section{Tensor spaces}\label{s:tensor_spaces}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.13}{13}.
\leavevmode\label{n:head.name}\label{n:head.description}\label{n:head.dim}\label{n:head.decomposition}A row is a space of Cartesian tensors, with its Jahn symbol (name), its description in words, its dimension (dim) and the irreps of $\grp{D_{7}}$ that it carries, with their multiplicities (decomposition). \label{n:label.jahn}The same symbol names the space in the isotypic components (V a polar vector, e an axial factor, a power the number of factors, brackets their symmetrized product). The descriptions name the symmetric polar tensors of a rank (the homogeneous polynomials of that degree), the axial vectors and the symmetric axial tensors of ranks 2 (optical activity) and 3. The elastic stiffness tensors are symmetric in their first two indices, in their last two and under the exchange of the two pairs. The photoelastic tensors are symmetric in their first two and in their last two indices, as are the electrostriction and the quadratic electro-optic tensors. The piezoelectric tensors are symmetric in their last two indices, the third-harmonic susceptibilities in their last three and the electrogyration tensors, which are axial, in their first two. The general polar tensors of rank 4 have no symmetry.

\section{Subduction from \texorpdfstring{$\grp{U(\mathnormal{N})}$}{U(N)}}\label{s:unitary}

\leavevmode\label{n:local.level.dimension}A level of $\grp{D_{7}}$ is a real irrep $\Gamma$ of dimension $N \ge 2$: a degenerate mode of vibration, or a set of degenerate orbitals. \label{n:group.unitary}Its matrices $\repD{\Gamma}(g)$ lie in $\grp{U(\mathnormal{N})}$. \label{n:irrep.schur}\label{n:shape.partition}\label{n:local.partition}\label{n:function.multiplicity.unitary}The branching rule $\grp{U(\mathnormal{N})} \supset \grp{D_{7}}$ gives the multiplicity $b_\Lambda(\lambda)$ of the irrep $\Lambda$ of $\grp{D_{7}}$ in the irrep $\schur{\lambda}$ of $\grp{U(\mathnormal{N})}$ of highest weight $\lambda = \shape{\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots}$, restricted along $\repD{\Gamma}$. \label{n:local.partition.power}\label{n:local.electrons}\label{n:local.spin}The quanta of a degenerate mode fill the one-row shapes $\schur{\shape{v}}\Gamma = \operatorname{Sym}^{v}\Gamma$ (section~\ref{s:overtones}), and $n$ electrons of spin $S$ fill the two-column shapes $\lambda = \shape{2^{n/2-S}, 1^{2S}}$ (section~\ref{s:terms}).

\section{Vibrational overtone levels}\label{s:overtones}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.19}{19}.
\leavevmode\label{n:index.quanta}\label{n:operator.symmetric.power}The states of $v$ quanta of a degenerate mode $\Gamma$, a level of section~\ref{s:unitary}, carry $\operatorname{Sym}^{v}\Gamma = \schur{\shape{v}}\Gamma$, and $b_\Lambda(v) = b_\Lambda(\shape{v})$. \label{n:local.quadratic.form}\label{n:set.harmonic}They split by harmonic degree $\ell = v, v - 2, \dots$ into $r^{v-\ell}H_\ell$, where $r^2$ is the invariant quadratic form of the level and $H_\ell$ consists of the states of $\ell$ quanta annihilated by the invariant Laplacian. \label{n:function.multiplicity.harmonic}Thus the multiplicity $\mathnormal{h}_\Lambda(\ell)$ of $\Lambda$ in $H_\ell$ is the same at every $v$, and $b_\Lambda(v) = \mathnormal{h}_\Lambda(v) + \mathnormal{h}_\Lambda(v - 2) + \dots$. \label{n:function.generating.level}With $H^{\Gamma}_\Lambda(t) = \sum_{\ell\ge0} \mathnormal{h}_\Lambda(\ell)\,t^{\ell}$, Molien's formula reads
\begin{equation}\label{e:molien}\frac{H^{\Gamma}_\Lambda(t)}{1 - t^2} = \sum_{v\ge0} b_\Lambda(v)\,t^v = \frac{1}{|\grp{D_{7}}|}\sum_{g}\frac{\conj{\irchar{\Lambda}(g)}}{\det\bigl(1 - t\,\repD{\Gamma}(g)\bigr)} = \frac{P_\Lambda(t)}{\prod_k (1-t^{a_k})},\end{equation}
where the factor $1/(1 - t^2) = 1 + t^2 + t^4 + \dots$ counts the powers of $r^2$, which raise $v$ by 2 and keep the irrep. The denominator is that of least $\prod_k a_k$ for which every $P_\Lambda(t)$ has nonnegative integer coefficients. Here $a_1 = 2$ is the degree of $r^2$, so that $H^{\Gamma}_\Lambda(t) = P_\Lambda(t)/\prod_{k\ge2}(1 - t^{a_k})$. The Hilbert series of the polynomials in the partners of the level that $\grp{D_{7}}$ leaves invariant is $H^{\Gamma}_{\irr{a}_{1}}(t)/(1 - t^2)$. For a level of dimension 2 the invariants are the polynomials in $r^2$ and in one invariant of degree $a_2$. \label{n:index.overtone.period}\label{n:index.increment.unitary}For such a level, $\ell$ is the vibrational angular momentum, and $b_\Lambda(v + L) = b_\Lambda(v) + \Delta_\Lambda$ at the $v$ of the parity at which $\Lambda$ occurs, where $L$ is the least common multiple of the $a_k$. \label{n:local.torsion.angle}The functions $\expe^{\mp\iunit\tau}$ of a torsion angle $\tau$ that $\grp{D_{7}}$ maps affinely may transform as the partners of such a level (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.19}{19}). The angle's free-rotor states $\expe^{\mp\iunit\ell\tau}$ are then $\occ{\ell 0}$ and $\occ{0 \ell}$, of harmonic degree $\ell$. A table of numerators leaves out the irreps that occur at no $v$. The vector is $\mathrm{tot}^{-1/2}\sum e\,\sdet{\dots}$, where $\mathrm{tot} = \sum |e|^2$, and lowering the spin gives the other components.
