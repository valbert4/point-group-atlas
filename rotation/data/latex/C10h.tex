\leavevmode\label{n:element.g}\label{n:matrix.element}\label{n:vector.point}\label{n:vector.coordinates}\label{n:element.h}An element $g$ is the matrix $P(g)$ that moves the point $\pointr = (x, y, z)$, a row, to $\pointr\,P(g)$; products compose as
\begin{equation}\label{e:product}P(g)\,P(h) = P(gh),\qquad gh = \text{first } g\text{, then } h.\end{equation}
On column vectors the matrix of $g$ is the transpose $P(g)^{\top}$, and products compose in the reverse order.
\leavevmode\label{n:element.rotation.axis}\label{n:angle.omega}\label{n:vector.axis}\label{n:element.rotoreflection.axis}\label{n:element.mirror.axis}A proper element is the rotation $\elt{R_{\vv}}(\omega)$ by the angle $\omega$ about the axis $\vv$, counterclockwise as seen from the tip of $\vv$; an improper element is the rotoreflection
\begin{equation}\label{e:axis}\elt{S_{\vv}}(\omega) = \elt{\sigma_{\vv}}\, \elt{R_{\vv}}(\omega),\end{equation}
$\elt{\sigma_{\vv}}$ the reflection in the plane normal to $\vv$ (so $\omega = 0$ is that mirror). \label{n:vector.pole}$\elt{E}$ and $\elt{i}$ have no axis (a dash); $\vv$ is a direction, not normalized. \label{n:angle.euler.alpha}\label{n:angle.euler.beta}\label{n:angle.euler.gamma}$(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ are the Euler angles of the rotation $(\det g)\,g$: by $\alpha$ about $z$, then $\beta$ about $y$, then $\gamma$ about $z$ (fixed axes). \label{n:index.element}\label{n:index.element.inverse}\label{n:head.sym}\label{n:head.class}\label{n:head.ord}$k$ is the index of $g$, sym its symbol and class its conjugacy class, with the class size before the symbol (section~\ref{s:classes}); ord is its order and $k^{-1}$ the index of $g^{-1}$.
\leavevmode\label{n:constant.golden}\label{n:constant.root}\label{n:index.root.order}Exact values are rational numbers, square roots, the cosines and sines of rational multiples of $\pi$, and the constants
\begin{equation}\label{e:constants}\goldenratio = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1+\sqrt{5}\bigr),\qquad \rootunity{n} = \expe^{2\pi\iunit/n}\quad(n = 5),\end{equation}
with $\iunit$ the imaginary unit and $\expe$ the base of the natural logarithm.

Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.17}{17}.
\leavevmode\label{n:element.placement}\label{n:operator.cosets}A molecule with symmetry $\grp{C_{10h}}$ placed by $Q \in \grp{O(3)}$ is the same set of atoms when placed by $gQ$ with $g \in \grp{C_{10h}}$, as $P(gQ) = P(g)\,P(Q)$: its orientations are the cosets $\grp{C_{10h}}Q = \{gQ : g \in \grp{C_{10h}}\}$, the points of the space $\grp{C_{10h}}\backslash\grp{O(3)}$. On column vectors the matrices of a coset are $P(Q)^{\top}P(g)^{\top}$, a left coset of the transposes and a point of $\grp{O(3)}/\grp{C_{10h}}$.
\leavevmode\label{n:operator.volume}Volumes are those of the invariant metric of \href{O3.pdf}{$\grp{O(3)}$} in which the distance from $\elt{E}$ to a rotation by $\omega \le \pi$ is $\omega$, so that $\vol\grp{SO(3)} = 8\pi^2$ and $\vol\grp{O(3)} = 16\pi^2$; a finite group has the counting measure, $\vol\grp{C_{10h}} = |\grp{C_{10h}}| = 20$.

\leavevmode\label{n:vector.rodrigues}\label{n:vector.unitaxis}A rotation $\elt{R_{\vv}}(\omega)$ with $0 \le \omega \le \pi$ has the Rodrigues vector $\rodrigues = \tan(\omega/2)\,\unitaxis$, $\unitaxis = \vv/\lVert\vv\rVert$ its unit axis, the rotations by $\pi$ at infinity; the volume element of \href{SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$} is $8\,(1 + \lVert\rodrigues\rVert^2)^{-2}\,\diff^3\rodrigues$.
\leavevmode\label{n:set.cell}The rotations nearer to $\elt{E}$ than to any other element of $\grp{C_{10h}} \cap \grp{SO(3)}$ form the cell
\begin{equation}\label{e:cell}\fcell = \bigl\{\rodrigues : \lvert\rodrigues\cdot\unitaxis_g\rvert \le \tan(\omega_g/4)\ \text{for every $g$ of the table}\bigr\},\end{equation}

\leavevmode\label{n:index.dimension}\label{n:index.frobenius}\label{n:mark.determinant}\label{n:head.irrep}\label{n:head.reality}\label{n:head.conjugate}\label{n:head.over.real}\label{n:head.kernel}\label{n:head.image}$d$ is the dimension of the irrep and $\fsind = |\grp{C_{10h}}|^{-1}\allowbreak\sum_{g\in\grp{C_{10h}}}\irchar{\Gamma}(g^2)$ its Frobenius--Schur indicator, which gives its reality: real ($\fsind = +1$), pseudoreal ($\fsind = -1$) or complex ($\fsind = 0$, paired with its conjugate); conjugate is the conjugate of a complex irrep (a dash for the others), over $\mathbb{R}$ the irrep over $\mathbb{R}$ in which it lies; kernel the subgroup $\ker\Gamma$ of the elements that it maps to the identity, named as in section~\ref{s:subgroups}; and image the group that its matrices form, isomorphic to $\grp{C_{10h}}/\ker\Gamma$. The image of an irrep of dimension 1 is the cyclic group of the characters, roots of unity that turn the complex plane. The irreps are irreducible over $\mathbb{C}$. A real irrep, in a basis with real matrices, is also an irrep over $\mathbb{R}$; the two irreps $\conjpair{1}\irr{e}$, $\conjpair{2}\irr{e}$ of a complex-conjugate pair together form one irrep over $\mathbb{R}$ of dimension $2d$ and character $2\operatorname{Re}\irchar{\conjpair{1}\irr{e}}$, labeled $\irr{e}$ (the label without its prefix; \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.6.4}{6.4} and \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.7}{7}). Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, sections \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.12.4}{12.4} to \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.12.6}{12.6}.
\leavevmode\label{n:local.orbit.size}\label{n:element.coset.least}\label{n:index.coset}\label{n:local.coset.image}\label{n:local.matrix.permutation}Every class of subgroups $\grp{H}$ is the stabilizer of a figure in space, the subgroup of the elements that carry it into itself, and $\grp{C_{10h}}$ permutes the $N = [\grp{C_{10h}} : \grp{H}]$ copies of the figure as it permutes the right cosets $\grp{H}\xi_j$, $j = 0, 1, \dots, N - 1$, $\xi_j$ the element of least index of its coset, in the order of these indices ($\xi_0 = \cls{E}$): the copy $j$ is the figure moved by $\xi_j$. The element $g$ moves the copy $j$ to the copy $j'$, where $\grp{H}\xi_j g = \grp{H}\xi_{j'}$, and the permutation matrix $D(g)$, whose row $j$ has its one nonzero entry in column $j'$,

\section{Invariant polynomials}\label{s:invariant_polynomials}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.13.1}{13.1}.
The polynomials in $x$, $y$, $z$ that take the same value at $\pointr$ and at $\pointr\,P(g)$ for every point $\pointr$ and every element $g$, the invariants, form a ring with the Hilbert series $H_{\irr{a}_{\ptag{g}}}(t)/(1-t^{2}) = (1 + t^{10})/((1-t^{2})(1-t^{2})(1-t^{10}))$, in which the coefficient of a power of $t$ is the number of linearly independent invariants of that degree. \label{n:head.degree}\label{n:head.role}A row is an invariant of a minimal generating set of the ring (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.13.1}{13.1}): degree is its degree, polynomial the invariant and role whether it is a primary or a secondary invariant; every invariant is in one way a sum of 1 and the secondary invariants, each times a polynomial in the primary invariants.
