\leavevmode\label{n:element.inversion}The field \texttt{type} is I when $\grp{O(3)}$ contains only rotations, II when $\grp{O(3)}$ contains the inversion $\elt{i}$ and III otherwise. The field \texttt{polar} is yes when $\grp{O(3)}$ fixes a nonzero vector. Crystallographers call a group chiral, or enantiomorphic, when its type is I, and centrosymmetric when it contains $\elt{i}$. The field \texttt{laue\symbol{95}class} is the group generated by $\grp{O(3)}$ and $\elt{i}$.
In \texttt{product\symbol{95}form}, after a semidirect product, ${\times}\infty$ marks infinitely many subgroups conjugate to its second factor. The braces contain the classes of $\grp{O(3)}$ that the finite second factor meets: atoms of the class space (section~\ref{s:class_space}) and distinguished points of its arcs (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.16}{16}).

\section{Class space}\label{s:class_space}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.16}{16}.
\leavevmode\label{n:angle.phi}\label{n:head.component}A row is a component of the class space of $\grp{O(3)}$, an arc of classes. \label{n:head.coset}\label{n:head.element}A row gives whether its elements are proper or improper (coset) and the element about $z$ of the class at its angle. The matrix $P$ of the element is in the row-vector convention (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.4}{4}). On column vectors its matrix is the transpose $P^{\top}$, and products compose in the reverse order.
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}\toprule
component & coset & element & $P$\\\midrule
$\comp{C(\phi)}$ & proper & $\comp{C(\phi)}$ & $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\phi & \sin\phi & 0\\-\sin\phi & \cos\phi & 0\\0 & 0 & 1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$\\\addlinespace[6pt]
$\comp{i\,C(\phi)}$ & improper & $\comp{i\,C(\phi)}$ & $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-\cos\phi & -\sin\phi & 0\\\sin\phi & -\cos\phi & 0\\0 & 0 & -1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$\\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
The component $\comp{C(\phi)}$ runs over the classes of the rotations by $\phi$ about any axis, from $\cls{E}$ to $\cls{C_2}$, the rotations by $\pi$. The component $\comp{i\,C(\phi)}$ runs over the classes of the products of these rotations with the inversion, from $\cls{i}$ to $\cls{\sigma_h}$, the mirrors.
\leavevmode\label{n:local.haar.h}The Haar measure, of total mass 1, is $\diff h = \bigl(1-\cos\phi\bigr)\,\diff\phi/(2\pi)$ on $\comp{C(\phi)}$, $\phi\in\bigl[0, \pi\bigr]$, of mass $\tfrac{1}{2}$, and $\diff h = \bigl(1-\cos\phi\bigr)\,\diff\phi/(2\pi)$ on $\comp{i\,C(\phi)}$, $\phi\in\bigl[0, \pi\bigr]$, of mass $\tfrac{1}{2}$.

\section{Fundamental domain}\label{s:fundamental_domain}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.17}{17}.
\leavevmode\label{n:element.placement}A molecule with symmetry $\grp{O(3)}$ placed by $Q \in \grp{O(3)}$ is the same set of atoms when placed by $gQ$ with $g \in \grp{O(3)}$, since $P(gQ) = P(g)\,P(Q)$. \label{n:operator.cosets}Thus its orientations are the cosets $\grp{O(3)}Q = \{gQ : g \in \grp{O(3)}\}$, the points of the space $\grp{O(3)}\backslash\grp{O(3)}$. On column vectors a coset is the left coset of the transposes $P(Q)^{\top}P(g)^{\top}$, $g \in \grp{O(3)}$, a point of $\grp{O(3)}/\grp{O(3)}$.
\leavevmode\label{n:operator.volume}Volumes are those of the invariant metric of $\grp{O(3)}$ in which the distance from $\elt{E}$ to a rotation by $\omega \le \pi$ is $\omega$, so that $\vol\grp{SO(3)} = 8\pi^2$ and $\vol\grp{O(3)} = 16\pi^2$.

\subsection{Matrices of a general element}\label{s:general}
\leavevmode\label{n:matrix.rotation}An element $g$ is its rotation part $R = (\det g)\,g$, times $\elt{i}$ when $\det g = -1$. \label{n:element.rotation.axis}The Euler angles of $R$ are $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.4.2}{4.2}): by $\alpha$ about $z$, then by $\beta$ about $y$, then by $\gamma$ about $z$ (fixed axes), that is, $R = \elt{R_z}(\alpha)\,\elt{R_y}(\beta)\,\elt{R_z}(\gamma)$. \label{n:index.parity}The parity $p$ of the family is $+1$ for $\irrJp{J}{+}$ and $-1$ for $\irrJp{J}{-}$, and the bracket $\iverson{\det g = -1}$ is 1 when $\det g = -1$ and 0 otherwise. \label{n:matrix.irrep.entry}In the basis $\jm{J}{m}$, rows $m'$ and columns $m = J, J-1, \dots, -J$, the matrix of $g$ is
\begin{equation}\label{e:euler}D_{m',m}(g) = p^{\iverson{\det g = -1}}\,\expe^{\iunit m'\alpha}\,\wignerd{J}_{m,m'}(\beta)\,\expe^{\iunit m\gamma} = p^{\iverson{\det g = -1}}\,(-1)^{m-m'}\,\expe^{\iunit m'\alpha}\,\wignerd{J}_{m',m}(\beta)\,\expe^{\iunit m\gamma},\end{equation}
\leavevmode\label{n:matrix.wigner}\label{n:matrix.operator}\label{n:operator.angularmomentum}that is, $D(g) = p^{\iverson{\det g = -1}}\,\wignerD{J}(R)^\dagger$ with $\wignerD{J}_{m',m}(R) = \matel{J}{m'}{U(R)}{J}{m}$ and $U(R) = \expe^{-\iunit\gamma J_z}\expe^{-\iunit\beta J_y}\expe^{-\iunit\alpha J_z}$, since $R$ acts on the kets as $U(R^{-1})$ (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.4.1}{4.1}). Here $J_y$ and $J_z$ are the components of the angular momentum operator about $y$ and $z$. \label{n:matrix.wigner.small}\label{n:index.halfangle}\label{n:local.sum.wigner}The Wigner matrix [\href{../../../REFERENCES.pdf\#ref.VMK88}{VMK88}, section 4.3] is
\begin{equation}\label{e:wigner}\begin{aligned}\wignerd{J}_{m',m}(\beta) &= \matel{J}{m'}{\expe^{-\iunit\beta J_y}}{J}{m}\\ &= \sum_\sigma (-1)^{m'-m+\sigma}\,\frac{\sqrt{(J+m')!\,(J-m')!\,(J+m)!\,(J-m)!}}{(J+m-\sigma)!\,\sigma!\,(m'-m+\sigma)!\,(J-m'-\sigma)!}\,c_\beta^{2J+m-m'-2\sigma}s_\beta^{m'-m+2\sigma},\end{aligned}\end{equation}
where $c_\beta = \cos\tfrac{\beta}{2}$ and $s_\beta = \sin\tfrac{\beta}{2}$, and the sum runs over the $\sigma$ for which every factorial argument is $\ge 0$. For the rotoreflection $\elt{S_{\vv}}(\omega)$ (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.4.2}{4.2}), $R = \elt{R_{\vv}}(\omega + \pi)$. \label{n:vector.unitaxis}\label{n:vector.axis.components}\label{n:index.cayleyklein}\label{n:local.sum.cayleyklein}In the angle $\phi$ and the unit axis $\unitaxis = \vv/\lVert\vv\rVert = (\hat{v}_x, \hat{v}_y, \hat{v}_z)$ of the rotation $\elt{R_{\vv}}(\phi)$, the matrix is
\begin{equation}\label{e:angleaxis}\begin{aligned}D_{m',m}(g) &= p^{\iverson{\det g = -1}}\,\sqrt{(J+m')!\,(J-m')!\,(J+m)!\,(J-m)!}\\ &\quad\times\sum_k\frac{a^{J+m-k}\,(\conj{a})^{J-m'-k}\,b^{m'-m+k}\,(-\conj{b})^k}{(J-m'-k)!\,(J+m-k)!\,(m'-m+k)!\,k!},\end{aligned}\end{equation}
where $a = \cos\tfrac{\phi}{2} + \iunit\hat{v}_z\sin\tfrac{\phi}{2}$ and $b = (\hat{v}_y + \iunit\hat{v}_x)\sin\tfrac{\phi}{2}$, and the sum runs over the $k$ for which every factorial argument is $\ge 0$.
\par At a rotation $R$ that moves $(0, 0, r)$ to the point $\pointr = (x, y, z)$, the row $m' = 0$ of (\ref{e:euler}) gives the kets $\jm{J}{m}$, the spherical harmonics $r^J Y_J^m$ (\href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.4.1}{4.1}). where the sum runs over the $k$ for which every factorial argument is $\ge 0$.

\section{Clebsch--Gordan coefficients}\label{s:clebsch_gordan}

\begin{romanlist}\raggedright
\item Every irrep in the product of two irreps (section~\ref{s:fusion}) occurs once: \href{SO3.pdf}{$\grp{SO(3)}$} and \href{O3.pdf}{$\grp{O(3)}$} are simply reducible.

\item Symmetries [\href{../../../REFERENCES.pdf\#ref.VMK88}{VMK88}, section 8.4]: $\cgc{J_1}{m_1}{J_2}{m_2}{J}{M} = (-1)^{J_1+J_2-J}\cgc{J_2}{m_2}{J_1}{m_1}{J}{M} = (-1)^{J_1+J_2-J}\cgc{J_1}{{-m_1}}{J_2}{{-m_2}}{J}{{-M}}$.

\leavevmode\label{n:index.quanta}\label{n:operator.symmetric.power}The states of $v$ quanta of a degenerate mode $\Gamma = \irrJp{J}{p}$, a level of section~\ref{s:unitary}, carry $\operatorname{Sym}^{v}\Gamma$. \label{n:local.quadratic.form}\label{n:set.harmonic}They split by harmonic degree $\ell = v, v - 2, \dots$ into $r^{v-\ell}H_\ell$, where $r^2$ is the invariant quadratic form of the level and $H_\ell$ consists of the states of $\ell$ quanta annihilated by the invariant Laplacian. \section{Overtone states for \texorpdfstring{$v \le 4$}{v \textleq\ 4}}\label{s:overtone_states}
Conventions: \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.19}{19}.
The table gives the highest-weight states of $v \le 4$ quanta of $\irrJ{1}$ and $\irrJ{2}$ by the rule of \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf}{CONVENTIONS.pdf}, section \href{../../CONVENTIONS.pdf\#conv.19}{19}. These states are partner 1 ($m = J'$) of each copy of $\irrJ{J'}$ in each $r^{v-\ell}H_\ell$. \label{n:vector.occupation}\label{n:local.quanta.partner}The state $\occ{v_1 \dots v_N}$ has $v_1, \dots, v_N$ quanta in the partners $\ket{J, J}, \dots, \ket{J, -J}$ in turn. A coefficient is that of the monomial in the creation operators, a rational times a square root.
