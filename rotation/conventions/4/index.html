4 Elements ¶

4.1 Matrices, products and the action on functions ¶

The picture is active: an operation moves points, and the axes and symmetry elements stay in place. A rotation by a positive angle about a pole is counterclockwise as seen from the pole's tip (the right-hand rule). An element's matrix P(𝘨)P(\mathsfit{g}) is exact (section 8) and in the row-vector convention: a point 𝐫=(x,y,z)\mathbf{r} = (x, y, z), written as a row, is moved to 𝐫 P(𝘨)\mathbf{r}\, P(\mathsfit{g}), and

(4.1)P(𝘨) P(𝘩)=P(𝘨𝘩),𝘨𝘩=the element that applies 𝘨 first, then 𝘩.P(\mathsfit{g})\, P(\mathsfit{h}) = P(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{h}), \qquad \mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{h} = \text{the element that applies } \mathsfit{g} \text{ first, then } \mathsfit{h}.

The rotation 𝘙𝘻(ϕ)\mathsfit{R_{z}}(\phi) by ϕ\phi about zz has the matrix P(𝘙𝘻(ϕ))=(cos⁡ϕsin⁡ϕ0−sin⁡ϕcos⁡ϕ0001)P(\mathsfit{R_{z}}(\phi)) = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} \cos\phi & \sin\phi & 0 \\ -\sin\phi & \cos\phi & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right). A library that uses column vectors works with the transposes P(𝘨)⊤P(\mathsfit{g})^{\top} and reverses every product (section 4.3).

The field elements.items[] lists the elements, and index is the position in the list and the key by which every other field names an element. The entry multiplication_table[g][h] is the index of the element whose matrix is P(𝘨) P(𝘩)P(\mathsfit{g})\, P(\mathsfit{h}).

An element acts on functions by

(4.2)(𝘨f)(𝐫)=f(𝐫 P(𝘨)),(\mathsfit{g} f)(\mathbf{r}) = f(\mathbf{r}\, P(\mathsfit{g})),

which composes in the order of the multiplication table, 𝘨(𝘩f)=(𝘨𝘩)f\mathsfit{g}(\mathsfit{h} f) = (\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{h}) f. On the coordinates xjx_j, the entries of 𝐫=(x,y,z)\mathbf{r} = (x, y, z) in order, 𝘨 xj=∑ixiP(𝘨)ij\mathsfit{g}\, x_j = \sum_i x_i P(\mathsfit{g})_{ij}. On kets ∣ mJ⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle with ⟨𝐫∣ mJ⟩=rJYJm\bigl\langle\mathbf{r}\bigm\vert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle = r^J Y_J^m (r=∥𝐫∥r = \lVert\mathbf{r}\rVert, Condon–Shortley phases), the action is the operator U(𝘨−1)U(\mathsfit{g}^{-1}) of section 9: 𝘙𝘻(ϕ)∣ mJ⟩=eimϕ∣ mJ⟩\mathsfit{R_{z}}(\phi) \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle = \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}m\phi} \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle, and 𝘙𝘻(2π/N)\mathsfit{R_{z}}(2\pi/N) multiplies x+iyx + \mathrm{i}y by ζN\zeta_N. Section 9 writes these kets as polynomials in xx, yy, zz.

4.2 Fields of elements ¶

The field elements has count, identity_index and items[]. Each entry of items[] has the fields from det to operation in this list:

  1. count: ∣𝖦∣\lvert \mathsf{G}\rvert.
  2. identity_index: 0.
  3. det: ±1\pm 1.
  4. order: the least k≥1k \ge 1 with 𝘨k=𝘌\mathsfit{g}^k = \mathsfit{E}.
  5. inverse: the index of 𝘨−1\mathsfit{g}^{-1}.
  6. angle: [p, q], the turn p/qp/q in [0,1)[0, 1), in lowest terms, of the angle ω=2πp/q\omega = 2\pi p/q. A proper 𝘨\mathsfit{g} is the rotation 𝘙𝐯(ω)\mathsfit{R_{\mathbf{v}}}(\omega) by ω\omega about its pole 𝐯\mathbf{v}. An improper 𝘨\mathsfit{g} is the rotoreflection 𝘚𝐯(ω)=σ𝐯𝘙𝐯(ω)\mathsfit{S_{\mathbf{v}}}(\omega) = \mathsfit{\sigma_{\mathbf{v}}} \mathsfit{R_{\mathbf{v}}}(\omega), where σ𝐯\mathsfit{\sigma_{\mathbf{v}}} is the reflection in the plane normal to 𝐯\mathbf{v}, so ω=0\omega = 0 is the mirror with normal 𝐯\mathbf{v} and ω=π\omega = \pi the inversion. The angle–axis form of 𝘨\mathsfit{g} is (ω,𝐯)(\omega, \mathbf{v}).

  7. pole: the axis 𝐯\mathbf{v}, in these forms:

    1. {"kind": "axis", "cartesian": [x, y, z]} for a rotation or rotoreflection.
    2. {"kind": "plane_normal", "cartesian": [x, y, z]} for a mirror, with the normal of its plane.
    3. {"kind": "none"} for 𝘌\mathsfit{E} and 𝘪\mathsfit{i}.

    Its cartesian is an exact, unnormalized direction: only its direction and sign are data. It lies in the positive hemisphere (z>0z > 0, or z=0z = 0 and x>0x > 0, or z=x=0z = x = 0 and y>0y > 0), except that an axis along a cube body diagonal (∣x∣=∣y∣=∣z∣\lvert x\rvert = \lvert y\rvert = \lvert z\rvert) takes the direction with xyz>0xyz > 0. Thus the rotation by 3/43/4 turn about zz is angle [3, 4] about the pole (0,0,1)(0, 0, 1).

  8. symbol: the symbol of (det, angle) by section 5.1. The elements 𝘌\mathsfit{E}, 𝘪\mathsfit{i} and the mirrors take the symbol of their class ("sigma_h", "sigma_v", "sigma_d", "sigma").
  9. class_id, class_label: the id and label of the element's class.
  10. euler: {"alpha", "beta", "gamma"}, each {"turn": [p, q]}, the Euler angles in turns of the rotation part 𝘙=(det⁡𝘨) 𝘨\mathsfit{R} = (\det \mathsfit{g})\,\mathsfit{g}, with P(𝘙)=(det⁡𝘨) P(𝘨)P(\mathsfit{R}) = (\det \mathsfit{g})\, P(\mathsfit{g}). This rotation is the product 𝘙=𝘙𝘻(α) 𝘙𝘺(β) 𝘙𝘻(γ)\mathsfit{R} = \mathsfit{R_{z}}(\alpha)\, \mathsfit{R_{y}}(\beta)\, \mathsfit{R_{z}}(\gamma) of section 4.1: the rotation by α\alpha about zz, then by β\beta about yy, then by γ\gamma about zz (fixed axes). The ranges are α,γ∈(−1/2,1/2]\alpha, \gamma \in (-1/2, 1/2], β∈[0,1/2]\beta \in [0, 1/2]. At β=0\beta = 0 only α+γ\alpha + \gamma is fixed, and at β=1/2\beta = 1/2 only γ−α\gamma - \alpha. In both cases α=0\alpha = 0. An angle that is not a rational turn (α\alpha and γ\gamma of some elements of 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}) is the structured null {"reason": "not-a-rational-turn", "value": null}, and β\beta is always a rational turn.

  11. operation: the name of the element as an operation, with these fields:

    1. root: the class label without its size prefix ("C_3^2" for 𝟦𝘊𝟥𝟤\mathsfit{4C_{3}^{2}}, "C_2'" for 𝟤𝘊𝟤′\mathsfit{2C_{2}'}).
    2. sense: "+" for 0<ω<π0 < \omega < \pi, "-" for π<ω<2π\pi < \omega < 2\pi, null for ω=0\omega = 0 and ω=π\omega = \pi.
    3. axis_count: the number of distinct poles among the members of the class (0 for 𝘌\mathsfit{E} and 𝘪\mathsfit{i}).
    4. axis_index: the number of the element's pole among these poles, 1, 2, … in element order, null for 𝘌\mathsfit{E} and 𝘪\mathsfit{i}.
    5. name: the root with (axis_index) after its base when axis_count >1> 1, and its exponent followed by the sense as the superscript: "C_4^+", "C_3(1)^2-" (pole 1 of the class 𝟦𝘊𝟥𝟤\mathsfit{4C_{3}^{2}}, turn 2/32/3), "C_2'(2)", "sigma_v(1)", "E". The pair (class_id, name) identifies an element within its group.
  12. items[]: grouped by class, in the class order of section 5, the identity first. Within a class of an axial group, the operations about zz are ordered by increasing angle, and binary rotations about horizontal axes and vertical mirrors by increasing azimuth. This azimuth, in [0,1/2)[0, 1/2), is that of the axis or the horizontal direction in the mirror plane. Within a class of an exceptional group, the elements with the same pole are adjacent, by increasing angle.

4.3 Functions that move with the points ¶

Many works write a point as a column and let a function move with the points [BC72, AH11]. The elements, poles and angles of this section are the same in these works, which have these conventions:

  1. the matrix P(𝘨)⊤P(\mathsfit{g})^{\top} on a column, and the product that applies its right factor first. Their product of 𝘨\mathsfit{g} and 𝘩\mathsfit{h}, with 𝘩\mathsfit{h} first, is the element multiplication_table[h][g].
  2. the action (𝘨f)(𝐫)=f(𝐫 P(𝘨−1))(\mathsfit{g} f)(\mathbf{r}) = f(\mathbf{r}\, P(\mathsfit{g}^{-1})). Their operator of 𝘨\mathsfit{g} is the operator of 𝘨−1\mathsfit{g}^{-1} of section 4.1, so a rotation 𝘙\mathsfit{R} acts on the kets by U(𝘙)U(\mathsfit{R}) of section 9 and 𝘙𝘻(ϕ)∣ mJ⟩=e−imϕ∣ mJ⟩\mathsfit{R_{z}}(\phi) \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle = \mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}m\phi} \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle.
  3. the matrix DΓ(𝘨)†D^\Gamma(\mathsfit{g})^\dagger of an irrep and the character χΓ(𝘨)∗\chi_\Gamma(\mathsfit{g})^*, for the same partner kets and the same partner law (sections 6 and 9).
  4. the Euler angles (γ,β,α)(\gamma, \beta, \alpha) of euler, in this order.

The kets of a block, the coupling coefficients, the invariants and the levels are the same in both, and an irrep keeps its name and its kets. Thus the two irreps of a complex pair exchange their characters, and with them their symbols of section 6.5. The mm of a ket is the same in both, so the residues of allowed_m (section 14) distinguish the two irreps of a pair whose kets have unequal residues.

In other words, with P(𝘨)P(\mathsfit{g}) read on a column and each product read from the right, every stored matrix, character and table entry holds in these works for the inverses of its elements. GAP, SymPy and Wolfram multiply permutations as this catalogue does (section 12.5), and GAP lets a matrix act on a row.
