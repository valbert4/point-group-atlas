18 Files ¶

This catalogue's files are in the repository's folder rotation/, and the paths below, like those the records hold, are relative to this folder. The repository root holds the references, REFERENCES.pdf (typeset from REFERENCES.md), and index.json. This file gives the size and SHA-256 digest of each file of the records, of each catalogue's conventions, lattice and record schemas, and of the README, the references and the license. For each record it also lists the records that the record names.

JSON is written as json.dumps(obj, indent=2, sort_keys=True, ensure_ascii=True) plus one newline: keys sorted, two-space indent, ASCII (the ∞ of schoenflies_unicode is the escape \u221e), LF line endings. A .json.gz file is the gzip stream of those bytes, with no file name and modification time 0.

The GAP, Wolfram and SymPy files hold the record as one literal bound to o3t, after two comment lines. The first names the record, and the second gives the action of its matrices and the order of its products (section 4.3). In the sqrt row, a=∣p∣a = \lvert p\rvert and ss is the sign of pp. Wolfram and SymPy write - in front when s=−1s = -1, and Wolfram writes Sqrt[a] when q=1q = 1. The SymPy file of a record holding a sqrt value also imports sqrt.

A .tex file typesets one record and its subgroups as in section 0.2, after a table of contents. Its numbered parts are The group, Representations, Subgroups, for a finite group Permutation representations (sections 12.5 and 12.6), Angular momentum, Tensors and, for a group with a level, Vibrational and electronic levels (section 19). Section p.s is the s-th section of part p, with the subsections p.s.t and the displays (p.s.n). A continuous group, which has no Permutation representations, numbers its later parts on.

For a finite group, part 1 has the sections 1.1 Names and structure, 1.2 Symmetry operations, 1.3 Multiplication table, 1.4 Conjugacy classes, 1.5 Fundamental domain, 1.6 Orbifold. Part 2 has 2.1 Irreducible representations, 2.2 Character table, 2.3 Representation matrices, 2.4 Direct products of irreps, 2.5 Clebsch–Gordan coefficients. Part 3 has 3.1 Subgroup lattice, 3.2 Descent of symmetry. Part 4 has 4.1 Figures, 4.2 Decompositions, 4.3 Cycle types. Part 5 has 5.1 Subduction from O(3), 5.2 Multiplicities and allowed m, 5.3 Symmetry-adapted bases. Part 6 has 6.1 s, p, d, f functions, 6.2 Tensor spaces, 6.3 Isotypic components, 6.4 Invariant polynomials. Part 7 has 7.1 Subduction from U(N), 7.2 Vibrational overtone levels, 7.3 Overtone states, 7.4 Electronic terms, 7.5 Term states. A section that some groups lack comes last in its part (a group of prime order lacks Descent of symmetry, and 𝖢𝟣\mathsf{C_{1}} lacks Cycle types). The levels, which an abelian group lacks, are the last part. Thus a section has one number in every finite group's document.

A PDF is its .tex compiled by pdflatex, and CONVENTIONS.pdf is this document typeset in the same style. A document's PDF holds numbered bookmarks and, in its metadata, the group's name, the author and the record's schema. Each section number of this document that the PDF cites links to that section of CONVENTIONS.pdf.

A group symbol in the table of the section Names and structure, in a table of subgroups or in a subduction table links to its record's document when there is one. In a table of subgroups, the other names of a subgroup's 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}-conjugacy class, the schoenflies and alternative_names of its record (section 2.2), follow its symbol after ≅\cong (𝖢𝟤𝗏≅𝖣𝟣𝗁\mathsf{C_{2v}} \cong \mathsf{D_{1h}}). The list of irreps (of irrep families, for an axial continuous group) boxes the determinant character, left out of the table of the section Names and structure. For 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}, whose determinant character is an instance of a family, the document names it in a sentence. In that table the row orbifold of a finite group cites the section Orbifold.

The part The group ends with the sections Fundamental domain and Orbifold (section 17). For a finite group the section Fundamental domain sets the cell's facets in a table, one row for each pair of opposite facets. It sets tan⁡(ω𝘨/4)\tan(\omega_{\mathsfit{g}}/4) as its value when that is rational or a real quadratic number, and as the tangent otherwise. The vertices are set in a table, except that those of a prism with N≥3N \ge 3 are set by the formula of section 17.2. The record holds every vertex. For every group the section also sets the numbers of the harmonics of section 17.5 for J≤10J \le 10 in a table, one row for each parity. Two parities with the same numbers share a row, and a parity without harmonics has no row.

The section Orbifold of a finite group sets the strata of the orbifold and the vertices and sides of its region in two tables, and the presentation in a display. For a continuous group it states the orbit space of section 17.6 in sentences.

For a finite group, the character table is split into blocks of columns fitting the line. The fusion rules of a group whose every product is one irrep are a grid of the products Γ1⊗Γ2\Gamma_1 \otimes \Gamma_2, Γ1≤Γ2\Gamma_1 \le \Gamma_2 in irrep order. A Clebsch–Gordan block whose two factors are one-dimensional is not set: it is ∣Γ⟩=∣Γ1⟩∣Γ2⟩\lvert\Gamma\rangle = \lvert\Gamma_1\rangle\lvert\Gamma_2\rangle with 𝒩=1\mathcal{N} = 1 (section 10.3).

For a finite group the document sets its subgroup_lattice (section 15.1) as a table and as a Hasse diagram. The table has the classes of 𝖦\mathsf{G} that each representative meets and those of its normalizer and its centralizer in 𝖦\mathsf{G}. In the diagram a gray letter (a, b, … in node order) distinguishes the classes of subgroups with the same symbol, which are not conjugate in 𝖦\mathsf{G}. The section Descent of symmetry sets the tables of section 12.2 with the labels of the subgroups' records. These tables have one column for each class of subgroups other than the trivial group and 𝖦\mathsf{G}, labeled with the same letters. A class of subgroups with alternative settings has one column for each setting (1 the stored map). A row images gives, under each setting, the generators whose images differ between the settings, each mapped to the class of 𝖦\mathsf{G} that contains its image.

For a finite group, the part Permutation representations (section 12.5) sets these items for each class of subgroups:

  1. the figure in space that the class stabilizes, with its copies and the uniform_shapes of the orbits whose shape the table leaves out or gives as a rectangle, a prism or a cuboid (Figures).
  2. the kernel of the permutation representation on the copies and its decomposition into irreps (Decompositions).
  3. the cycle types of the classes of elements on the copies (Cycle types).

The copies' points and their images under the elements are in the record only (sections 12.5 and 12.6).

In the part Angular momentum, for a group of type I, whose subduction from 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} is the same at both parities, one table gives both. In a group that contains 𝘪\mathsfit{i}, a table has only the irreps of its parity, the others having multiplicity 0 for every JJ. A table of multiplicities too wide for the line is split into blocks of columns of about equal size. A table of bases stops before the first JJ at which a vector fills more than four lines. Thus the bases of 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}} are set for J≤3J \le 3, and those of every other group for all the J≤10J \le 10 of its record. The records of 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}} hold theirs for J≤10J \le 10, and the files of section 14.2 up to J=60J = 60.

Continuous groups. A continuous group's document sets its class space (section 16) with its Haar measure. Its part Representations sets the irrep families and the matrices of their irreps on each component, as functions of the component's angle. These matrices form a table for each dimension, like the tables of section 9 of a finite group. A family of dimension 2 is set in the real and in the rotation-diagonal basis, and the families of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} on the elements about zz, then on a general element. The characters' coefficients are in the record only. The documents of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} set the Wigner dJd^J for J≤3J \le 3 and the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients of D(J1)⊗D(J2)D^{(J_1)} \otimes D^{(J_2)} for J1≤J2≤3J_1 \le J_2 \le 3, and the records hold them for J≤10J \le 10.

The part Subgroups of a continuous group lists its closed subgroups up to conjugacy in 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} from data/point_groups.json (section 15.2). These subgroups are the continuous groups below it, for every N≥1N \ge 1 the members of axial families and, below 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}, the exceptional groups. A group named singly, by its own symbol, links to its document. For every continuous group a table like a finite group's lists one subgroup up to conjugacy in 𝖦\mathsf{G} in each row, with its normalizer and centralizer in 𝖦\mathsf{G} (section 16.1). For 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} this table follows a table of the documents of the members of its families. Its rows are the continuous subgroups, then, for each family, its members of small NN and one row for all larger NN, and last, for 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}, the exceptional groups. That row's members up to N=12N = 12 all have its entries, functions of NN. A member whose 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}-conjugacy class has other names has a row of its own. For the axial continuous groups, the section Descent of symmetry sets the restriction to the continuous subgroups and, for each family of finite subgroups, the rules of section 16.1 and tables for N≤12N \le 12 and n≤6n \le 6. The settings are as in the finite documents, with an image as an element at its angle. For 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} the section sets the rules of subduction_rules (section 16.1), each with the generating functions of its multiplicities, and no tables of members. The documents of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} set their subduction from 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} (section 11) as its rule, without the tables of subduction_o3.instances.

The volume (python3 -B -m generator.build volume, the README) opens with a pictorial contents: the conventions, the references and the index of notation as lines, and the 98 groups family by family in rows of eight cells. Each cell is a group's figure over a line with its symbol and its first sheet's number, and the line links to the document.

A group's figure is a solid with exactly the group's symmetry in the frame of its record:

  1. for most groups, the convex hull of the orbits (section 12.5) of one or two points with small integer coordinates.
  2. for 𝖢N𝗁\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}h}} with N≥3N \ge 3, a slab, the prism of a 2N2N-gon whose vertices alternate between an outer point and an inner one at half the radius, turned by a quarter of a step.
  3. for 𝖲𝟤N\mathsf{S_{2\mathnormal{N}}} with N≥2N \ge 2, two such slabs, the lower turned by half a step, on an axle between them.
  4. for 𝖢𝟣\mathsf{C_{1}}, an irregular tetrahedron with an obtuse face.

The two groups of each of the four 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}-conjugacy classes with two records share one shape: 𝖢𝟣𝗁\mathsf{C_{1h}} and 𝖢𝟣𝗏\mathsf{C_{1v}}, 𝖢𝟤\mathsf{C_{2}} and 𝖣𝟣\mathsf{D_{1}}, 𝖢𝟤𝗏\mathsf{C_{2v}} and 𝖣𝟣𝗁\mathsf{D_{1h}}, 𝖢𝟤𝗁\mathsf{C_{2h}} and 𝖣𝟣𝖽\mathsf{D_{1d}}. The second group's shape is turned by the rotation carrying the first group's frame into its own. The seven exceptional figures are scaled to one footprint: the outline of each in its picture has the same area. The figure is a cone for 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}}, a cylinder for 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}} and a ball for 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}. The groups 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}}, 𝖢∞𝗁\mathsf{C_{\infty h}}, 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}} and 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} have no figure: no stationary solid has exactly their symmetry. When the volume is built, the symmetry group of every finite figure's vertices is computed and must be the record's group, with the same elements. A figure is drawn see-through, hidden edges dashed and hidden vertices hollow. Its viewpoint is 22° above the xyxy-plane (38° for a slab or a spindle), at the azimuth near 40° where the vertices stay clear of one another and of the edges in the picture.

18.1 Pointers ¶

A pointer is the string "<catalogue>/<name>#<JSON pointer>", with a JSON pointer (RFC 6901) after #. It names the value at that JSON pointer in the record <name> of the catalogue in the folder <catalogue>. An empty JSON pointer names the whole record ("rotation/C3v#"), and the name point_groups stands for the file rotation/data/point_groups.json (section 15).
