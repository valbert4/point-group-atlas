The files of the catalogue are in the folder rotation/ of the repository, and the paths below, like those that the records hold, are relative to it. The repository root holds the references, REFERENCES.pdf (typeset from REFERENCES.md), and index.json, which lists every record with the files that hold it or that it names, their sizes and SHA-256 digests.

Every JSON document under data/ carries its format in schema: "point-group-atlas/group/1", "point-group-atlas/group-continuous/1", "point-group-atlas/point-group-lattice/1", "point-group-atlas/symmetry-adapted-basis/1", "point-group-atlas/level-states/1", "point-group-atlas/shell-states/1", "point-group-atlas/pdf-numbers/1".

JSON is written as json.dumps(obj, indent=2, sort_keys=True, ensure_ascii=True) plus one newline: keys sorted, two-space indent, ASCII (the ∞ of schoenflies_unicode is the escape \u221e), LF line ends. A .json.gz file is the gzip stream of those bytes, with no file name and modification time 0.

In the sqrt row a=∣p∣a = \lvert p\rvert and ss is the sign of pp; Wolfram and SymPy write - in front when s=−1s = -1, and Wolfram writes Sqrt[a] when q=1q = 1. The SymPy file of a record that holds a sqrt value also imports sqrt.

A .tex file typesets one record and its subgroups, after a table of contents, in numbered parts, as in section 0.2: The group, Representations, Subgroups, for a finite group Permutation representations (sections 12.5 and 12.6), Angular momentum, Tensors and, for a group with a level, Vibrational and electronic levels (section 19). Section p.s is the s-th section of part p, its subsections are p.s.t and its displays (p.s.n); a continuous group, which has no Permutation representations, numbers its later parts on. A finite group has the sections 1.1 Names and structure, 1.2 Symmetry operations, 1.3 Multiplication table, 1.4 Conjugacy classes, 1.5 Fundamental domain; 2.1 Irreducible representations, 2.2 Character table, 2.3 Representation matrices, 2.4 Direct products of irreps, 2.5 Clebsch–Gordan coefficients; 3.1 Subgroup lattice, 3.2 Descent of symmetry; 4.1 Figures, 4.2 Decompositions, 4.3 Cycle types; 5.1 Subduction from O(3), 5.2 Multiplicities and allowed m, 5.3 Symmetry-adapted bases; 6.1 s, p, d, f functions, 6.2 Tensor spaces, 6.3 Isotypic components, 6.4 Invariant polynomials; 7.1 Subduction from U(N), 7.2 Vibrational overtone levels, 7.3 Overtone states, 7.4 Electronic terms, 7.5 Term states. A section that some groups lack comes last in its part (Descent of symmetry, which a group of prime order lacks, and Cycle types, which 𝖢𝟣\mathsf{C_{1}} lacks), and the levels, which an abelian group lacks, are the last part, so that a section has one number in every document of a finite group. Its PDF holds the group's name, the author and the record's schema in its metadata, numbered bookmarks, and links from the section numbers of this document that it cites to those sections of CONVENTIONS.pdf. A group symbol in the table of the section Names and structure, in a table of subgroups or in a subduction table links to the document of its record when there is one; in a table of subgroups the other names of a subgroup's 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} class, the schoenflies and alternative_names of its record (section 2.1), follow its symbol after ≅\cong (𝖢𝟤𝗏≅𝖣𝟣𝗁\mathsf{C_{2v}} \cong \mathsf{D_{1h}}). The list of irreps (of irrep families, for an axial continuous group) boxes the determinant character, which that table leaves out; 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}, whose determinant character is an instance of a family, name it in a sentence.

The part The group ends with the section Fundamental domain (section 17). For a finite group it sets the facets of the cell in a table, one row per pair of opposite facets, tan⁡(ω𝘨/4)\tan(\omega_{\mathsfit{g}}/4) as its value when that is rational or a real quadratic number and as the tangent otherwise, and the vertices in a table, those of a prism with N≥3N \ge 3 by the formula of section 17.2; the record holds every vertex.

For a finite group the part Permutation representations (section 12.5) holds, per class of subgroups, the figure in space that the class stabilizes, with its copies and the uniform_shapes of the orbits whose shape the table leaves out or gives as a rectangle, a prism or a cuboid (Figures); the kernel of the permutation representation on the copies and its decomposition into irreps (Decompositions); and the cycle types of the classes of elements on the copies (Cycle types). The points of the copies and their images under the elements are in the record only (sections 12.5 and 12.6).

For a finite group the document holds its subgroup_lattice (section 15.1) as a table, with the classes of 𝖦\mathsf{G} that each representative meets and the classes of its normalizer and its centralizer in 𝖦\mathsf{G}, and as a Hasse diagram, where a gray letter (a, b, … in node order) tells apart the classes of subgroups with the same symbol, which are not conjugate in 𝖦\mathsf{G}. The section Descent of symmetry holds the tables of section 12.2, one column per class of subgroups other than the trivial group and 𝖦\mathsf{G}, labeled with the same letters, with the labels of the subgroups' records; under a class with alternative settings there is one column per setting (1 the stored map), and a row images gives under each setting the generators whose images differ between the settings, each mapped to the class of 𝖦\mathsf{G} that holds its image. The character table is split into blocks of columns that fit the line. The fusion rules of a group whose every product is one irrep are a grid of the products Γ1⊗Γ2\Gamma_1 \otimes \Gamma_2, Γ1≤Γ2\Gamma_1 \le \Gamma_2 in irrep order. A Clebsch–Gordan block whose two factors are one-dimensional is not set: it is ∣Γ⟩=∣Γ1⟩∣Γ2⟩\lvert\Gamma\rangle = \lvert\Gamma_1\rangle\lvert\Gamma_2\rangle with 𝒩=1\mathcal{N} = 1 (section 10.3). In a group of rotations, whose subduction from 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} is the same at both parities, one table holds both; in a group that holds 𝘪\mathsfit{i} a table has only the irreps of its parity, the others having multiplicity 0 for every JJ. A table of multiplicities too wide for the line is split into blocks of columns of about equal size. A table of bases stops before the first JJ at which a vector fills more than four lines: the bases of 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}} are set for J≤3J \le 3, those of every other group for all the J≤10J \le 10 of its record; the records of 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}} hold theirs for J≤10J \le 10 and the files of section 14.2 up to J=60J = 60.

For a continuous group the document holds its class space (section 16) with its Haar measure and, in the part Representations, its irrep families and the matrices of their irreps on each component as functions of the component's angle, a table per dimension like the tables of section 9 of a finite group: a family of dimension 2 in the real and in the rotation-diagonal basis, and those of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} on the elements about zz, then the matrices of a general element; the characters' coefficients are in the record only. 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} set the Wigner dJd^J for J≤3J \le 3 and the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients of D(J1)⊗D(J2)D^{(J_1)} \otimes D^{(J_2)} for J1≤J2≤3J_1 \le J_2 \le 3; the records hold them for J≤10J \le 10. The part Subgroups lists its closed subgroups up to conjugacy in 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} from data/point_groups.json (section 15.2): the continuous groups below it, for every N≥1N \ge 1 the members of axial families and, below 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}, the exceptional groups; a group named on its own, not as a member of a family, links to its document. For the five axial continuous groups and 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} a table like that of a finite group lists one subgroup up to conjugacy in 𝖦\mathsf{G} per row, with its normalizer and its centralizer in 𝖦\mathsf{G} (section 16.1): the continuous subgroups, then per family its members of small NN and one row for every larger NN, whose entries, functions of NN, the members up to N=12N = 12 all have; a member whose class has other names has a row of its own. For these five the section Descent of symmetry holds the restriction to the continuous subgroups and, per family of finite subgroups, the rules of section 16.1 and tables for N≤12N \le 12 and n≤6n \le 6, the settings as in the finite documents with an image as an element at its angle; for 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} it holds the rules of subduction_rules (section 16.1), each with the generating functions of its multiplicities, and no tables of members. 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} give their subduction from 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} (section 11) as its rule, without the tables of subduction_o3.instances.

A PDF is its .tex compiled by pdflatex. CONVENTIONS.pdf is this document typeset in the same style.

The volume (python3 -B -m generator.build volume, the README) opens with a pictorial contents: the conventions, the references and the index of notation as lines, and the 98 groups family by family in rows of eight cells, each cell a group's figure over a line with its symbol and the number of its first sheet, the line a link to the document. The figure of a group is a solid with exactly the symmetry of the group in the frame of its record: for most groups the convex hull of the orbits (section 12.5) of one or two points with small integer coordinates; for 𝖢N𝗁\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}h}} with N≥3N \ge 3 a slab, the prism of a 2N2N-gon whose vertices alternate between an outer point and an inner one at half the radius, turned by a quarter of a step; for 𝖲𝟤N\mathsf{S_{2\mathnormal{N}}} with N≥2N \ge 2 two such slabs, the lower turned by half a step, on an axle between them; for 𝖢𝟣\mathsf{C_{1}} an irregular tetrahedron with an obtuse face. The two groups of each of the four classes with two records (𝖢𝟣𝗁\mathsf{C_{1h}} and 𝖢𝟣𝗏\mathsf{C_{1v}}, 𝖢𝟤\mathsf{C_{2}} and 𝖣𝟣\mathsf{D_{1}}, 𝖢𝟤𝗏\mathsf{C_{2v}} and 𝖣𝟣𝗁\mathsf{D_{1h}}, 𝖢𝟤𝗁\mathsf{C_{2h}} and 𝖣𝟣𝖽\mathsf{D_{1d}}) share one shape, the second's turned by the rotation that carries the first's frame into its own. The seven exceptional figures are scaled to one footprint: the outline of each in its picture has the same area. 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}} is a cone, 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}} a cylinder and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} a ball; 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}}, 𝖢∞𝗁\mathsf{C_{\infty h}}, 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}} and 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} have no figure, no stationary solid having exactly their symmetry. When the volume is built, the symmetry group of the vertices of every finite figure is computed and must be the record's group, element by element. A figure is drawn see-through, from 22° above the xyxy-plane (38° for a slab or a spindle) and from the azimuth near 40° at which its vertices stay clear of one another and of the edges in the picture, hidden edges dashed and hidden vertices hollow.
