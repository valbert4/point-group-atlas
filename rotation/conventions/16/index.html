16 Continuous groups ¶

A continuous record has domain = "continuous" and character_functions in place of elements and character_table. The group 𝖢∞𝗁\mathsf{C_{\infty h}} is also written 𝖲∞\mathsf{S_{\infty}}, and 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} are 𝖪\mathsf{K} and 𝖪𝗁\mathsf{K_{h}} (group.alternative_names, section 2.2).

Each component has these fields:

  1. id, element and label.
  2. display (LaTeX): on the arcs, 𝘊(ϕ)\mathsfit{C}(\phi) and 𝘪 𝘊(ϕ)\mathsfit{i}\,\mathsfit{C}(\phi) in 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}, and 𝘊∞(ϕ)\mathsfit{C_\infty}(\phi) and 𝘪 𝘊∞(ϕ)\mathsfit{i}\,\mathsfit{C_\infty}(\phi) in the other five groups.
  3. matrix: the matrix of the table above as rows of formulas in xx such as "cos 2x", and in 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} that of the element about zz.
  4. coset: "proper" or "improper".

  5. kind: one of these values:

    1. "arc": a continuum of classes with a coordinate in turns (coordinate: symbol "t", unit "turn", phi "phi = 2*pi*t", so ϕ\phi is 2π2\pi times the coordinate, which runs from t0 to t1). Two of its points, of measure zero, are also listed as distinguished_points (id, label, display, turn, class_size): E (ϕ=0\phi = 0) and C2 (ϕ=π\phi = \pi) on Cphi, i (ϕ=0\phi = 0) and sh (ϕ=π\phi = \pi) on iCphi.
    2. "atom": a single class with infinitely many elements (sv, c2p).

  6. class_size: the number of elements in a class of an arc (1, or 2 where 𝘊(ϕ)\mathsfit{C}(\phi) and 𝘊(−ϕ)\mathsfit{C}(-\phi) are conjugate), and null continuous-group on an atom. A distinguished point's class_size is the number of elements in its class.

    In 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} every class of an arc is a continuum, and so are the classes of C2 and sh (all the rotations by π\pi, all the mirrors). Their class_size is null continuous-group, and that of E and i is 1.

  7. measure: d𝘩=density dϕ/(2π)\mathrm{d}\mathsfit{h} = \text{density}\,\mathrm{d}\phi/(2\pi) on an arc (kind "absolutely_continuous", density_dt), and a mass on an atom (kind "atom", mass). The density_dt is a rational, or on the arcs of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} the Weyl density, a trigonometric polynomial in ϕ\phi (below).

    The Weyl density is 2−2cos⁡ϕ=4sin⁡2(ϕ/2)2 - 2\cos\phi = 4\sin^2(\phi/2) on the arc of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} (mass 1) and 1−cos⁡ϕ1 - \cos\phi on each arc of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} (mass 1/2). Thus the integral of a class function over the group is its integral over the arcs with this density.

  8. haar_mass: the component's total mass.
  9. square: where the squares of the elements lie, {"component", "t_multiplier"} (the square of the element at ϕ\phi is the element of component at t_multiplier ⋅ϕ\cdot \phi) or {"component", "turn"} (every square is the element at turn).

Characters are trigonometric polynomials {"f": "trig", "var": "phi", "terms": [...]} in the component's angle ϕ\phi: the sum over the terms of cos⋅cos⁡(harmonic⋅ϕ)+sin⋅sin⁡(harmonic⋅ϕ)\texttt{cos} \cdot \cos(\texttt{harmonic} \cdot \phi) + \texttt{sin} \cdot \sin(\texttt{harmonic} \cdot \phi), where cos and sin are exact values and harmonic =coefficient⋅n+constant= \texttt{coefficient} \cdot n + \texttt{constant}. In a family index_symbol names nn, and coefficient is 0 in an instance. A polynomial without terms is the character 0. A term whose harmonic has sum (symbol, from, to) is a range, the sum of cos⋅cos⁡(harmonic⋅ϕ)+sin⋅sin⁡(harmonic⋅ϕ)\texttt{cos} \cdot \cos(\texttt{harmonic} \cdot \phi) + \texttt{sin} \cdot \sin(\texttt{harmonic} \cdot \phi) over k=k = from, …, to, empty when to << from. Thus the character of D(J)D^{(J)} on Cphi is 1+2∑k=1Jcos⁡(kϕ)=sin⁡((2J+1)ϕ/2)/sin⁡(ϕ/2)1 + 2\sum_{k=1}^{J} \cos(k\phi) = \sin((2J+1)\phi/2)/\sin(\phi/2), the constant term 1 and the range of 2cos⁡(kϕ)2\cos(k\phi) over k=1,2,…,Jk = 1, 2, \dots, J. 16.1 Subgroups and subduction ¶

The records of 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}}, 𝖢∞𝗁\mathsf{C_{\infty h}}, 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}}, 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}} and 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}} hold subduction_subgroups: the restriction of their irreps to their closed subgroups, up to conjugacy in 𝖦\mathsf{G}, with its intertwiners. The records of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} hold none: the restriction of D(J,p)D^{(J,p)} to a subgroup, JJ by JJ, is in the subgroup's record (subduction_o3, section 11). Its intertwiners are the subgroup's symmetry-adapted bases (section 14). The record of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} holds the rules of its restrictions for every JJ (subduction_rules, below). As 𝘪\mathsfit{i} commutes with every element, every map of section 12.1 onto a subgroup of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} or 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} is also a conjugation in 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)}. Thus each restriction of D(J,p)D^{(J,p)} has one setting.
