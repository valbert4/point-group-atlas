  1. An "arc" is a continuum of classes with a coordinate in turns (coordinate: symbol "t", unit "turn", phi "phi = 2*pi*t", so ϕ\phi is 2π2\pi times the coordinate, which runs from t0 to t1). Two of its points, of measure zero, are also listed as distinguished_points (id, label, display, turn, class_size): E (ϕ=0\phi = 0) and C2 (ϕ=π\phi = \pi) on Cphi, i (ϕ=0\phi = 0) and sh (ϕ=π\phi = \pi) on iCphi. class_size is the number of elements in a class of the arc (1, or 2 where 𝘊(ϕ)\mathsfit{C}(\phi) and 𝘊(−ϕ)\mathsfit{C}(-\phi) are conjugate), and that of a point the number of elements in its class. In 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} every class of an arc is a continuum, and so are the classes of C2 and sh (all the rotations by π\pi, all the reflections): their class_size is null continuous-group, and that of E and i is 1.
  2. An "atom" is a single class with infinitely many elements (sv, c2p); its class_size is null continuous-group.
  3. Haar measure (class_space.haar): normalized to total mass 1 (normalisation, total_mass [1, 1], symbol "dh"). On an arc d𝘩=density dϕ/(2π)\mathrm{d}\mathsfit{h} = \text{density}\,\mathrm{d}\phi/(2\pi) (measure: kind "absolutely_continuous", density_dt); an atom has a mass (measure: kind "atom", mass). haar_mass is the component's total mass. density_dt is a rational, or on the arcs of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} the Weyl density, a trigonometric polynomial in ϕ\phi (below): 2−2cos⁡ϕ=4sin⁡2(ϕ/2)2 - 2\cos\phi = 4\sin^2(\phi/2) on the arc of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} (mass 1), and 1−cos⁡ϕ1 - \cos\phi on each arc of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} (mass 1/2), so that the integral of a class function over the group is its integral over the arcs with this density.
  4. square: where the squares of the elements lie: {"component", "t_multiplier"} (the square of the element at ϕ\phi is the element of component at t_multiplier ⋅ϕ\cdot \phi) or {"component", "turn"} (every square is the element at turn).

16.1 Subgroups and subduction ¶

The records of 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}}, 𝖢∞𝗁\mathsf{C_{\infty h}}, 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}}, 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}} and 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}} hold subduction_subgroups: the restriction of their irreps to their closed subgroups, up to conjugacy in 𝖦\mathsf{G}, with its intertwiners. 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} have none: the restriction of D(J,p)D^{(J,p)} to a subgroup, JJ by JJ, is in the subgroup's record (subduction_o3, section 11), and its intertwiners are the subgroup's symmetry-adapted bases (section 14); 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} holds the rules of its restrictions for every JJ (subduction_rules, below). Every map of section 12.1 onto a subgroup is a conjugation in 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} as well, 𝘪\mathsfit{i} commuting with every element, so each restriction has one setting.
