17 Fundamental domains ¶

The block fundamental_domain gives a fundamental domain of the orientations of a molecule with symmetry 𝖦\mathsf{G}, and the volumes of 𝖦\mathsf{G}, of the domain and of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}.

17.1 Cosets, volumes and the cell ¶

A molecule with symmetry 𝖦\mathsf{G} placed by 𝘘∈𝖮(𝟥)\mathsfit{Q} \in \mathsf{O(3)} is the set of its atoms moved by P(𝘘)P(\mathsfit{Q}) (section 4.1). As P(𝘨𝘘)=P(𝘨) P(𝘘)P(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q}) = P(\mathsfit{g})\,P(\mathsfit{Q}), the placements 𝘘\mathsfit{Q} and 𝘨𝘘\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q}, 𝘨∈𝖦\mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{G}, give the same set: the orientations of the molecule are the cosets 𝖦𝘘={𝘨𝘘:𝘨∈𝖦}\mathsf{G}\mathsfit{Q} = \{\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q} : \mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{G}\}, the points of the space 𝖦\𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G}\backslash\mathsf{O(3)}; on column vectors a coset is P(𝘘)⊤P(𝘨)⊤P(\mathsfit{Q})^{\top}P(\mathsfit{g})^{\top}, 𝘨∈𝖦\mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{G}, a point of 𝖮(𝟥)/𝖦\mathsf{O(3)}/\mathsf{G}. A fundamental domain ℱ\mathcal{F} holds one element of every coset, more only on its boundary, which has volume 0.

Volumes are those of the invariant metric of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} in which the distance from 𝘌\mathsfit{E} to a rotation by ω≤π\omega \le \pi is ω\omega: vol⁡𝖲𝖮(𝟥)=8π2\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{SO(3)} = 8\pi^2 and vol⁡𝖮(𝟥)=16π2\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{O(3)} = 16\pi^2. A finite group has the counting measure, vol⁡𝖦=∣𝖦∣\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{G} = \lvert\mathsf{G}\rvert; a continuous group other than 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} is a union of circles of length 2π2\pi, one for each component of its class space (section 16), so that vol⁡𝖦=2π\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{G} = 2\pi for 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}}, 4π4\pi for 𝖢∞𝗁\mathsf{C_{\infty h}}, 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}} and 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}}, and 8π8\pi for 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}}. The space 𝖦\𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G}\backslash\mathsf{O(3)} has the metric in which two cosets are as far apart as their nearest elements, and vol⁡ℱ\operatorname{vol}\mathcal{F} is its volume (for a finite 𝖦\mathsf{G}, the volume of ℱ\mathcal{F} in 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}). Every coset is a copy of 𝖦\mathsf{G}:

(17.1)vol⁡𝖦⋅vol⁡ℱ=vol⁡𝖮(𝟥)=16π2.\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{G} \cdot \operatorname{vol}\mathcal{F} = \operatorname{vol}\mathsf{O(3)} = 16\pi^2.

The cell 𝒟⊂𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathcal{D} \subset \mathsf{SO(3)} holds one element of every coset of 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}. A coset 𝖦𝘘\mathsf{G}\mathsfit{Q} of a group of type I (section 2.2) lies in 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} or in 𝘪 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsfit{i}\,\mathsf{SO(3)}: ℱ=𝒟∪𝘪 𝒟\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{D} \cup \mathsfit{i}\,\mathcal{D}, and 𝖦\𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G}\backslash\mathsf{O(3)} is two copies of (𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥))\𝖲𝖮(𝟥)(\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)})\backslash\mathsf{SO(3)}. A group of type II or III holds an improper element 𝘩\mathsfit{h}, and a coset 𝖦𝘘\mathsf{G}\mathsfit{Q} meets 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)}, in 𝘘\mathsfit{Q} or in 𝘩𝘘\mathsfit{h}\mathsfit{Q}, in one coset of 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}: ℱ=𝒟\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{D}, one copy. The spaces (𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥))\𝖲𝖮(𝟥)(\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)})\backslash\mathsf{SO(3)} are, as in the table of quotient spaces of [ACP20], the lens space 𝖫(2N,1)\mathsf{L}(2N, 1) for 𝖢N\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}}} (𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} itself for N=1N = 1, and 𝖫(4,1)\mathsf{L}(4, 1) for the rotations by π\pi about xx of D1, D1h and D1d), a prism space for 𝖣N\mathsf{D_{\mathnormal{N}}}, N≥2N \ge 2, the octahedral space for 𝖳\mathsf{T}, the truncated-cube space for 𝖮\mathsf{O}, the Poincaré dodecahedral space for 𝖨\mathsf{I}, the sphere 𝖲2\mathsf{S}^{2} for 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}}, the projective plane ℝ𝖯2\mathbb{R}\mathsf{P}^{2} for 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}} (isomorphic to 𝖮(𝟤)\mathsf{O(2)}) and a point for 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)}.

17.2 Finite groups ¶

A rotation 𝘙𝐯(ω)\mathsfit{R_{\mathbf{v}}}(\omega) with 0≤ω≤π0 \le \omega \le \pi (section 4.2) has the Rodrigues vector 𝝆=tan⁡(ω/2) 𝐯^\boldsymbol{\rho} = \tan(\omega/2)\,\hat{\mathbf{v}}, with 𝐯^=𝐯/∥𝐯∥\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \mathbf{v}/\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert its unit axis. 𝝆\boldsymbol{\rho} runs over all of space, the rotations by π\pi at infinity, and the volume element of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} is 8 (1+∥𝝆∥2)−2 d3𝝆8\,(1 + \lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}\rVert^2)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}^3\boldsymbol{\rho}. The cell is the Voronoi cell of 𝘌\mathsfit{E} [Pos92], [ACP20, appendix B], the rotations nearer to 𝘌\mathsfit{E} than to any other element of 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}:

(17.2)𝒟={𝝆:∣𝝆⋅𝐯^𝘨∣≤tan⁡(ω𝘨/4) for every 𝘨∈𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥) with 0<ω𝘨≤π},\mathcal{D} = \bigl\{\boldsymbol{\rho} : \lvert\boldsymbol{\rho}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathsfit{g}}\rvert \le \tan(\omega_{\mathsfit{g}}/4) \text{ for every } \mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)} \text{ with } 0 < \omega_{\mathsfit{g}} \le \pi\bigr\},

with ω𝘨\omega_{\mathsfit{g}} and 𝐯^𝘨\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathsfit{g}} the angle and the unit axis of 𝘨\mathsfit{g}, an element whose angle is at most 1/21/2 turn (𝘨−1\mathsfit{g}^{-1} has the same pole and the angle 2π−ω𝘨2\pi - \omega_{\mathsfit{g}}). The plane 𝝆⋅𝐯^𝘨=tan⁡(ω𝘨/4)\boldsymbol{\rho}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathsfit{g}} = \tan(\omega_{\mathsfit{g}}/4), through the rotation by ω𝘨/2\omega_{\mathsfit{g}}/2 about 𝐯^𝘨\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathsfit{g}}, holds the rotations as near to 𝘨\mathsfit{g} as to 𝘌\mathsfit{E}, and its opposite those as near to 𝘨−1\mathsfit{g}^{-1}; of the elements about one axis only the one of the least angle bounds 𝒟\mathcal{D}, and the facets come in opposite pairs. vol⁡𝒟=8π2/∣𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)∣\operatorname{vol}\mathcal{D} = 8\pi^2/\lvert\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}\rvert and vol⁡ℱ=16π2/∣𝖦∣\operatorname{vol}\mathcal{F} = 16\pi^2/\lvert\mathsf{G}\rvert. The 91 finite records have 27 cells: all of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} for 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)=𝖢𝟣\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)} = \mathsf{C_{1}}; an unbounded slab between two opposite facets for 𝖢N\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}}}, N≥2N \ge 2, and for the rotations by π\pi about xx; for 𝖣N\mathsf{D_{\mathnormal{N}}} a prism over the regular 2N2N-gon (for N=2N = 2 the cube), with the 4N4N vertices

(17.3)(cos⁡(2u+1)π2Ncos⁡π2N, sin⁡(2u+1)π2Ncos⁡π2N, ±tan⁡π2N),u=0,1,…,2N−1;\Bigl(\frac{\cos\frac{(2u+1)\pi}{2N}}{\cos\frac{\pi}{2N}},\ \frac{\sin\frac{(2u+1)\pi}{2N}}{\cos\frac{\pi}{2N}},\ \pm\tan\frac{\pi}{2N}\Bigr), \qquad u = 0, 1, \dots, 2N - 1;

for 𝖳\mathsf{T} the regular octahedron of the vertices (±1,0,0)(\pm 1, 0, 0), (0,±1,0)(0, \pm 1, 0) and (0,0,±1)(0, 0, \pm 1), for 𝖮\mathsf{O} the truncated cube and for 𝖨\mathsf{I} the regular dodecahedron.

17.3 Continuous groups ¶

In the Euler angles of section 4.2 the volume element of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} is sin⁡β dα dβ dγ\sin\beta\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}\beta\,\mathrm{d}\gamma. The rotation by α\alpha about zz is applied first, so 𝘊∞(ϕ) 𝘘\mathsfit{C_{\infty}}(\phi)\,\mathsfit{Q} has the angles (α+ϕ,β,γ)(\alpha + \phi, \beta, \gamma): a coset of 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}} is a circle of α\alpha at fixed (β,γ)(\beta, \gamma), the polar and azimuthal angles of the axis (0,0,1) P(𝘘)(0, 0, 1)\,P(\mathsfit{Q}) of the molecule. The slice of the rotations with α=0\alpha = 0, β∈[0,π]\beta \in [0, \pi] and γ∈(−π,π]\gamma \in (-\pi, \pi] is its cell; in the metric of the cosets it is the unit sphere of the axes, of area element sin⁡β dβ dγ\sin\beta\,\mathrm{d}\beta\,\mathrm{d}\gamma and area 4π4\pi. 𝘊𝟤′(ψ)\mathsfit{C_2'}(\psi) carries the rotation of the angles (0,β,γ)(0, \beta, \gamma) to that of (−2ψ,π−β,γ+π)(-2\psi, \pi - \beta, \gamma + \pi), of the opposite axis, so the cell of 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}} is the half slice β∈[0,π/2]\beta \in [0, \pi/2], of area 2π2\pi, the projective plane of the axis lines. 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} is one coset of itself, and its cell is the point 𝘌\mathsfit{E}, of volume 1.

17.4 Fields ¶

  1. components: ["proper", "improper"] for a group of type I (ℱ=𝒟∪𝘪 𝒟\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{D} \cup \mathsfit{i}\,\mathcal{D}), else ["proper"], in the words of coset (section 16).
  2. cell: 𝒟\mathcal{D}, by its shape: "whole", "slab", "cube", "prism", "octahedron", "truncated cube" or "dodecahedron" for a finite group, with facets and vertices; "slice" or "point" (the cell {𝘌}\{\mathsfit{E}\}) for a continuous one. facets[] has one entry per pair of opposite facets, by element: element and inverse, the indices of 𝘨\mathsfit{g} and 𝘨−1\mathsfit{g}^{-1}; angle, the turn of 𝘨\mathsfit{g}; distance, tan⁡(ω𝘨/4)\tan(\omega_{\mathsfit{g}}/4); unit_axis, 𝐯^𝘨\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathsfit{g}}. vertices[] lists the vertices 𝝆\boldsymbol{\rho}, by decreasing third coordinate, then second, then first (none for "whole" and "slab"). A "slice" has alpha (its turn, 0), beta and gamma (t0 and t1, in turns) and volume_element, "sin(beta) dbeta dgamma".
  3. space: (𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥))\𝖲𝖮(𝟥)(\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)})\backslash\mathsf{SO(3)}, by its name ("lens space", "prism space", "octahedral space", "truncated-cube space", "Poincare dodecahedral space", "sphere", "projective plane" or "point"), and lens, [2∣𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)∣,1][2\lvert\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}\rvert, 1] for a lens space, else null not-defined-for-family.
  4. volumes: group, cell, domain and o3, each the volume of a set of dimension 0 (a number of elements) to 3, coefficient times π\pi to the power pi_power; domain is cell times the number of components.
