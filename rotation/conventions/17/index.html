17 Fundamental domains ¶

The field fundamental_domain gives a fundamental domain of the orientations of a molecule with symmetry 𝖦\mathsf{G}, and the volumes of 𝖦\mathsf{G}, of the domain and of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}.

17.1 Cosets, volumes and the cell ¶

A molecule with symmetry 𝖦\mathsf{G} placed by 𝘘∈𝖮(𝟥)\mathsfit{Q} \in \mathsf{O(3)} is the set of its atoms moved by P(𝘘)P(\mathsfit{Q}) (section 4.1). As P(𝘨𝘘)=P(𝘨) P(𝘘)P(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q}) = P(\mathsfit{g})\,P(\mathsfit{Q}), the placements 𝘘\mathsfit{Q} and 𝘨𝘘\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q}, 𝘨∈𝖦\mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{G}, give the same set. Thus the orientations of the molecule are the cosets 𝖦𝘘={𝘨𝘘:𝘨∈𝖦}\mathsf{G}\mathsfit{Q} = \{\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q} : \mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{G}\}, the points of the space 𝖦\𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G}\backslash\mathsf{O(3)}. On column vectors a coset is P(𝘘)⊤P(𝘨)⊤P(\mathsfit{Q})^{\top}P(\mathsfit{g})^{\top}, 𝘨∈𝖦\mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{G}, a point of 𝖮(𝟥)/𝖦\mathsf{O(3)}/\mathsf{G}. A fundamental domain ℱ\mathcal{F} contains one element of every coset, more only on its boundary, which has volume 0.

Volumes are those of the invariant metric of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} in which the distance from 𝘌\mathsfit{E} to a rotation by ω≤π\omega \le \pi is ω\omega: vol⁡𝖲𝖮(𝟥)=8π2\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{SO(3)} = 8\pi^2 and vol⁡𝖮(𝟥)=16π2\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{O(3)} = 16\pi^2. A finite group has the counting measure, vol⁡𝖦=∣𝖦∣\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{G} = \lvert\mathsf{G}\rvert. A continuous group other than 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} is a union of circles of length 2π2\pi, one for each component of its class space (section 16). Thus vol⁡𝖦=2π\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{G} = 2\pi for 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}}, 4π4\pi for 𝖢∞𝗁\mathsf{C_{\infty h}}, 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}} and 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}}, and 8π8\pi for 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}}. The space 𝖦\𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G}\backslash\mathsf{O(3)} has the metric in which two cosets are as far apart as their nearest elements, and vol⁡ℱ\operatorname{vol}\mathcal{F} is its volume (for a finite 𝖦\mathsf{G}, the volume of ℱ\mathcal{F} in 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}). Every coset is a copy of 𝖦\mathsf{G}:

(17.1)vol⁡𝖦⋅vol⁡ℱ=vol⁡𝖮(𝟥)=16π2.\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{G} \cdot \operatorname{vol}\mathcal{F} = \operatorname{vol}\mathsf{O(3)} = 16\pi^2.

The cell 𝒟⊂𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathcal{D} \subset \mathsf{SO(3)} contains one element of every coset of 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}. A coset 𝖦𝘘\mathsf{G}\mathsfit{Q} of a group of type I (section 2.2) lies in 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} or in 𝘪 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsfit{i}\,\mathsf{SO(3)}: ℱ=𝒟∪𝘪 𝒟\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{D} \cup \mathsfit{i}\,\mathcal{D}, and 𝖦\𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G}\backslash\mathsf{O(3)} is two copies of (𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥))\𝖲𝖮(𝟥)(\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)})\backslash\mathsf{SO(3)}. A group of type II or III contains an improper element 𝘩\mathsfit{h}, and a coset 𝖦𝘘\mathsf{G}\mathsfit{Q} meets 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} in one coset of 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)} through 𝘘\mathsfit{Q} or 𝘩𝘘\mathsfit{h}\mathsfit{Q}: ℱ=𝒟\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{D}, one copy. The spaces (𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥))\𝖲𝖮(𝟥)(\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)})\backslash\mathsf{SO(3)} are these, as in the table of quotient spaces of [ACP20]:

  1. the lens space 𝖫(2N,1)\mathsf{L}(2N, 1) for 𝖢N\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}}} (𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} itself for N=1N = 1, and 𝖫(4,1)\mathsf{L}(4, 1) for the rotations by π\pi about xx of D1, D1h and D1d).
  2. a prism space for 𝖣N\mathsf{D_{\mathnormal{N}}}, N≥2N \ge 2.
  3. the octahedral space for 𝖳\mathsf{T}.
  4. the truncated-cube space for 𝖮\mathsf{O}.
  5. the Poincaré dodecahedral space for 𝖨\mathsf{I}.
  6. the sphere 𝖲2\mathsf{S}^{2} for 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}}.
  7. the projective plane ℝ𝖯2\mathbb{R}\mathsf{P}^{2} for 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}} (isomorphic to 𝖮(𝟤)\mathsf{O(2)}).
  8. a point for 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)}.

17.2 Finite groups ¶

A rotation 𝘙𝐯(ω)\mathsfit{R_{\mathbf{v}}}(\omega) with 0≤ω≤π0 \le \omega \le \pi (section 4.2) has the Rodrigues vector 𝝆=tan⁡(ω/2) 𝐯^\boldsymbol{\rho} = \tan(\omega/2)\,\hat{\mathbf{v}}, where 𝐯^=𝐯/∥𝐯∥\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \mathbf{v}/\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert is its unit axis. The vector 𝝆\boldsymbol{\rho} runs over all of space, the rotations by π\pi at infinity, and the volume element of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} is 8 (1+∥𝝆∥2)−2 d3𝝆8\,(1 + \lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}\rVert^2)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}^3\boldsymbol{\rho}. The cell is the Voronoi cell of 𝘌\mathsfit{E} [Pos92], [ACP20, appendix B], the rotations nearer to 𝘌\mathsfit{E} than to any other element of 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}:

(17.2)𝒟={𝝆:∣𝝆⋅𝐯^𝘨∣≤tan⁡(ω𝘨/4) for every 𝘨∈𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥) with 0<ω𝘨≤π},\mathcal{D} = \bigl\{\boldsymbol{\rho} : \lvert\boldsymbol{\rho}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathsfit{g}}\rvert \le \tan(\omega_{\mathsfit{g}}/4) \text{ for every } \mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)} \text{ with } 0 < \omega_{\mathsfit{g}} \le \pi\bigr\},

where ω𝘨\omega_{\mathsfit{g}} is the angle and 𝐯^𝘨\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathsfit{g}} is the unit axis of 𝘨\mathsfit{g}, an element whose angle is at most 1/21/2 turn (𝘨−1\mathsfit{g}^{-1} has the same pole and the angle 2π−ω𝘨2\pi - \omega_{\mathsfit{g}}). The plane 𝝆⋅𝐯^𝘨=tan⁡(ω𝘨/4)\boldsymbol{\rho}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathsfit{g}} = \tan(\omega_{\mathsfit{g}}/4), through the rotation by ω𝘨/2\omega_{\mathsfit{g}}/2 about 𝐯^𝘨\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathsfit{g}}, contains the rotations as near to 𝘨\mathsfit{g} as to 𝘌\mathsfit{E}, and its opposite contains the rotations as near to 𝘨−1\mathsfit{g}^{-1}. Of the elements about one axis, only the element of the least angle bounds 𝒟\mathcal{D}, and the facets come in opposite pairs. The volumes are vol⁡𝒟=8π2/∣𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)∣\operatorname{vol}\mathcal{D} = 8\pi^2/\lvert\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}\rvert and vol⁡ℱ=16π2/∣𝖦∣\operatorname{vol}\mathcal{F} = 16\pi^2/\lvert\mathsf{G}\rvert. The 91 finite records have 27 cells. The cell is all of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} for 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)=𝖢𝟣\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)} = \mathsf{C_{1}}, and an unbounded slab between two opposite facets for 𝖢N\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}}}, N≥2N \ge 2, and for the rotations by π\pi about xx. It is a prism over the regular 2N2N-gon for 𝖣N\mathsf{D_{\mathnormal{N}}} (the cube for N=2N = 2), with the 4N4N vertices

(17.3)(cos⁡(2u+1)π2Ncos⁡π2N, sin⁡(2u+1)π2Ncos⁡π2N, ±tan⁡π2N),u=0,1,…,2N−1;\Bigl(\frac{\cos\frac{(2u+1)\pi}{2N}}{\cos\frac{\pi}{2N}},\ \frac{\sin\frac{(2u+1)\pi}{2N}}{\cos\frac{\pi}{2N}},\ \pm\tan\frac{\pi}{2N}\Bigr), \qquad u = 0, 1, \dots, 2N - 1;

the regular octahedron of the vertices (±1,0,0)(\pm 1, 0, 0), (0,±1,0)(0, \pm 1, 0) and (0,0,±1)(0, 0, \pm 1) for 𝖳\mathsf{T}, the truncated cube for 𝖮\mathsf{O} and the regular dodecahedron for 𝖨\mathsf{I}.

17.3 Continuous groups ¶

In the Euler angles of section 4.2 the volume element of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} is sin⁡β dα dβ dγ\sin\beta\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}\beta\,\mathrm{d}\gamma. The rotation by α\alpha about zz is applied first, so 𝘊∞(ϕ) 𝘘\mathsfit{C_{\infty}}(\phi)\,\mathsfit{Q} has the angles (α+ϕ,β,γ)(\alpha + \phi, \beta, \gamma). Thus a coset of 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}} is a circle of α\alpha at fixed (β,γ)(\beta, \gamma), the polar and azimuthal angles of the molecule's axis (0,0,1) P(𝘘)(0, 0, 1)\,P(\mathsfit{Q}). The slice of the rotations with α=0\alpha = 0, β∈[0,π]\beta \in [0, \pi] and γ∈(−π,π]\gamma \in (-\pi, \pi] is the cell of 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}}. In the metric of the cosets it is the unit sphere of the axes, of area element sin⁡β dβ dγ\sin\beta\,\mathrm{d}\beta\,\mathrm{d}\gamma and area 4π4\pi. The element 𝘊𝟤′(ψ)\mathsfit{C_2'}(\psi) carries the rotation of the angles (0,β,γ)(0, \beta, \gamma) to that of (−2ψ,π−β,γ+π)(-2\psi, \pi - \beta, \gamma + \pi), of the opposite axis. Therefore the cell of 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}} is the half slice β∈[0,π/2]\beta \in [0, \pi/2], of area 2π2\pi, the projective plane of the axis lines. The group 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} is one coset of itself, and its cell is the point 𝘌\mathsfit{E}, of volume 1.

17.4 Fields ¶

The field fundamental_domain has these fields:

  1. components: ["proper", "improper"] for a group of type I (ℱ=𝒟∪𝘪 𝒟\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{D} \cup \mathsfit{i}\,\mathcal{D}), otherwise ["proper"], in the words of coset (section 16).

  2. cell: 𝒟\mathcal{D}, with these fields:

    1. shape: "whole", "slab", "cube", "prism", "octahedron", "truncated cube" or "dodecahedron" for a finite group, with facets and vertices, and "slice" or "point" (the cell {𝘌}\{\mathsfit{E}\}) for a continuous group.
    2. facets[]: one entry for each pair of opposite facets, by element, with element and inverse (the indices of 𝘨\mathsfit{g} and 𝘨−1\mathsfit{g}^{-1}), angle (the turn of 𝘨\mathsfit{g}), distance (tan⁡(ω𝘨/4)\tan(\omega_{\mathsfit{g}}/4)) and unit_axis (𝐯^𝘨\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathsfit{g}}).
    3. vertices[]: the vertices 𝝆\boldsymbol{\rho}, by decreasing third coordinate, then second, then first (none for "whole" and "slab").
    4. alpha (a "slice"): turn, 0.
    5. beta, gamma (a "slice"): t0 and t1, in turns.
    6. volume_element (a "slice"): "sin(beta) dbeta dgamma".
  3. space: (𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥))\𝖲𝖮(𝟥)(\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)})\backslash\mathsf{SO(3)}, by its name ("lens space", "prism space", "octahedral space", "truncated-cube space", "Poincare dodecahedral space", "sphere", "projective plane" or "point"), and lens, [2∣𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)∣,1][2\lvert\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}\rvert, 1] for a lens space, or null not-defined-for-family otherwise.
  4. volumes: group, cell, domain and o3, each the volume of a set of dimension 0 (a number of elements) to 3, coefficient times π\pi to the power pi_power. The volume domain is cell times the number of components.
  5. harmonics: the harmonics on 𝖦\𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G}\backslash\mathsf{O(3)} (section 17.5).

17.5 Harmonics on the orientation space ¶

The field harmonics of fundamental_domain gives the harmonics on 𝖦\𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G}\backslash\mathsf{O(3)}: functions on the orientations of section 17.1, built from the matrices of section 9 and the blocks of section 14. Let c=(cJ,cJ−1,…,c−J)c = (c_J, c_{J-1}, \dots, c_{-J}) be the amplitudes of the kets of the vector of the copy κ\kappa at JJ of the trivial irrep or of the determinant character (group.determinant_character). The vector of the trivial irrep is invariant under the matrices D(J,p)D^{(J,p)} of p=(−1)Jp = (-1)^J, and the vector of the determinant character under the matrices of p=−(−1)Jp = -(-1)^J (sections 11 and 14). The harmonics of the vector are the 2J+12J + 1 functions

(17.4)fκ,m(𝘘)=∑m′cm′∗ Dm′,m(J,p)(𝘘),m=J,J−1,…,−J,f_{\kappa,m}(\mathsfit{Q}) = \sum_{m'} c_{m'}^{*}\, D^{(J,p)}_{m',m}(\mathsfit{Q}), \qquad m = J, J - 1, \dots, -J,

of the elements 𝘘\mathsfit{Q} of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}. As D(J,p)D^{(J,p)} is unitary, the row c†c^{\dagger} obeys c†D(J,p)(𝘨)=c†c^{\dagger} D^{(J,p)}(\mathsfit{g}) = c^{\dagger} for every element 𝘨\mathsfit{g} of 𝖦\mathsf{G}. Thus fκ,m(𝘨𝘘)=fκ,m(𝘘)f_{\kappa,m}(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q}) = f_{\kappa,m}(\mathsfit{Q}), and fκ,mf_{\kappa,m} is a function on the cosets 𝖦𝘘\mathsf{G}\mathsfit{Q}.

At (J,p)(J, p) the harmonics number (2J+1) bΓ(J,p)(2J + 1)\, b_{\Gamma}(J, p), where bΓ(J,p)b_{\Gamma}(J, p) is the multiplicity of the trivial irrep Γ\Gamma in D(J,p)D^{(J,p)} restricted to 𝖦\mathsf{G} (subduction_o3, section 11). Over both parities, the harmonics at JJ on each of the components of ℱ\mathcal{F} number 2J+12J + 1 times the multiplicity of the trivial irrep of 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)} in D(J)D^{(J)}. Harmonics of different (J,p,κ,m)(J, p, \kappa, m) are orthogonal, and the integral of ∣fκ,m∣2\lvert f_{\kappa,m}\rvert^{2} over ℱ\mathcal{F} is vol⁡ℱ/(2J+1)\operatorname{vol}\mathcal{F}/(2J + 1) in the volumes of section 17.1. The harmonics are the eigenfunctions of the Laplacian of the metric of section 17.1, of the eigenvalue J (J+1)J\,(J + 1). The members with m=0m = 0 depend only on the direction 𝐝=(0,0,1) P(𝘘)−1\mathbf{d} = (0, 0, 1)\, P(\mathsfit{Q})^{-1} (section 17.6). Each such member is 4π/(2J+1)\sqrt{4\pi/(2J + 1)} times the conjugate of the function of its block (section 4.1) at 𝐝\mathbf{d}, an invariant spherical harmonic. With the vectors cμc_\mu of a block of another irrep Λ\Lambda, the same sum gives the functions fμ,mf_{\mu,m} with fμ,m(𝘨𝘘)=∑νDμ,νΛ(𝘨) fν,m(𝘘)f_{\mu,m}(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q}) = \sum_\nu D^{\Lambda}_{\mu,\nu}(\mathsfit{g})\, f_{\nu,m}(\mathsfit{Q}).

For 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}} the vectors are the kets with m=0m = 0, so its harmonics at p=(−1)Jp = (-1)^J are 4π/(2J+1)\sqrt{4\pi/(2J + 1)} times the spherical harmonics of the axis (0,0,1) P(𝘘)(0, 0, 1)\, P(\mathsfit{Q}) of section 17.3. 17.6 The orbifold of the directions ¶

The field orbifold of a finite record gives the orbifold 𝖲2/𝖦\mathsf{S}^{2}/\mathsf{G} of the directions. A direction is a unit vector 𝐫\mathbf{r}, moved by an element as a point (section 4.1): 𝐫↦𝐫 P(𝘨)\mathbf{r} \mapsto \mathbf{r}\, P(\mathsfit{g}). The orbifold is the set of the orbits of 𝖦\mathsf{G} on the sphere 𝖲2\mathsf{S}^{2} of the directions, each orbit marked by the class of the stabilizer of its directions. The stabilizer of a direction is the group of the rotations about it, with the mirrors through it when there are any. The strata are the orbits of directions by their stabilizer:

  1. 𝖢𝟣\mathsf{C_{1}}: the generic points.
  2. 𝖢N\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}}}, N≥2N \ge 2: a cone point of order NN.
  3. 𝖢𝗌\mathsf{C_{s}}: a point of an open arc of the mirror edge.
  4. 𝖢N𝗏\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}v}}, N≥2N \ge 2: a corner of order NN, of angle π/N\pi/N, on the mirror edge.

Each cone point and each corner is one orbit, so the two poles of 𝖢N\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}}} are two cone points with one class of stabilizers. The orders of the cone points and of the corners are the numbers of the symbol group.orbifold (section 2.2). The orbifold lies on a sphere when 𝖦\mathsf{G} is of type I, on a disk when 𝖦\mathsf{G} contains a mirror, and otherwise on a projective plane, the cross-cap x of the symbol. The Euler characteristic χ\chi of the orbifold (euler_characteristic) is

(17.5)χ=χ0−∑cone points(1−1N)−∑corners12(1−1N)=2∣𝖦∣,\chi = \chi_0 - \sum_{\text{cone points}} \Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{N}\Bigr) - \sum_{\text{corners}} \frac{1}{2}\Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{N}\Bigr) = \frac{2}{\lvert \mathsf{G}\rvert},

where χ0\chi_0 is 2 for the sphere and 1 for the disk and the projective plane. The area of the orbifold is 2πχ=4π/∣𝖦∣2\pi\chi = 4\pi/\lvert \mathsf{G}\rvert. Each stratum names the node of its stabilizer's class in subgroup_lattice (section 15.1) and the entry of permutation_representations.actions with that node (section 12.5). Thus strata with one class of stabilizers name one entry. For a subgroup 𝖧\mathsf{H} of 𝖦\mathsf{G}, 𝖲2/𝖧\mathsf{S}^{2}/\mathsf{H} covers 𝖲2/𝖦\mathsf{S}^{2}/\mathsf{G} with [𝖦:𝖧][\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{H}] sheets.

The direction 𝐝(𝘘)=(0,0,1) P(𝘘)−1\mathbf{d}(\mathsfit{Q}) = (0, 0, 1)\, P(\mathsfit{Q})^{-1} is the axis zz in the frame of a molecule placed by 𝘘\mathsfit{Q} (section 17.1). As 𝐝(𝘨𝘘)=𝐝(𝘘) P(𝘨)−1\mathbf{d}(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q}) = \mathbf{d}(\mathsfit{Q})\, P(\mathsfit{g})^{-1}, the map 𝘘↦𝐝(𝘘)\mathsfit{Q} \mapsto \mathbf{d}(\mathsfit{Q}) carries the space (𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥))\𝖲𝖮(𝟥)(\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)})\backslash\mathsf{SO(3)} of section 17.1 onto 𝖲2/(𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥))\mathsf{S}^{2}/(\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}). The fiber over a point is the circle of the turns 𝘘 𝘙𝘻(ϕ)\mathsfit{Q}\,\mathsfit{R_{z}}(\phi), which change the Euler angle γ\gamma of section 4.2. Over a cone point of order NN the fiber is NN times shorter. For a group with an improper element, 𝖦\𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G}\backslash\mathsf{O(3)} is (𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥))\𝖲𝖮(𝟥)(\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)})\backslash\mathsf{SO(3)} (section 17.1), and 𝖲2/(𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥))\mathsf{S}^{2}/(\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}) covers 𝖲2/𝖦\mathsf{S}^{2}/\mathsf{G} with 2 sheets.

The lens space 𝖫(p,q)\mathsf{L}(p, q) of section 17.1 is the quotient of the sphere 𝖲3\mathsf{S}^{3} of the complex unit vectors by the matrix diag⁡(ζp,ζpq)\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_p, \zeta_p^{q}). The sphere is oriented as the boundary of the unit ball. The group 𝖲𝖴(𝟤)\mathsf{SU(2)} (section 19) is oriented as 𝖲3\mathsf{S}^{3} by the first column of its matrices, and 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} so that the covering of section 21.1 preserves orientation. As U(𝘨𝘘)=±U(𝘘) U(𝘨)U(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q}) = \pm U(\mathsfit{Q})\, U(\mathsfit{g}), and U(𝘨)U(\mathsfit{g}) is diagonal for 𝘨\mathsfit{g} in 𝖢N\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}}}, the first column of U(𝘘)U(\mathsfit{Q}) changes by a 2N2Nth root of unity. Thus the map from 𝘘\mathsfit{Q} to that column carries 𝖢N\𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}}}\backslash\mathsf{SO(3)} onto 𝖫(2N,1)\mathsf{L}(2N, 1), the lens space of lens (section 17.4), and preserves orientation. In the same way 𝖲𝖴(𝟤)\mathsf{SU(2)} modulo a cyclic subgroup of order NN, the space of section 21.3, is 𝖫(N,1)\mathsf{L}(N, 1). In this orientation the Euler angles (α,β,γ)(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) of section 4.2 are a negative chart, and two fibers link −1/2-1/2. The rotations about xx, yy and zz by small positive angles, in this order, form a negative frame at 𝘌\mathsfit{E}. Then (𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥))\𝖲𝖮(𝟥)(\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)})\backslash\mathsf{SO(3)} is the Seifert fibered space over the sphere with the rr cone points of 𝖲2/(𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥))\mathsf{S}^{2}/(\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}), of orders α1,…,αr\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_r, with the invariants

(17.6)(b; (α1,β1),…,(αr,βr))=(−2; (α1,α1−1),…,(αr,αr−1)),e=−b−∑iβiαi=2∣𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)∣,(b;\ (\alpha_1, \beta_1), \dots, (\alpha_r, \beta_r)) = (-2;\ (\alpha_1, \alpha_1 - 1), \dots, (\alpha_r, \alpha_r - 1)), \qquad e = -b - \sum_i \frac{\beta_i}{\alpha_i} = \frac{2}{\lvert \mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}\rvert},

normalized by 0<βi<αi0 < \beta_i < \alpha_i, where ee is its Euler number. The opposite orientation, that of the Euler angles, gives (2−r; (α1,1),…,(αr,1))(2 - r;\ (\alpha_1, 1), \dots, (\alpha_r, 1)) and e=−2/∣𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)∣e = -2/\lvert \mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}\rvert. The first homology of (𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥))\𝖲𝖮(𝟥)(\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)})\backslash\mathsf{SO(3)} has the order ∣e∣\lvert e\rvert times the product of the αi\alpha_i. This order is the index of the commutator subgroup of the preimage of 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)} (section 20.4), and for a lens space the first entry of lens (section 17.4).

Continuous groups. The orbits of a continuous group on the sphere form no orbifold of this kind. They are the points of an arc of the polar angle θ\theta, or one point for 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}. The arc runs from 0 to π\pi for 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}} and 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}} and from 0 to π/2\pi/2 for 𝖢∞𝗁\mathsf{C_{\infty h}}, 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}} and 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}}. Each end and the interior of the arc is a stratum, with its orbit and the class of its stabilizer among the subgroups of section 16.1.

17.7 The region ¶

The field region gives a fundamental region of 𝖦\mathsf{G} on the sphere: a convex spherical polygon that holds one direction of every orbit, more only on its boundary. Its vertices are exact, unnormalized directions in counterclockwise order seen from outside the sphere. The first vertex is (0,0,1)(0, 0, 1) when it is a vertex, and otherwise (1,0,0)(1, 0, 0) for an axial group and (0,1,φ)(0, 1, \varphi) for 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}. Side ii runs from vertex ii to vertex i+1i + 1, the last side back to vertex 0, along a great circle given by the inward normal of its plane.

The region lies in one chamber of the mirrors of 𝖦\mathsf{G}, and its sides on mirrors form the mirror edge of the orbifold. In the azimuth ϕ\phi from the axis xx and the polar angle θ\theta from the axis zz, the families have these regions:

  1. 𝖢N\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}}}: 0≤ϕ≤2π/N0 \le \phi \le 2\pi/N, the sphere for N=1N = 1.
  2. 𝖢N𝗏\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}v}}: 0≤ϕ≤π/N0 \le \phi \le \pi/N.
  3. 𝖢N𝗁\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}h}}, 𝖲𝟤N\mathsf{S_{2\mathnormal{N}}} and 𝖣N\mathsf{D_{\mathnormal{N}}}: 0≤ϕ≤2π/N0 \le \phi \le 2\pi/N and θ≤π/2\theta \le \pi/2, the equator side cut at ϕ=π/N\phi = \pi/N in 𝖲𝟤N\mathsf{S_{2\mathnormal{N}}} and 𝖣N\mathsf{D_{\mathnormal{N}}}.
  4. 𝖣N𝗁\mathsf{D_{\mathnormal{N}h}}: 0≤ϕ≤π/N0 \le \phi \le \pi/N and θ≤π/2\theta \le \pi/2.
  5. 𝖣N𝖽\mathsf{D_{\mathnormal{N}d}}: −π/(2N)≤ϕ≤π/(2N)-\pi/(2N) \le \phi \le \pi/(2N) and θ≤π/2\theta \le \pi/2, the equator side cut at ϕ=0\phi = 0.
  6. 𝖳\mathsf{T}: y≥∣x∣y \ge \lvert x\rvert and z≥∣x∣z \ge \lvert x\rvert.
  7. 𝖳𝖽\mathsf{T_{d}}: z≥y≥∣x∣z \ge y \ge \lvert x\rvert.
  8. 𝖳𝗁\mathsf{T_{h}} and 𝖮\mathsf{O}: x≥0x \ge 0, y≥0y \ge 0, z≥xz \ge x and z≥yz \ge y.
  9. 𝖮𝗁\mathsf{O_{h}}: z≥y≥x≥0z \ge y \ge x \ge 0.
  10. 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}: the triangle of the vertices (0,1,φ)(0, 1, \varphi), (1,0,φ2)(1, 0, \varphi^2) and (φ,1,φ2)(\varphi, 1, \varphi^2), on a 5-fold, a 3-fold and a 2-fold axis.
  11. 𝖨\mathsf{I}: the triangle of 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}} with its mirror image in the side from (0,1,φ)(0, 1, \varphi) to (1,0,φ2)(1, 0, \varphi^2), a kite with the fourth vertex (0,0,1)(0, 0, 1).

The vertices are the corners of the polygon, the cone points and the corners of the orbifold on its boundary, and the cut point of the equator side of 𝖲𝟤N\mathsf{S_{2\mathnormal{N}}}. A region bounded by one great circle has two vertices on it, where the rule of its family places them at N=1N = 1 or 2. These vertices are the poles for 𝖢𝟤\mathsf{C_{2}} and 𝖢𝟣𝗏\mathsf{C_{1v}} and the points (±1,0,0)(\pm 1, 0, 0) for 𝖢𝟣𝗁\mathsf{C_{1h}}, 𝖲𝟤\mathsf{S_{2}} and 𝖣𝟣\mathsf{D_{1}}. In 𝖣𝟣𝗁\mathsf{D_{1h}} and 𝖣𝟣𝖽\mathsf{D_{1d}} the two meridians of the rule form one side.

A side on a mirror of 𝖦\mathsf{G} has the kind "mirror", and its element is that mirror. Every other side has the kind "glued": its element carries it onto another side, its partner, and carries the region to the other side of the partner. The sense is "reversed" when the element carries from to the partner's to, as a rotation about a shared vertex does, and "same" when it carries from to the partner's from. A mirror side is its own partner, in the sense "same". The gluing identifies the vertices in classes, each a point of the orbifold. The angles of a class add up to 2π/N2\pi/N at a cone point of order NN, to π/N\pi/N at a corner of order NN and to π\pi at another point of the mirror edge. At a generic point their sum is 2π2\pi.

The elements of the sides generate 𝖦\mathsf{G}, and the vertex classes give its relations (Poincaré's theorem for polygons). The generators are one for each mirror side, its mirror, and one for each pair of glued sides, the element of the side of lower index. Each mirror side gives the relator 𝘴2\mathsfit{s}^2 of its mirror 𝘴\mathsfit{s}, and each vertex class gives one relator by a walk around it. From a vertex vv, the walk crosses a glued side tt at vv to the vertex v′v' onto which the element of tt carries vv. The walk then crosses the side at v′v' other than the partner of tt. Its word WW is the product, in the order of section 4.1, of the inverses of the elements of the sides crossed, from the last side crossed to the first. A walk that returns to its first vertex and side gives the relator WNW^N, where NN is the order of the class's point and 1 at a generic point. A walk from the mirror 𝘴\mathsfit{s} of a side to the mirror 𝘴′\mathsfit{s}' of a side gives (𝘴 W−1𝘴′ W)N(\mathsfit{s}\, W^{-1} \mathsfit{s}'\, W)^N, where NN is the order of the corner and 1 at another point of the mirror edge. The presented group has the order ∣𝖦∣\lvert \mathsf{G}\rvert, and the map of its generators to their elements is an isomorphism.

The field region has these fields:

  1. vertices[]: the vertices.
  2. sides[]: one for each side, with from and to (its vertices), normal (the inward normal of its plane), kind, element, partner and sense.
  3. angles[]: the interior angle at each vertex, in units of π\pi, as [n,d][n, d].
  4. vertex_classes[]: the classes, each with vertices, stratum ("cone point", "corner", "mirror arc" or "generic"), order (NN, and 1 for the last two) and angle_sum (in units of π\pi).
  5. contains: the image 𝐫0 P(𝘨)\mathbf{r}_0\, P(\mathsfit{g}) of the point 𝐫0=(1,2,4)\mathbf{r}_0 = (1, 2, 4) of the orbit of section 12.5 with the stabilizer 𝖢𝟣\mathsf{C_{1}}, inside the region, as direction, and its element 𝘨\mathsfit{g}.

The field presentation has generators[], each with element and side, and relators[], each with word, power and its source, a vertex_class or a side. A word is a list of [generator, exponent] pairs, multiplied in the order of section 4.1, the first applied first, and raised to power.

17.8 Fields of orbifold ¶

The field orbifold of a finite record has these fields:

  1. symbol: the pointer to group.orbifold.
  2. surface: "sphere", "disk" or "projective plane".
  3. orientable: whether 𝖦\mathsf{G} is of type I.
  4. euler_characteristic: the Euler characteristic χ=2/∣𝖦∣\chi = 2/\lvert \mathsf{G}\rvert, as [n,d][n, d].
  5. area: the area 4π/∣𝖦∣4\pi/\lvert \mathsf{G}\rvert in units of π\pi, as [n,d][n, d].
  6. cone_points[]: one for each cone point, in the order of its first vertex, with order, direction, stabilizer_node, orbit and vertex, the first vertex of its class.
  7. mirror: null no-mirror for a group without a mirror, otherwise the edge, with corners[] (as cone_points[]) and arcs[] (stabilizer_node, orbit and side). Both lists follow the edge from the first vertex of its mirror side of least index.
  8. cross_cap: whether the surface is the projective plane.
  9. generic: stabilizer_node and orbit of the generic points.
  10. region, presentation: section 17.7.

  11. orientation_space: the tie to fundamental_domain (section 17.6), with these fields:

    1. base: "self" for a group of type I, otherwise the pointer to orbifold in the record of 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}, named by product_form.rotation_subgroup.o3_class.
    2. sheets: the sheets of 𝖲2/(𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥))\mathsf{S}^{2}/(\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}) over 𝖲2/𝖦\mathsf{S}^{2}/\mathsf{G}, 1 or 2.
    3. seifert: the invariants of section 17.6, with genus (0), base_orientable (true), b, fibers[], euler_number (ee, as [n,d][n, d]) and first_homology_order. An entry of fibers[], one for each cone point of the base, has alpha, beta and cone_point, its index in the base's cone_points.

Continuous groups. The field orbifold of a continuous record is null continuous-group, and the field orbit_space has these fields:

  1. kind: "arc" or "point".
  2. theta: the ends t0 and t1 of the arc in turns, or null not-defined-for-family for a point.
  3. strata[]: the ends and the interior of the arc in order, or the point. A stratum has position ("t0", "interior", "t1" or "point"), orbit ("pole", "pair of poles", "circle", "pair of circles", "equator" or "sphere") and stabilizer. The stabilizer gives the o3_class and the schoenflies of its class among the subgroups of section 16.1.
