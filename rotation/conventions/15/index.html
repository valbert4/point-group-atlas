15 Subgroup lattices ¶

15.1 subgroup_lattice (finite groups) ¶

The key subgroup_lattice of a finite record is the lattice of the subgroups of 𝖦\mathsf{G}. Elements are the indices of the record's elements.items, multiplied by its multiplication_table; a subgroup is a set of indices; 𝖧𝘨={𝘨−1𝘩𝘨:𝘩∈𝖧}\mathsf{H}^{\mathsfit{g}} = \{\mathsfit{g}^{-1} \mathsfit{h} \mathsfit{g} : \mathsfit{h} \in \mathsf{H}\}. Every list of indices is ascending.

  1. nodes[]: the 𝖦\mathsf{G}-conjugacy classes of subgroups, sorted by (order, element list of the representative) and named n00, n01, … (the ids of section 12.1). Per node: order ∣𝖧∣\lvert \mathsf{H}\rvert; index [𝖦:𝖧][\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{H}]; class_size, the number of subgroups in the class; normal (class_size = 1); normalizer_order ∣𝖭𝖦(𝖧)∣\lvert \mathsf{N}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H})\rvert; normalizer and centralizer, the subgroups 𝖭𝖦(𝖧)={𝘨:𝖧𝘨=𝖧}\mathsf{N}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H}) = \{\mathsfit{g} : \mathsf{H}^{\mathsfit{g}} = \mathsf{H}\} and 𝖢𝖦(𝖧)={𝘨:𝘨𝘩=𝘩𝘨 for every 𝘩∈𝖧}\mathsf{C}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H}) = \{\mathsfit{g} : \mathsfit{g} \mathsfit{h} = \mathsfit{h} \mathsfit{g} \text{ for every } \mathsfit{h} \in \mathsf{H}\} of the representative, each with node, the id of its class, and elements; o3_class as in section 12.1 (the record whose elements have the same multiset of (det⁡\det, turn)); o3_schoenflies, that record's schoenflies, with C_s for 𝖢𝟣𝗏\mathsf{C_{1v}} and C_i for 𝖲𝟤\mathsf{S_{2}}; embedding: kind, the fixed string "G-conjugacy class of subgroups"; members, every subgroup of the class sorted by element list, each with elements, conjugator (the smallest index 𝘨\mathsfit{g} with member=representative𝘨\text{member} = \text{representative}^{\mathsfit{g}}) and conjugator_symbol (the symbol of 𝘨\mathsfit{g}); representative, the first member, with elements, the symbols and poles of those elements (as in the record) and class_ids (the sorted ids of the classes of 𝖦\mathsf{G} it meets).
  2. edges[]: the covering pairs of the order lower≤upper  ⟺  \text{lower} \le \text{upper} \iff some member of the lower class lies in the representative of the upper class, sorted by (index of the lower node, index of the upper node): lower, upper (node ids); index, the order of the upper node divided by that of the lower node; embeddings_in_upper, the number of orbits, under conjugation by the representative of the upper class, of the members of the lower class that lie in that representative; witness, the first member of the lower class (in members order) that lies in the representative of the upper class, with lower_elements, lower_symbols, lower_poles, conjugator, conjugator_symbol (as in members) and upper_elements (the representative of the upper class).
  3. counts: subgroups (∑\sum class_size), g_classes (the number of nodes), o3_classes (the number of distinct o3_class), g_classes_by_order and o3_classes_by_order (keyed by the order as a decimal string).

15.2 data/point_groups.json ¶

The containment lattice of the 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}-conjugacy classes of the catalogue's groups:

  1. nodes[]: one per class, named by the group name of a record of that class (94: 87 finite, 7 continuous). The four groups whose class is that of another record are not nodes; aliases.finite maps each to its node: C1h →\to C1v, D1 →\to C2, D1d →\to C2h, D1h →\to C2v. Per node: id; kind ("finite" or "continuous"); order; type (the record's); family and N (the record's family); schoenflies (the record's, with C_s and C_i as in section 15.1). order is null for the seven continuous nodes, family and N for the seven exceptional and the seven continuous nodes.
  2. Nodes are sorted by (order, id) with the continuous nodes last; edges by (order of lower, lower, order of upper, upper) in the same way.
  3. counts: nodes; relations, the number of ordered pairs (a,b)(a, b), a≠ba \ne b, such that a group of class aa is contained in a group of class bb; covering_edges.
  4. edges[]: the covering pairs of that order, each with covering true, lower, upper and a witness: for a finite upper, {"kind": "explicit subgroup of the record", "in", "elements", "symbols"}, the subgroup of the record in = upper (in the frame of section 3) that lies in class lower and has the smallest element list, by its element indices and symbols in that record; for a continuous upper, {"kind": "argument", "reason"} with a one-sentence reason.
  5. family_rules.rules: 49 entries, one per (upper_family, lower_family) over the axial families in the order Cn, S2n, Cnh, Cnv, Dn, Dnh, Dnd (upper family outer). rule is the condition on (N,M)(N, \texttt{M}) under which the group of upper_family with parameter NN has a subgroup 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}-conjugate to the group of lower_family with parameter M, after the N=1N = 1 identifications (𝖣𝟣≅𝖢𝟤\mathsf{D_{1}} \cong \mathsf{C_{2}}, 𝖢𝟣𝗁≅𝖢𝟣𝗏\mathsf{C_{1h}} \cong \mathsf{C_{1v}}, 𝖣𝟣𝗁≅𝖢𝟤𝗏\mathsf{D_{1h}} \cong \mathsf{C_{2v}}, 𝖣𝟣𝖽≅𝖢𝟤𝗁\mathsf{D_{1d}} \cong \mathsf{C_{2h}}), as a short formula ("M | N", "M >= 2 and 2M | N", …) or "never". For the lower families Cnh, Dn, Dnh and Dnd a rule covers the members with M≥2\texttt{M} \ge 2; those with M=1\texttt{M} = 1, 𝖢𝟣𝗁\mathsf{C_{1h}}, 𝖣𝟣\mathsf{D_{1}}, 𝖣𝟣𝗁\mathsf{D_{1h}} and 𝖣𝟣𝖽\mathsf{D_{1d}}, are read under the family of their node: as Cnv with M=1\texttt{M} = 1, Cn with M=2\texttt{M} = 2, Cnv with M=2\texttt{M} = 2 and Cnh with M=2\texttt{M} = 2.
