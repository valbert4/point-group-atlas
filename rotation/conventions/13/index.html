13 Cartesian tensors ¶

In cartesian_tensors, an element acts on polynomials in xx, yy, zz by T(𝘨) xj=∑ixiP(𝘨)ijT(\mathsfit{g})\, x_j = \sum_i x_i P(\mathsfit{g})_{ij} (section 4), extended multiplicatively; the axial vector transforms by (det⁡𝘨) T(𝘨)(\det \mathsfit{g})\, T(\mathsfit{g}). A monomial key lists the exponents of xx, yy, zz ("2,1,0" is x2yx^2 y), and a polynomial is an object from monomial keys to exact coefficients.

The elastic stiffness tensor cijklc_{ijkl} is symmetric in its first two indices, in its last two and under the exchange of the two pairs; the piezoelectric tensor dijkd_{ijk} is symmetric in its last two indices; the photoelastic tensor pijklp_{ijkl} is symmetric in its first two and in its last two indices, as are the electrostriction and the quadratic electro-optic tensors; the susceptibility χijkl(3)\chi^{(3)}_{ijkl} of third-harmonic generation is symmetric in its last three indices. All four are polar tensors, and an element acts on each of their indices by P(𝘨)P(\mathsfit{g}) as on the coefficients of a polynomial in xx, yy, zz, not as on the coordinates of a point. The electrogyration tensor gijk\mathnormal{g}_{ijk}, the change of the gyration tensor linear in an electric field, is symmetric in its first two indices and axial: an element acts on it as on a polar tensor, times det⁡𝘨\det \mathsfit{g}. The first three and the electrogyration tensor are written in Voigt coordinates, which number the index pairs 11, 22, 33, 23, 13, 12 (and 32, 31, 21) from 1 to 6: the elastic stiffness constants are cIJ=cijklc_{IJ} = c_{ijkl}, the first number at most the second (21 coordinates; the matrix of the cIJc_{IJ} is symmetric), the piezoelectric moduli are diJ=dijkd_{iJ} = d_{ijk} for the pairs numbered 1, 2, 3 and diJ=2 dijkd_{iJ} = 2\,d_{ijk} for those numbered 4, 5, 6 (18 coordinates), the factor 2 of the standard tables of material constants, the electrogyration coefficients are gIk=gijk\mathnormal{g}_{Ik} = \mathnormal{g}_{ijk} and gIk=2 gijk\mathnormal{g}_{Ik} = 2\,\mathnormal{g}_{ijk} in the same way, the pair first (18 coordinates), and the photoelastic constants are pIJ=pijklp_{IJ} = p_{ijkl} (36 coordinates). The third-harmonic susceptibility is written by its components χijkl(3)\chi^{(3)}_{ijkl} with j≤k≤lj \le k \le l (30 coordinates). The symmetric axial tensors of ranks 2 (optical activity) and 3 are the quadratic and the cubic polynomials, on which an element acts by (det⁡𝘨) T(𝘨)(\det \mathsfit{g})\, T(\mathsfit{g}). The documents label the spaces by their Jahn symbols (V a polar vector, e an axial factor, a power the number of factors, brackets their symmetrized product): V0\mathrm{V^{0}}, V\mathrm{V}, [V2]\mathrm{[V^{2}]}, [V3]\mathrm{[V^{3}]}, [V4]\mathrm{[V^{4}]}, eV\mathrm{eV}, e[V2]\mathrm{e[V^{2}]}, e[V3]\mathrm{e[V^{3}]}, [[V2]2]\mathrm{[[V^{2}]^{2}]}, V[V2]\mathrm{V[V^{2}]}, e[V2]V\mathrm{e[V^{2}]V}, [V2]2\mathrm{[V^{2}]^{2}}, V[V3]\mathrm{V[V^{3}]} and V4\mathrm{V^{4}}, in the order of spaces[].

  1. spaces[], in this order: the polar spaces of rank 0,1,…,40, 1, \dots, 4 (the homogeneous polynomials of degree rank), the axial spaces of rank 1, 2 and 3, the elastic stiffness tensors, the piezoelectric tensors, the electrogyration tensors, the photoelastic tensors, the third-harmonic susceptibilities and the polar tensors of rank 4 without symmetry: id ("polar-0" … "polar-4", "axial-1" … "axial-3", "elastic", "piezoelectric", "electrogyration", "photoelastic", "third-harmonic", "general-4"), kind ("axial" for the axial spaces and the electrogyration tensors, else "polar"), rank (4, 3, 3, 4, 4 and 4 for the last six); dim, the number of coordinates: the (rank+1)(rank+2)/2(\texttt{rank} + 1)(\texttt{rank} + 2)/2 monomials, 21, 18, 18, 36, 30 and 81; harmonic_dim (the spaces polar-0 to polar-4 and axial-1 to axial-3), 2⋅rank+12 \cdot \texttt{rank} + 1, the dimension of the harmonic polynomials of that degree; multiplicities, each irrep of nonzero multiplicity in the whole space with that multiplicity; and blocks[] (not for "general-4"), one per irrep of nonzero multiplicity, in irrep order: irrep, multiplicity, dim (multiplicity times the dimension of the irrep: the dimension of the isotypic component), field ("Q" if every coefficient is rational, else "Q(zeta)") and basis, the reduced row echelon basis of the isotypic component, each vector scaled so that its power-basis coordinates are coprime integers with a positive pivot. The coordinates are, in order, the monomials of a polar or axial space by their keys descending (x3x^3, x2yx^2 y, x2zx^2 z, xy2x y^2, xyzxyz, xz2x z^2, y3y^3, y2zy^2 z, yz2y z^2, z3z^3), the cIJc_{IJ} keyed "IJ" ("11", "12", …, "16", "22", …, "66"), the diJd_{iJ} keyed "iJ" ("11", …, "16", "21", …, "36"), the gIk\mathnormal{g}_{Ik} keyed "Ik" ("11", "12", "13", "21", …, "63"), the pIJp_{IJ} keyed "IJ" ("11", "12", …, "16", "21", …, "66") and the χijkl(3)\chi^{(3)}_{ijkl} keyed "ijkl" ("1111", "1112", "1113", "1122", …, "3333").
  2. functions[]: the real solid harmonics of degree J≤3J \le 3 with coprime integer coefficients and the axial Rx\mathrm{R}_x, Ry\mathrm{R}_y, Rz\mathrm{R}_z: s 11; p_x xx, p_y yy, p_z zz; d_z2 2z2−x2−y22z^2 - x^2 - y^2, d_x2-y2 x2−y2x^2 - y^2, d_xy xyxy, d_yz yzyz, d_zx zxzx; f_z3 2z3−3x2z−3y2z2z^3 - 3x^2 z - 3y^2 z, f_xz2 4xz2−x3−xy24xz^2 - x^3 - xy^2, f_yz2 4yz2−x2y−y34yz^2 - x^2 y - y^3, f_z(x2-y2) x2z−y2zx^2 z - y^2 z, f_xyz xyzxyz, f_x(x2-3y2) x3−3xy2x^3 - 3xy^2, f_y(3x2-y2) 3x2y−y33x^2 y - y^3; R_x xx, R_y yy, R_z zz in the axial space. Each has name, set (s, p, d, f, axial), J (the degree), kind ("axial" for Rx\mathrm{R}_x, Ry\mathrm{R}_y, Rz\mathrm{R}_z, else "polar"), space (the id of its space), poly, irreps (the irreps whose isotypic components of the function are nonzero, in irrep order) and single_irrep (whether irreps has exactly one entry). A function whose irreps are the two irreps of a complex-conjugate pair lies in one irrep over ℝ\mathbb{R}, the one that the pair forms (section 6.4), which the documents print beside them.

A finite group's isotypic component of an irrep Γ\Gamma of dimension dd is the range of the projector (d/∣𝖦∣)∑𝘨χΓ(𝘨)∗ T(𝘨)(d/\lvert\mathsf{G}\rvert) \sum_{\mathsfit{g}} \chi_\Gamma(\mathsfit{g})^*\, T(\mathsfit{g}), and its multiplicity comes from the traces of the class sums of T(𝘨)T(\mathsfit{g}).

As representations of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}, each of the spaces polar-0 to polar-4 is Hrank⊕r2 (the polar space of rank rank−2)H_{\texttt{rank}} \oplus r^2\,(\text{the polar space of rank } \texttt{rank}-2), with HJH_J the harmonic polynomials of degree JJ, which carry D(J,(−1)J)D^{(J,(-1)^J)}, and r2=x2+y2+z2r^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2; the axial vectors carry D(1,+)D^{(1,+)}, the symmetric axial tensors of rank 2 D(0,−)⊕D(2,−)D^{(0,-)} \oplus D^{(2,-)} and those of rank 3 D(1,+)⊕D(3,+)D^{(1,+)} \oplus D^{(3,+)}. In general the content follows from the weights: give x+iyx + \mathrm{i}y the weight 1, x−iyx - \mathrm{i}y the weight −1-1 and zz the weight 0, so that each space has a basis of products of these, each of weight the sum of its factors'; the multiplicity of D(J,p)D^{(J,p)} is then the number of these products of weight JJ less the number of weight J+1J + 1, and the parity pp is (−1)rank(-1)^{\texttt{rank}} in a polar space and −(−1)rank-(-1)^{\texttt{rank}} in an axial one. The elastic tensors carry 2D(0,+)⊕2D(2,+)⊕D(4,+)2D^{(0,+)} \oplus 2D^{(2,+)} \oplus D^{(4,+)}, the piezoelectric ones 2D(1,−)⊕D(2,−)⊕D(3,−)2D^{(1,-)} \oplus D^{(2,-)} \oplus D^{(3,-)}, the electrogyration ones 2D(1,+)⊕D(2,+)⊕D(3,+)2D^{(1,+)} \oplus D^{(2,+)} \oplus D^{(3,+)}, the photoelastic ones 2D(0,+)⊕D(1,+)⊕3D(2,+)⊕D(3,+)⊕D(4,+)2D^{(0,+)} \oplus D^{(1,+)} \oplus 3D^{(2,+)} \oplus D^{(3,+)} \oplus D^{(4,+)}, the third-harmonic susceptibilities D(0,+)⊕D(1,+)⊕2D(2,+)⊕D(3,+)⊕D(4,+)D^{(0,+)} \oplus D^{(1,+)} \oplus 2D^{(2,+)} \oplus D^{(3,+)} \oplus D^{(4,+)} and the general ones 3D(0,+)⊕6D(1,+)⊕6D(2,+)⊕3D(3,+)⊕D(4,+)3D^{(0,+)} \oplus 6D^{(1,+)} \oplus 6D^{(2,+)} \oplus 3D^{(3,+)} \oplus D^{(4,+)}. So multiplicities is the sum, over the irreps D(J,p)D^{(J,p)} that the space carries, of their rows of subduction_o3 (section 11) times their multiplicities, from parities.g for p=+1p = +1 and parities.u for p=−1p = -1. In the block of an irrep of one of the spaces polar-0 to polar-4, the harmonic part (spanned by the bases of section 14 at J=rankJ = \texttt{rank}) has dimension the multiplicity of that row J=rankJ = \texttt{rank} times the dimension of the irrep, and the rest is r2r^2 times the block of the same irrep at rank rank−2\texttt{rank} - 2; the basis vectors are in general not harmonic.

A continuous record holds the same spaces, its irreps written by their instance labels ("1E_1", "E_2g", "D^(2,+)", section 16) and its blocks by parity (g first), then by index, then by family. In the five axial groups the isotypic component of "1E_m" is spanned by the products above of weight mm, that of "2E_m" by those of weight −m-m and that of a two-dimensional "E_m" by those of the weights ±m\pm m; at weight 0 the flip, σ𝘷(0)\mathsfit{\sigma_v}(0) in 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}} and 𝘊𝟤𝘹\mathsfit{C_{2x}} in 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}} and 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}}, separates "A_1" from "A_2", and the inversion gives the parity of the space. In 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} the isotypic component of D(J)D^{(J)} is the eigenspace of the Casimir operator, the sum of the squares of the derivatives at angle 0 of the action of the rotations about xx, yy and zz, for the eigenvalue minus the product of JJ and J+1J + 1 (in a polar space, r2kr^{2k} times the harmonic polynomials of degree JJ). The functions are those of the finite groups.

By a theorem of Hermann [Her34], a tensor invariant under a rotation whose order exceeds the tensor's rank is invariant under every rotation about the same axis, and 𝘚𝟪\mathsfit{S_{8}} acts on the polar tensors of even rank as a rotation of order 8; so in each of these spaces a finite group that is not crystallographic (section 2) has the invariant tensors, the block of the trivial irrep, of the continuous group that it and the rotations about its axis generate (𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} for 𝖨\mathsf{I}, 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} for 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}).

13.1 Invariant polynomials ¶

invariants describes the invariant polynomials, those ff in xx, yy, zz with T(𝘨)f=fT(\mathsfit{g}) f = f for every element 𝘨\mathsfit{g}; they form a ring. Let HΓ(t)H_\Gamma(t) (section 14) be the generating function of the trivial irrep Γ\Gamma, for a finite group PΓ(t)/((1−ta1)(1−ta2))P_\Gamma(t)/((1 - t^{a_1})(1 - t^{a_2})) (section 14). Every polynomial is in one way a sum of r2kr^{2k} times harmonic polynomials, so the ring has the Hilbert series HΓ(t)/(1−t2)H_\Gamma(t)/(1 - t^2), whose coefficient of tkt^k is the number of linearly independent invariants of degree kk.

  1. hilbert_series: numerator, the coefficients of PΓ(t)P_\Gamma(t) of the trivial irrep from t0t^0 ([1] for a continuous group), and denominator, the exponents of the factors 1−tk1 - t^k of the denominator: 2, then those of subduction_basis.generating_function (for a continuous group, those of its trivial family).
  2. polynomial_ring: whether the ring is a polynomial ring, generated by algebraically independent invariants.
  3. polynomials[]: one minimal generating set of the ring, by degree, with r2r^2 first in its degree: degree, poly (a polynomial as above), primary (whether it is a primary invariant; else it is a secondary invariant), generator (false only for r2r^2 in 𝖢𝟣\mathsf{C_{1}}, which xx, yy, zz generate: it is a primary invariant there), and in 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}} factors, the linear forms whose product poly is, by their number of terms and then by their coefficients of xx, yy, zz, descending.

The generating set follows one rule. r2r^2 is a generator unless the products of the generators of lower degree give it (in 𝖢𝟣\mathsf{C_{1}} only). The other generators of degree kk are a basis of the harmonic invariants of degree kk that are orthogonal, for the Fischer product (distinct monomials orthogonal, the square of a monomial the product of the factorials of its exponents), to the harmonic parts of the products of the generators of lower degree: their reduced row echelon basis, as for the blocks above, each scaled to coprime integer coordinates. In 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}} the generators of degree 6, 10 and 15 (15 in 𝖨\mathsf{I} only) are instead the products of the linear forms of the 6 five-fold, 10 three-fold and 15 two-fold axes, each axis scaled so that its coordinates are integer combinations of 1 and φ\varphi, the least nonzero one in absolute value is 1 and the first nonzero one is positive.

The primary invariants are r2r^2 and, for a finite group, the first generators of the degrees a1a_1 and a2a_2 (section 14) with which it has no common complex zero but the origin; the other polynomials are the secondary invariants. The primary invariants are algebraically independent, and every invariant is in one way a sum of 1 and the secondary invariants, each times a polynomial in the primary invariants (the ring is a free module over the polynomials in the primary invariants); the coefficient of tkt^k in PΓ(t)P_\Gamma(t) is the number of secondary invariants of degree kk, the constant 1 counted at k=0k = 0. A continuous group's ring is the polynomial ring in its primary invariants: zz and r2r^2 for 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}} and 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}}, r2r^2 and x2+y2−2z2x^2 + y^2 - 2z^2 for 𝖢∞𝗁\mathsf{C_{\infty h}}, 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}} and 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}}, r2r^2 for 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}.
