13 Cartesian tensors ¶

In cartesian_tensors, an element acts on polynomials in xx, yy, zz by T(𝘨) xj=∑ixiP(𝘨)ijT(\mathsfit{g})\, x_j = \sum_i x_i P(\mathsfit{g})_{ij} (section 4), extended multiplicatively, and the axial vector transforms by (det⁡𝘨) T(𝘨)(\det \mathsfit{g})\, T(\mathsfit{g}). A monomial key lists the exponents of xx, yy, zz ("2,1,0" is x2yx^2 y), and a polynomial is an object from monomial keys to exact coefficients.

In a finite group, the isotypic component of an irrep Γ\Gamma of dimension dd is the range of the projector (d/∣𝖦∣)∑𝘨χΓ(𝘨)∗ T(𝘨)(d/\lvert\mathsf{G}\rvert) \sum_{\mathsfit{g}} \chi_\Gamma(\mathsfit{g})^*\, T(\mathsfit{g}), and its multiplicity comes from the traces of the class sums of T(𝘨)T(\mathsfit{g}).

The elastic stiffness, piezoelectric, photoelastic and third-harmonic tensors are polar, and an element acts on each of their indices by P(𝘨)P(\mathsfit{g}) as on the coefficients of a polynomial in xx, yy, zz. It does not act as on the coordinates of a point. The Voigt coordinates number the index pairs 11, 22, 33, 23, 13, 12 (and 32, 31, 21) from 1 to 6.

The field cartesian_tensors has these fields:

  1. spaces[]: the spaces, in this order, each with its id, rank and dim, the number of coordinates:

    1. "polar-0" … "polar-4": the polar spaces of rank 0,1,…,40, 1, \dots, 4, the homogeneous polynomials of degree rank. Their coordinates are the (rank+1)(rank+2)/2(\texttt{rank} + 1)(\texttt{rank} + 2)/2 monomials, by their keys descending (x3x^3, x2yx^2 y, x2zx^2 z, xy2x y^2, xyzxyz, xz2x z^2, y3y^3, y2zy^2 z, yz2y z^2, z3z^3).
    2. "axial-1" … "axial-3": the axial spaces of rank 1, 2 and 3, with the same coordinates. The symmetric axial tensors of ranks 2 (optical activity) and 3 are the quadratic and the cubic polynomials, on which an element acts by (det⁡𝘨) T(𝘨)(\det \mathsfit{g})\, T(\mathsfit{g}).
    3. "elastic": the elastic stiffness tensors cijklc_{ijkl} of rank 4, symmetric in their first two indices, in their last two and under the exchange of the two pairs. Their 21 Voigt coordinates are the elastic stiffness constants cIJ=cijklc_{IJ} = c_{ijkl}, the first number at most the second, keyed "IJ" ("11", "12", …, "16", "22", …, "66"). The matrix of the cIJc_{IJ} is symmetric.
    4. "piezoelectric": the piezoelectric tensors dijkd_{ijk} of rank 3, symmetric in their last two indices. Their 18 Voigt coordinates are the piezoelectric moduli diJd_{iJ} keyed "iJ" ("11", …, "16", "21", …, "36"), with diJ=dijkd_{iJ} = d_{ijk} for the pairs numbered 1, 2, 3 and diJ=2 dijkd_{iJ} = 2\,d_{ijk} for those numbered 4, 5, 6. The factor 2 is that of the standard tables of material constants.
    5. "electrogyration": the electrogyration tensors gijk\mathnormal{g}_{ijk} of rank 3, the changes of the gyration tensor linear in an electric field. They are symmetric in their first two indices and axial: an element acts on them as on a polar tensor, times det⁡𝘨\det \mathsfit{g}. Their 18 Voigt coordinates are the electrogyration coefficients gIk\mathnormal{g}_{Ik} keyed "Ik" ("11", "12", "13", "21", …, "63"), with gIk=gijk\mathnormal{g}_{Ik} = \mathnormal{g}_{ijk} and gIk=2 gijk\mathnormal{g}_{Ik} = 2\,\mathnormal{g}_{ijk} in the same way, the pair first.
    6. "photoelastic": the photoelastic tensors pijklp_{ijkl} of rank 4, symmetric in their first two and in their last two indices, as are the electrostriction and the quadratic electro-optic tensors. Their 36 Voigt coordinates are the photoelastic constants pIJp_{IJ} keyed "IJ" ("11", "12", …, "16", "21", …, "66"), with pIJ=pijklp_{IJ} = p_{ijkl}.
    7. "third-harmonic": the susceptibilities χijkl(3)\chi^{(3)}_{ijkl} of third-harmonic generation, of rank 4, symmetric in their last three indices. Their 30 coordinates are the components χijkl(3)\chi^{(3)}_{ijkl} with j≤k≤lj \le k \le l, keyed "ijkl" ("1111", "1112", "1113", "1122", …, "3333").
    8. "general-4": the polar tensors of rank 4 without symmetry, of dim 81.

    The spaces have these other fields:

    1. kind: "axial" for the axial spaces and the electrogyration tensors, otherwise "polar".
    2. harmonic_dim (the spaces polar-0 to polar-4 and axial-1 to axial-3): 2⋅rank+12 \cdot \texttt{rank} + 1, the dimension of the harmonic polynomials of that degree.
    3. multiplicities: each irrep of nonzero multiplicity in the whole space, with that multiplicity.
    4. blocks[] (not for "general-4"): one for each irrep of nonzero multiplicity, in irrep order, with irrep, multiplicity and dim (multiplicity times the dimension of the irrep, the dimension of the isotypic component). A block's field is "Q" if every coefficient is rational and "Q(zeta)" otherwise, and its basis is the reduced row echelon basis of the isotypic component, in the order of the coordinates above. Each vector is scaled so that its power-basis coordinates are coprime integers with a positive pivot.

    The documents label the spaces by their Jahn symbols (V a polar vector, e an axial factor, a power the number of factors, brackets their symmetrized product): V0\mathrm{V^{0}}, V\mathrm{V}, [V2]\mathrm{[V^{2}]}, [V3]\mathrm{[V^{3}]}, [V4]\mathrm{[V^{4}]}, eV\mathrm{eV}, e[V2]\mathrm{e[V^{2}]}, e[V3]\mathrm{e[V^{3}]}, [[V2]2]\mathrm{[[V^{2}]^{2}]}, V[V2]\mathrm{V[V^{2}]}, e[V2]V\mathrm{e[V^{2}]V}, [V2]2\mathrm{[V^{2}]^{2}}, V[V3]\mathrm{V[V^{3}]} and V4\mathrm{V^{4}}, in the order of spaces[].

  2. functions[]: the real solid harmonics of degree J≤3J \le 3 with coprime integer coefficients and the axial Rx\mathrm{R}_x, Ry\mathrm{R}_y, Rz\mathrm{R}_z:

    1. s 11.
    2. p_x xx, p_y yy, p_z zz.
    3. d_z2 2z2−x2−y22z^2 - x^2 - y^2, d_x2-y2 x2−y2x^2 - y^2, d_xy xyxy, d_yz yzyz, d_zx zxzx.
    4. f_z3 2z3−3x2z−3y2z2z^3 - 3x^2 z - 3y^2 z, f_xz2 4xz2−x3−xy24xz^2 - x^3 - xy^2, f_yz2 4yz2−x2y−y34yz^2 - x^2 y - y^3, f_z(x2-y2) x2z−y2zx^2 z - y^2 z, f_xyz xyzxyz, f_x(x2-3y2) x3−3xy2x^3 - 3xy^2, f_y(3x2-y2) 3x2y−y33x^2 y - y^3.
    5. R_x xx, R_y yy, R_z zz in the axial space.

    Each function has name, set (s, p, d, f, axial), J (the degree), kind ("axial" for Rx\mathrm{R}_x, Ry\mathrm{R}_y, Rz\mathrm{R}_z, otherwise "polar"), space (the id of its space) and poly. It also has irreps (the irreps whose isotypic components of the function are nonzero, in irrep order) and single_irrep (whether irreps has exactly one entry). A function whose irreps are the two irreps of a complex-conjugate pair lies in one irrep over ℝ\mathbb{R}, the irrep formed by the pair (section 6.4), and the documents set this irrep beside them.

As representations of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}, the harmonic polynomials HJH_J of degree JJ carry D(J,(−1)J)D^{(J,(-1)^J)}. With r2=x2+y2+z2r^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2, each of the spaces polar-0 to polar-4 is Hrank⊕r2 (the polar space of rank rank−2)H_{\texttt{rank}} \oplus r^2\,(\text{the polar space of rank } \texttt{rank}-2). The axial vectors carry D(1,+)D^{(1,+)}, and the symmetric axial tensors carry D(0,−)⊕D(2,−)D^{(0,-)} \oplus D^{(2,-)} at rank 2 and D(1,+)⊕D(3,+)D^{(1,+)} \oplus D^{(3,+)} at rank 3.

In general the content follows from the weights: 1 for x+iyx + \mathrm{i}y, −1-1 for x−iyx - \mathrm{i}y and 0 for zz. Each space has a basis of products of these polynomials, each of weight the sum of the weights of its factors. The multiplicity of D(J,p)D^{(J,p)} is then the number of these products of weight JJ minus the number of weight J+1J + 1. The parity pp is (−1)rank(-1)^{\texttt{rank}} in a polar space and −(−1)rank-(-1)^{\texttt{rank}} in an axial one. The other spaces carry these representations:

  1. "elastic": 2D(0,+)⊕2D(2,+)⊕D(4,+)2D^{(0,+)} \oplus 2D^{(2,+)} \oplus D^{(4,+)}.
  2. "piezoelectric": 2D(1,−)⊕D(2,−)⊕D(3,−)2D^{(1,-)} \oplus D^{(2,-)} \oplus D^{(3,-)}.
  3. "electrogyration": 2D(1,+)⊕D(2,+)⊕D(3,+)2D^{(1,+)} \oplus D^{(2,+)} \oplus D^{(3,+)}.
  4. "photoelastic": 2D(0,+)⊕D(1,+)⊕3D(2,+)⊕D(3,+)⊕D(4,+)2D^{(0,+)} \oplus D^{(1,+)} \oplus 3D^{(2,+)} \oplus D^{(3,+)} \oplus D^{(4,+)}.
  5. "third-harmonic": D(0,+)⊕D(1,+)⊕2D(2,+)⊕D(3,+)⊕D(4,+)D^{(0,+)} \oplus D^{(1,+)} \oplus 2D^{(2,+)} \oplus D^{(3,+)} \oplus D^{(4,+)}.
  6. "general-4": 3D(0,+)⊕6D(1,+)⊕6D(2,+)⊕3D(3,+)⊕D(4,+)3D^{(0,+)} \oplus 6D^{(1,+)} \oplus 6D^{(2,+)} \oplus 3D^{(3,+)} \oplus D^{(4,+)}.

Thus multiplicities is the sum, over the irreps D(J,p)D^{(J,p)} carried by the space, of their rows of subduction_o3 (section 11) times their multiplicities, from parities.g for p=+1p = +1 and parities.u for p=−1p = -1. In the spaces polar-0 to polar-4 the block of an irrep has a harmonic part, spanned by the bases of section 14 at J=rankJ = \texttt{rank}. This part has dimension the multiplicity in the row J=rankJ = \texttt{rank} of subduction_o3 times the dimension of the irrep, and the rest is r2r^2 times the block of the same irrep at rank rank−2\texttt{rank} - 2. The basis vectors are in general not harmonic.

Continuous groups. A continuous record holds the same spaces and the functions of the finite groups. It writes its irreps by their instance labels ("1E_1", "E_2g", "D^(2,+)", section 16) and orders its blocks by parity (g first), then by index, then by family. In the five axial continuous groups the isotypic component of an irrep is spanned by the products above of weight mm for "1E_m", −m-m for "2E_m" and ±m\pm m for a two-dimensional "E_m". At weight 0 the flip, σ𝘷(0)\mathsfit{\sigma_v}(0) in 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}} and 𝘊𝟤𝘹\mathsfit{C_{2x}} in 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}} and 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}}, separates "A_1" from "A_2", and the inversion gives the parity of the space. The Casimir operator is the sum of the squares of the derivatives at angle 0 of the action of the rotations about xx, yy and zz. In 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} the isotypic component of D(J)D^{(J)} is the operator's eigenspace for the eigenvalue minus the product of JJ and J+1J + 1. In a polar space this component is r2kr^{2k} times the harmonic polynomials of degree JJ, where 2k=rank−J2k = \texttt{rank} - J.

By a theorem of Hermann [Her34], a tensor invariant under a rotation whose order exceeds the tensor's rank is invariant under every rotation about the same axis. The element 𝘚𝟪\mathsfit{S_{8}} acts on the polar tensors of even rank as a rotation of order 8. Thus in each of these spaces a finite group that is not crystallographic (section 2) has the invariant tensors of a continuous group, the block of its trivial irrep. The finite group and the rotations about its axis generate this group (𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} for 𝖨\mathsf{I}, 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} for 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}).

13.1 Invariant polynomials ¶

The field invariants describes the ring of invariant polynomials, those ff in xx, yy, zz with T(𝘨)f=fT(\mathsfit{g}) f = f for every element 𝘨\mathsfit{g}. The generating function HΓ(t)H_\Gamma(t) (section 14) of the trivial irrep Γ\Gamma is, for a finite group, PΓ(t)/((1−ta1)(1−ta2))P_\Gamma(t)/((1 - t^{a_1})(1 - t^{a_2})) (section 14). Every polynomial is in one way a sum of r2kr^{2k} times harmonic polynomials. Thus the ring has the Hilbert series HΓ(t)/(1−t2)H_\Gamma(t)/(1 - t^2), whose coefficient of tkt^k is the number of linearly independent invariants of degree kk.

  1. hilbert_series: numerator, the coefficients of PΓ(t)P_\Gamma(t) of the trivial irrep from t0t^0 ([1] for a continuous group), and denominator, the exponents of the factors 1−tk1 - t^k of the denominator. These exponents are 2, then those of subduction_basis.generating_function (for a continuous group, those of its trivial family).
  2. polynomial_ring: whether the ring is a polynomial ring, generated by algebraically independent invariants.

  3. polynomials[]: one minimal generating set of the ring, by degree, with r2r^2 first in its degree. Its entries have these fields:

    1. degree and poly (a polynomial as above).
    2. primary: whether it is a primary invariant (otherwise secondary).
    3. generator: false only for r2r^2 in 𝖢𝟣\mathsf{C_{1}}, where it is generated by xx, yy, zz and is a primary invariant.
    4. factors (in 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}): the linear forms whose product is poly, by their number of terms and then by their coefficients of xx, yy, zz, descending.

The generating set follows one rule. The invariant r2r^2 is a generator unless the products of the generators of lower degree give it (in 𝖢𝟣\mathsf{C_{1}} only). For the Fischer product, distinct monomials are orthogonal and the square of a monomial is the product of the factorials of its exponents. The other generators of degree kk are a basis of the harmonic invariants of degree kk orthogonal, for the Fischer product, to the harmonic parts of the products of the generators of lower degree. This basis is the reduced row echelon basis, as for the blocks above, each vector scaled to coprime integer coordinates. In 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}} the generators of degree 6, 10 and 15 (15 in 𝖨\mathsf{I} only) are instead the products of the linear forms of the six 5-fold, ten 3-fold and fifteen 2-fold axes. Each axis is scaled so that its coordinates are integer combinations of 1 and φ\varphi, the least nonzero coordinate in absolute value is 1 and the first nonzero coordinate is positive.

The primary invariants are r2r^2 and, for a finite group, the first generators of the degrees a1a_1 and a2a_2 (section 14) with which r2r^2 has no common complex zero except the origin. The other polynomials are the secondary invariants. The primary invariants are algebraically independent. Every invariant is in one way a sum of 1 and the secondary invariants, each times a polynomial in the primary invariants (the ring is a free module over the polynomials in the primary invariants). The coefficient of tkt^k in PΓ(t)P_\Gamma(t) is the number of secondary invariants of degree kk, the constant 1 counted at k=0k = 0.

Continuous groups. A continuous group's ring is the polynomial ring in its primary invariants: zz and r2r^2 for 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}} and 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}}, r2r^2 and x2+y2−2z2x^2 + y^2 - 2z^2 for 𝖢∞𝗁\mathsf{C_{\infty h}}, 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}} and 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}}, and r2r^2 for 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}.

13.2 Tensor properties ¶

The tensor of a property is a space of cartesian_tensors or a sum of them, named by its Jahn symbol (braces an antisymmetrized product). 13.3 Selection rules ¶

An operator whose components transform as the partners of an irrep Γ2\Gamma_2 (section 10.8) joins a state of Γ1\Gamma_1 to a state of Γ\Gamma only when Γ\Gamma occurs in Γ1⊗Γ2\Gamma_1 \otimes \Gamma_2 (fusion, section 10.1). The electric dipole moment transforms as p_x, p_y, p_z (the space polar-1) and the magnetic dipole moment as Rx\mathrm{R}_x, Ry\mathrm{R}_y, Rz\mathrm{R}_z (axial-1). The polarizability of Raman scattering, a symmetric tensor of rank 2, transforms as the functions of the sets s and d (polar-2).

The vibrational ground state carries the trivial irrep. Thus one quantum of a mode of the irrep Γ\Gamma is infrared active when Γ\Gamma is an irrep of polar-1 and Raman active when it is an irrep of polar-2. Two quanta of one degenerate mode carry the symmetric square of its irrep (symmetric), and one quantum in each of two modes carries the product of their irreps (terms). In a group that contains 𝘪\mathsfit{i}, the irreps of polar-1 are u\mathrm{u} and those of polar-2 are g\mathrm{g}, so no fundamental is both infrared and Raman active.

Inside the level of a real irrep Γ\Gamma (frobenius_schur +1+1), the components of an operator even under time reversal carry the irreps of the symmetric square of Γ\Gamma (symmetric). Those of an operator odd under time reversal carry the irreps of the antisymmetric square of Γ\Gamma (antisymmetric) [Ceu22, section 3.4]. Inside the level of a pseudoreal spinor irrep (volume II) the two squares exchange [Jah38], [BC72, section 4.8]. Thus the displacements that split a degenerate orbital level of a real irrep in first order, the Jahn–Teller modes [JT37], carry the irreps of its symmetric square other than the trivial one. No displacement splits a spinor level of dimension 2 in first order.

Continuous groups. In 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} the electric dipole moment carries D(1,−)D^{(1,-)} and the magnetic dipole moment D(1,+)D^{(1,+)}. Each changes JJ by 0 or ±1\pm 1, never from 0 to 0, and the electric dipole moment also changes the parity. The electric dipole moment thus changes the JJ of one electron, whose parity is (−1)J(-1)^J, by ±1\pm 1. In 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}} it carries Σ+\Sigma^+ along zz and Π\Pi across it (section 16), Σu+\Sigma_{\mathrm{u}}^+ and Πu\Pi_{\mathrm{u}} in 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}}, so it never joins Σ+\Sigma^+ to Σ−\Sigma^-.
