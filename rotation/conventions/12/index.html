12 Subgroups, subduction and induction ¶

12.1 Embeddings ¶

An embedding, an entry of subduction_embeddings.embeddings[], is a 𝖦\mathsf{G}-conjugacy class of subgroups 𝖧′\mathsf{H}' of 𝖦\mathsf{G} (finer than 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}-conjugacy) with the admissible monomorphisms from the subgroup's record 𝖧\mathsf{H} onto its representative. Subgroups of one 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} class that are not conjugate in 𝖦\mathsf{G} are separate entries: 𝖣𝟦\mathsf{D_{4}} has two classes of 𝖣𝟤\mathsf{D_{2}}, one through the 𝘊𝟤′\mathsfit{C_{2}'} axes and one through the 𝘊𝟤′′\mathsfit{C_{2}''} axes. The embeddings are the nodes of the record's subgroup_lattice (section 15.1), with the same ids n00, n01, ….

The admissible monomorphisms are the maps 𝘩↦𝘨𝘩𝘨−1\mathsfit{h} \mapsto \mathsfit{g} \mathsfit{h} \mathsfit{g}^{-1} for the 𝘨∈𝖮(𝟥)\mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{O(3)} with 𝘨𝖧𝘨−1=𝖧′\mathsfit{g} \mathsf{H} \mathsfit{g}^{-1} = \mathsf{H}' (a rotation 𝘨\mathsfit{g} suffices, as 𝘪\mathsfit{i} commutes with every element). Two such 𝘨\mathsfit{g} give one map when they differ by an element of the centralizer 𝖢(𝖧′)\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{H}'), so there are [𝖭(𝖧′):𝖢(𝖧′)][\mathsf{N}(\mathsf{H}') : \mathsf{C}(\mathsf{H}')] maps, 𝖭\mathsf{N} and 𝖢\mathsf{C} the normalizer and the centralizer in 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}, the centralizer.normalizer_index of the subgroup's record (section 2.2). Two maps that differ by the conjugation with an element of 𝖭𝖦(𝖧′)=𝖭(𝖧′)∩𝖦\mathsf{N}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H}') = \mathsf{N}(\mathsf{H}') \cap \mathsf{G} give the same subduction table, as the matrix DΓD^\Gamma of that element carries one restriction into the other. The maps thus fall into [𝖭(𝖧′):𝖭𝖦(𝖧′) 𝖢(𝖧′)][\mathsf{N}(\mathsf{H}') : \mathsf{N}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H}')\, \mathsf{C}(\mathsf{H}')] classes, count divided by [𝖭𝖦(𝖧′):𝖢𝖦(𝖧′)][\mathsf{N}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H}') : \mathsf{C}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H}')] with 𝖢𝖦(𝖧′)=𝖢(𝖧′)∩𝖦\mathsf{C}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H}') = \mathsf{C}(\mathsf{H}') \cap \mathsf{G} (the normalizer and the centralizer of the node of the subgroup_lattice with the same id, section 15.1), and the settings of the entry are the distinct tables of these classes: the table of the stored map, and one entry of alternative_settings for each other table. The table of map composed with an automorphism of 𝖧\mathsf{H} is that of map with every irrep Λ\Lambda of 𝖧\mathsf{H} replaced by Λ\Lambda composed with that automorphism, so two settings differ by a permutation of the irreps of 𝖧\mathsf{H}, from an automorphism that a conjugation in 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} induces on 𝖧′\mathsf{H}' and none in 𝖦\mathsf{G} does. For example:

  1. 𝖢𝟥\mathsf{C_{3}} in 𝖢𝟥\mathsf{C_{3}}: [𝖣∞𝗁:𝖢∞𝗁]=2[\mathsf{D_{\infty h}} : \mathsf{C_{\infty h}}] = 2 classes, the identity and the inversion 𝘩↦𝘩−1\mathsfit{h} \mapsto \mathsfit{h}^{-1}, which exchanges 1E{}^1\mathrm{\scriptsize E} and 2E{}^2\mathrm{\scriptsize E}: two settings;
  2. each 𝖣𝟤\mathsf{D_{2}} of 𝖣𝟦\mathsf{D_{4}}: [𝖮𝗁:𝖣𝟦 𝖣𝟤𝗁]=[𝖮𝗁:𝖣𝟦𝗁]=3[\mathsf{O_{h}} : \mathsf{D_{4}}\, \mathsf{D_{2h}}] = [\mathsf{O_{h}} : \mathsf{D_{4h}}] = 3 classes, three settings, which permute B1\mathrm{\scriptsize B}_1, B2\mathrm{\scriptsize B}_2 and B3\mathrm{\scriptsize B}_3;
  3. 𝖣𝟤\mathsf{D_{2}} in 𝖳\mathsf{T}: [𝖮𝗁:𝖳 𝖣𝟤𝗁]=[𝖮𝗁:𝖳𝗁]=2[\mathsf{O_{h}} : \mathsf{T}\, \mathsf{D_{2h}}] = [\mathsf{O_{h}} : \mathsf{T_{h}}] = 2 classes and one setting, as every irrep of 𝖳\mathsf{T} restricts to A\mathrm{\scriptsize A} or to B1⊕B2⊕B3\mathrm{\scriptsize B}_1 \oplus \mathrm{\scriptsize B}_2 \oplus \mathrm{\scriptsize B}_3, which the exchange of two axes leaves as they are. Eleven embeddings have two classes and one setting: 𝖣𝟤\mathsf{D_{2}} in 𝖳\mathsf{T}, 𝖳𝗁\mathsf{T_{h}}, 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}; 𝖢𝟤𝗏\mathsf{C_{2v}} and 𝖣𝟤𝗁\mathsf{D_{2h}} in 𝖳𝗁\mathsf{T_{h}} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}; 𝖳\mathsf{T} in 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}; and 𝖳𝗁\mathsf{T_{h}} in 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}. Every other embedding has as many settings as classes.

Per entry:

  1. id; elements, the representative subgroup (ascending indices of elements.items); class_size, the number of subgroups 𝖦\mathsf{G}-conjugate to it; normal (class_size = 1); normalizer_order ∣𝖭𝖦(𝖧′)∣\lvert \mathsf{N}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H}')\rvert, 𝖧′\mathsf{H}' the representative;
  2. transversal: the left cosets 𝘨𝖧′\mathsfit{g} \mathsf{H}', each by its least element index, ascending: [𝖦:𝖧][\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{H}] indices, the first 0, the identity (section 12.4);
  3. subgroup: o3_class, the name of the record whose group is 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}-conjugate to 𝖧\mathsf{H} (never C1h, D1, D1d or D1h: 𝖢𝗌\mathsf{C_{s}} is C1v, 𝖢𝟤\mathsf{C_{2}} is C2, 𝖢𝟤𝗁\mathsf{C_{2h}} is C2h, 𝖢𝟤𝗏\mathsf{C_{2v}} is C2v); schoenflies, that record's group.schoenflies; order; index =[𝖦:𝖧]= [\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{H}];
  4. monomorphism: an admissible monomorphism maps the subgroup's record onto the representative and preserves det⁡\det and min⁡(ω,2π−ω)\min(\omega, 2\pi - \omega) of every element, hence the trace of the 3×33\times 3 matrices: it is realized by a conjugation in 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}. map[h] is the index in this record of the image of element 𝘩\mathsfit{h} of the subgroup's record; the map stored is the admissible one whose list is lexicographically least. count is the number of admissible monomorphisms onto the representative, distinct_subductions the number of distinct subduction tables they give, and determined whether that number is 1. setting lists the images of the generators of the subgroup's record (its elements in index order, each one that the earlier ones do not generate), each with h_index, h_symbol (in the subgroup's record), g_index, g_symbol and g_class (in this record);
  5. subduction, the table of the stored map (section 12.2); alternative_settings (only when determined is false), one entry per other table, with the least map that gives it, its setting and its subduction, sorted by map.

count is the number of embeddings, that is of 𝖦\mathsf{G}-conjugacy classes of subgroups, embeddings_with_alternative_settings the number with determined false, and alternative_table_count the number of alternative tables (the entries of all alternative_settings).

12.2 Subduction tables ¶

subduction[] has one entry per irrep Γ\Gamma of 𝖦\mathsf{G}, in irrep order: irrep (its id), dim, and terms, the irreps Λ\Lambda of the subgroup's record (its ids) with nonzero multiplicity ∣𝖧∣−1∑𝘩χΓ(map[𝘩]) χΛ(𝘩)∗\lvert \mathsf{H}\rvert^{-1} \sum_{\mathsfit{h}} \chi_\Gamma(\texttt{map}[\mathsfit{h}])\, \chi_\Lambda(\mathsfit{h})^*, and blocks, the intertwiner of the restriction Γ ⁣↓𝖧\Gamma\!\downarrow \mathsf{H}. A block, one per copy κ=1,2,…,bΛ\kappa = 1, 2, \dots, b_\Lambda of each Λ\Lambda in the order of terms, is the dΓ×dΛd_\Gamma \times d_\Lambda matrix BB with

(12.1)DΓ(map[𝘩]) B=B DΛ(𝘩)for every 𝘩∈𝖧,D^\Gamma(\texttt{map}[\mathsfit{h}])\, B = B\, D^\Lambda(\mathsfit{h}) \quad \text{for every } \mathsfit{h} \in \mathsf{H},

with the matrices of section 9 of the two records; as kets, ∣ μΛ;κ⟩=∑ν∣ νΓ⟩ B[ν,μ]\bigl\lvert{}^{\Lambda}_{\,\mu};\kappa\bigr\rangle = \sum_\nu \bigl\lvert{}^{\Gamma}_{\,\nu}\bigr\rangle\, B[\nu, \mu]. Side by side, the blocks of an entry form a unitary matrix, which carries DΓ(map[𝘩])D^\Gamma(\texttt{map}[\mathsfit{h}]) into the direct sum of the DΛ(𝘩)D^\Lambda(\mathsfit{h}) in that order. A block has irrep (Λ\Lambda), kappa, N and partners as in section 10.3 (B=ℰ/𝒩B = \mathcal{E}/\sqrt{\mathcal{N}} with the same phase and scale; a term of a partner is i =ν= \nu and e), and separation, "unique" when bΛ=1b_\Lambda = 1 and "projection" otherwise. The copies are fixed by projection:

  1. P1,1Λ=∑𝘩D1,1Λ(𝘩)∗ DΓ(map[𝘩])P^\Lambda_{1,1} = \sum_{\mathsfit{h}} D^\Lambda_{1,1}(\mathsfit{h})^*\, D^\Gamma(\texttt{map}[\mathsfit{h}]), a multiple of the projector on the first partners of the copies of Λ\Lambda;
  2. the columns P1,1Λe1,P1,1Λe2,…P^\Lambda_{1,1} e_1, P^\Lambda_{1,1} e_2, \dots are taken in order, and each one that is independent of the ones kept before it is kept, replaced by its part orthogonal to them; the κ\kappa-th is the seed of copy κ\kappa;
  3. column μ\mu of the block of copy κ\kappa, for every μ=1,…,dΛ\mu = 1, \dots, d_\Lambda, is ∑𝘩Dμ,1Λ(𝘩)∗ DΓ(map[𝘩])\sum_{\mathsfit{h}} D^\Lambda_{\mu,1}(\mathsfit{h})^*\, D^\Gamma(\texttt{map}[\mathsfit{h}]) times its seed (column 1 is ∣𝖧∣/dΛ\lvert \mathsf{H}\rvert / d_\Lambda times the seed), before the scale of section 10.3.

When the restricted matrices already are the direct sum of the DΛ(𝘩)D^\Lambda(\mathsfit{h}) in the order of terms, the unitary matrix of the blocks is the identity. An alternative setting has its own blocks, from its own map.

12.3 Product forms ¶

group.product_form lists every way 𝖦\mathsf{G} factors into two of its non-trivial subgroups. For finite 𝖦\mathsf{G}, from the subgroup classes of section 12.1:

  1. direct: every unordered pair of distinct normal subgroups whose orders lie strictly between 1 and ∣𝖦∣\lvert \mathsf{G}\rvert and multiply to ∣𝖦∣\lvert \mathsf{G}\rvert, and whose intersection is {𝘌}\{\mathsfit{E}\}, the first factor being the one of larger order (on equal order, the one with the smaller node index);
  2. semidirect: every pair of a normal first factor and a second factor that is not normal (the second factor up to 𝖦\mathsf{G}-conjugacy: one entry per node), with the same conditions on orders and intersection;
  3. each list is sorted by the order of the first factor, descending, then by its node index, then by the node index of the second factor;
  4. a factor is {node, o3_class, schoenflies, order, class_size, class_ids}: o3_class as in section 12.1, schoenflies the subgroup's Schoenflies symbol with C_s for 𝖢𝟣𝗏\mathsf{C_{1v}} and C_i for 𝖲𝟤\mathsf{S_{2}}, and class_ids the sorted ids of the classes of 𝖦\mathsf{G} that its elements lie in; symbol is "H x K" (direct) or "H : K" (semidirect) with those names, x and : set in the documents as ×\times and ⋊\rtimes, the semidirect product with its normal factor first ("C_3 : C_s" is 𝖢𝟥⋊𝖢𝗌\mathsf{C_{3}} \rtimes \mathsf{C_{s}});
  5. count gives the two list lengths and decomposable whether either list is non-empty;
  6. rotation_subgroup: the node whose elements are 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}, with node, o3_class, schoenflies, index =[𝖦:𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)]= [\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}], and improper_complements: the second factor of every direct form whose first factor is that node, then the first factor of every direct form whose second factor is that node, then the second factor of every semidirect form whose first factor is that node, each {node, o3_class, schoenflies, kind} (kind "direct" or "semidirect").

For a continuous 𝖦\mathsf{G} the forms are into closed subgroups, up to 𝖦\mathsf{G}-conjugacy of the pair. The first factor always contains 𝖲𝖮(𝟤)\mathsf{SO(2)} and is normal; it has index, its index in 𝖦\mathsf{G}, and no node. order is null continuous-group for an infinite factor; class_size is 1 for a normal factor and null continuous-group for a second factor with a continuum of conjugates; class_ids name the classes of 𝖦\mathsf{G} that a factor meets, an atom by its component id and a class on an arc by its distinguished-point id (section 16; a factor with class_ids is finite); the improper complements have class_ids in place of node. The lists are sorted by the first factor's index, then its name, then the second factor's name, then its class_ids.

12.4 Induction ¶

The transversal of an embedding gives the left cosets of its representative 𝖧′\mathsf{H}': 𝖦=⋃j𝘨j𝖧′\mathsf{G} = \bigcup_j \mathsfit{g}_j \mathsf{H}' with 𝘨1=𝘌,𝘨2,…,𝘨[𝖦:𝖧]\mathsfit{g}_1 = \mathsfit{E}, \mathsfit{g}_2, \dots, \mathsfit{g}_{[\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{H}]}. For an irrep Λ\Lambda of the subgroup's record and the map ϕ\phi of a setting, Ind⁡𝖧𝖦Λ\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathsf{H}}^{\mathsf{G}} \Lambda has a basis indexed by (j,ν)(j, \nu) (j=1,2,…,[𝖦:𝖧]j = 1, 2, \dots, [\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{H}], ν=1,2,…,dΛ\nu = 1, 2, \dots, d_\Lambda) and the matrices

(12.2)D(j,ν),(j′,ν′)Ind(𝘨)=[𝘨j−1𝘨 𝘨j′∈𝖧′]  Dν,ν′Λ(ϕ−1(𝘨j−1𝘨 𝘨j′)),D^{\mathrm{Ind}}_{(j,\nu),(j',\nu')}(\mathsfit{g}) = [\mathsfit{g}_j^{-1} \mathsfit{g}\, \mathsfit{g}_{j'} \in \mathsf{H}']\; D^\Lambda_{\nu,\nu'}\bigl(\phi^{-1}(\mathsfit{g}_j^{-1} \mathsfit{g}\, \mathsfit{g}_{j'})\bigr),

the products read from the multiplication table, so that DInd(𝘨) DInd(𝘨′)=DInd(𝘨𝘨′)D^{\mathrm{Ind}}(\mathsfit{g})\, D^{\mathrm{Ind}}(\mathsfit{g}') = D^{\mathrm{Ind}}(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{g}'). By Frobenius reciprocity Γ\Gamma occurs in Ind⁡𝖧𝖦Λ\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathsf{H}}^{\mathsf{G}} \Lambda as often as Λ\Lambda occurs in Γ ⁣↓𝖧\Gamma\!\downarrow \mathsf{H}: the subduction table of section 12.2 read down a column. For the block BB of copy κ\kappa of Λ\Lambda in Γ ⁣↓𝖧\Gamma\!\downarrow \mathsf{H}, the dΓd_\Gamma columns

(12.3)W(j,ν),μ=dΓ[𝖦:𝖧] dΛ  (DΓ(𝘨j) B)μ,ν∗W_{(j,\nu),\mu} = \sqrt{\frac{d_\Gamma}{[\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{H}]\, d_\Lambda}}\; \bigl(D^\Gamma(\mathsfit{g}_j)\, B\bigr)_{\mu,\nu}^*

span a copy of Γ\Gamma: DInd(𝘨) W=W DΓ(𝘨)D^{\mathrm{Ind}}(\mathsfit{g})\, W = W\, D^\Gamma(\mathsfit{g}) and W†W=1W^\dagger W = 1. Over all Γ\Gamma and κ\kappa the WW are orthonormal and fill Ind⁡𝖧𝖦Λ\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathsf{H}}^{\mathsf{G}} \Lambda. The permutation representation of 𝖦\mathsf{G} on the cosets of 𝖧′\mathsf{H}' is Ind⁡𝖧𝖦\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathsf{H}}^{\mathsf{G}} of the trivial irrep.

12.5 Permutation representations ¶

permutation_representations lists the orbits of 𝖦\mathsf{G} on the points of space, one for each class of subgroups 𝖧\mathsf{H} (a node of subgroup_lattice, section 15.1, in node order) that is the stabilizer of a point 𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0, the subgroup of the elements 𝘩\mathsfit{h} with 𝐫0 P(𝘩)=𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0\, P(\mathsfit{h}) = \mathbf{r}_0. 𝖧\mathsf{H} is a stabilizer when every element that fixes all the points that 𝖧\mathsf{H} fixes lies in 𝖧\mathsf{H}; the points whose stabilizer is 𝖧\mathsf{H} are then dense in that space, of dimension fixed_dimension (0 the origin, 1 an axis, 2 a plane, 3 all of space). Every point that another subgroup fixes has a larger stabilizer, so that the orbit of every point is one of the list up to the choice of its point, and a finite set of points that 𝖦\mathsf{G} maps to itself is a union of such orbits. The orbit has N=[𝖦:𝖧]N = [\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{H}] (degree) points

(12.4)𝐫j=𝐫0 P(ξj),j=0,1,…,N−1,\mathbf{r}_j = \mathbf{r}_0\, P(\mathsfit{\xi}_j), \qquad j = 0, 1, \dots, N - 1,

with ξj\mathsfit{\xi}_j the element of least index of the right coset 𝖧ξj\mathsf{H}\mathsfit{\xi}_j, in the order of these indices (ξ0=𝘌\mathsfit{\xi}_0 = \mathsfit{E}), as the cosets of a perm-dft irrep (section 9). The element 𝘨\mathsfit{g} moves 𝐫j\mathbf{r}_j to 𝐫j′\mathbf{r}_{j'}, where 𝖧ξj𝘨=𝖧ξj′\mathsf{H}\mathsfit{\xi}_j \mathsfit{g} = \mathsf{H}\mathsfit{\xi}_{j'}, and its permutation matrix D(𝘨)D(\mathsfit{g}), with 1 in row jj and column j′j' and 0 elsewhere,

(12.5)𝐫j P(𝘨)=𝐫j′,D(𝘨)j,j′=1,D(𝘨) D(𝘩)=D(𝘨𝘩),\mathbf{r}_j\, P(\mathsfit{g}) = \mathbf{r}_{j'}, \qquad D(\mathsfit{g})_{j,j'} = 1, \qquad D(\mathsfit{g})\, D(\mathsfit{h}) = D(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{h}),

is the matrix of 𝘨\mathsfit{g} on the vectors ∣ej⟩\lvert e_j\rangle of the points, on which an element acts as on functions (section 4.1): 𝘨 ∣ej′⟩=∣ej⟩\mathsfit{g}\, \lvert e_{j'}\rangle = \lvert e_j\rangle. It is Ind⁡𝖧𝖦\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathsf{H}}^{\mathsf{G}} of the trivial irrep (section 12.4) when the point 𝐫j\mathbf{r}_j stands for the left coset that holds the inverse of ξj\mathsfit{\xi}_j. Per orbit:

  1. subgroup_node: the node of subgroup_lattice, and the entry of subduction_embeddings (section 12.1), whose representative is 𝖧\mathsf{H};
  2. degree NN; fixed_dimension; seed, the point 𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0 (exact values, section 8), the origin when fixed_dimension is 0;
  3. cosets: the NN indices of the ξj\mathsfit{\xi}_j;
  4. images: per element, in the order of elements.items, the NN indices j′j' of the points to which it moves 𝐫0,𝐫1,…\mathbf{r}_0, \mathbf{r}_1, \dots. The images of 𝘨𝘩\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{h} are those of 𝘩\mathsfit{h} taken at the images of 𝘨\mathsfit{g}, as 𝘨𝘩\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{h} applies 𝘨\mathsfit{g} first, so that GAP's PermList(List(images, j -> j + 1)), SymPy's Permutation(images) and Wolfram's PermutationCycles[images + 1] multiply as the multiplication table;
  5. class_cycle_types: per class (section 5), the cycle type of j↦j′j \mapsto j' for its elements, [length, count] pairs by increasing length, the fixed points (cycles of length 1) included; the documents write ℓc\ell^{c} for cc cycles of length ℓ\ell;
  6. shape: what the NN points form when every point with stabilizer 𝖧\mathsf{H} forms the same, up to its size and, for a rectangle, a prism or a cuboid, its proportions; otherwise the structured null depends-on-the-point (section 8). The orbits on an axis or at the origin are a point, a segment, a regular polygon, or a tetrahedron, octahedron, cube, cuboctahedron, icosahedron, dodecahedron or icosidodecahedron, regular or uniform. Of the others, in an axial group (section 3): an orbit with as many points as the group has rotations about zz, at least 3, is a regular polygon; in a group with σ𝘩\mathsfit{\sigma_{h}}, an orbit with twice as many, at least 6, whose stabilizer does not hold σ𝘩\mathsfit{\sigma_{h}}, is a prism over a regular polygon; an orbit of four points whose two diagonals have one midpoint and one length for every point with stabilizer 𝖧\mathsf{H} is a rectangle; and in a group with the mirrors x=0x = 0, y=0y = 0 and z=0z = 0, none in 𝖧\mathsf{H}, an orbit of eight points, (±x,±y,±z)(\pm x, \pm y, \pm z), is a cuboid;
