12 Subgroups, subduction and induction ¶

12.1 Embeddings ¶

An embedding, an entry of subduction_embeddings.embeddings[], is a 𝖦\mathsf{G}-conjugacy class of subgroups 𝖧′\mathsf{H}' of 𝖦\mathsf{G} (finer than 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}-conjugacy) with the admissible monomorphisms from the subgroup's record 𝖧\mathsf{H} onto its representative. Subgroups of one 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}-conjugacy class that are not conjugate in 𝖦\mathsf{G} are separate entries: 𝖣𝟦\mathsf{D_{4}} has two classes of 𝖣𝟤\mathsf{D_{2}}, one through the 𝘊𝟤′\mathsfit{C_{2}'} axes and one through the 𝘊𝟤′′\mathsfit{C_{2}''} axes. The embeddings are the nodes of the record's subgroup_lattice (section 15.1), with the same ids n00, n01, ….

An admissible monomorphism preserves det⁡\det and min⁡(ω,2π−ω)\min(\omega, 2\pi - \omega) of every element, hence the trace of the 3×33\times 3 matrices: it is realized by a conjugation in 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}. The admissible monomorphisms are the maps 𝘩↦𝘨𝘩𝘨−1\mathsfit{h} \mapsto \mathsfit{g} \mathsfit{h} \mathsfit{g}^{-1} for the 𝘨∈𝖮(𝟥)\mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{O(3)} with 𝘨𝖧𝘨−1=𝖧′\mathsfit{g} \mathsf{H} \mathsfit{g}^{-1} = \mathsf{H}' (a rotation 𝘨\mathsfit{g} suffices, as 𝘪\mathsfit{i} commutes with every element). Two such 𝘨\mathsfit{g} give one map when they differ by an element of the centralizer 𝖢(𝖧′)\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{H}'). There are thus [𝖭(𝖧′):𝖢(𝖧′)][\mathsf{N}(\mathsf{H}') : \mathsf{C}(\mathsf{H}')] maps, the centralizer.normalizer_index of the subgroup's record (section 2.2), where 𝖭\mathsf{N} and 𝖢\mathsf{C} are the normalizer and centralizer in 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}.

Two maps that differ by conjugation with an element of 𝖭𝖦(𝖧′)=𝖭(𝖧′)∩𝖦\mathsf{N}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H}') = \mathsf{N}(\mathsf{H}') \cap \mathsf{G} give the same subduction table, as the matrix DΓD^\Gamma of that element carries one restriction into the other. The maps thus fall into [𝖭(𝖧′):𝖭𝖦(𝖧′) 𝖢(𝖧′)][\mathsf{N}(\mathsf{H}') : \mathsf{N}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H}')\, \mathsf{C}(\mathsf{H}')] classes, the number of maps (count) divided by [𝖭𝖦(𝖧′):𝖢𝖦(𝖧′)][\mathsf{N}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H}') : \mathsf{C}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H}')] with 𝖢𝖦(𝖧′)=𝖢(𝖧′)∩𝖦\mathsf{C}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H}') = \mathsf{C}(\mathsf{H}') \cap \mathsf{G} (the normalizer and the centralizer of the node with the same id, section 15.1). The settings of the entry are the distinct tables of these classes: the table of the stored map and the tables of alternative_settings. The table of map composed with an automorphism of 𝖧\mathsf{H} is that of map with every irrep Λ\Lambda of 𝖧\mathsf{H} replaced by Λ\Lambda composed with that automorphism. Two settings thus differ by a permutation of the irreps of 𝖧\mathsf{H}, from an automorphism induced on 𝖧′\mathsf{H}' by a conjugation in 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} and none in 𝖦\mathsf{G}. For example:

  1. 𝖢𝟥\mathsf{C_{3}} in 𝖢𝟥\mathsf{C_{3}}: [𝖣∞𝗁:𝖢∞𝗁]=2[\mathsf{D_{\infty h}} : \mathsf{C_{\infty h}}] = 2 classes of maps (the identity and the inversion 𝘩↦𝘩−1\mathsfit{h} \mapsto \mathsfit{h}^{-1}, which exchanges 1E{}^1\mathrm{\scriptsize E} and 2E{}^2\mathrm{\scriptsize E}), hence two settings.
  2. Each 𝖣𝟤\mathsf{D_{2}} of 𝖣𝟦\mathsf{D_{4}}: [𝖮𝗁:𝖣𝟦 𝖣𝟤𝗁]=[𝖮𝗁:𝖣𝟦𝗁]=3[\mathsf{O_{h}} : \mathsf{D_{4}}\, \mathsf{D_{2h}}] = [\mathsf{O_{h}} : \mathsf{D_{4h}}] = 3 classes of maps and three settings, which permute B1\mathrm{\scriptsize B}_1, B2\mathrm{\scriptsize B}_2 and B3\mathrm{\scriptsize B}_3.
  3. 𝖣𝟤\mathsf{D_{2}} in 𝖳\mathsf{T}: [𝖮𝗁:𝖳 𝖣𝟤𝗁]=[𝖮𝗁:𝖳𝗁]=2[\mathsf{O_{h}} : \mathsf{T}\, \mathsf{D_{2h}}] = [\mathsf{O_{h}} : \mathsf{T_{h}}] = 2 classes of maps and one setting, as every irrep of 𝖳\mathsf{T} restricts to A\mathrm{\scriptsize A} or to B1⊕B2⊕B3\mathrm{\scriptsize B}_1 \oplus \mathrm{\scriptsize B}_2 \oplus \mathrm{\scriptsize B}_3, which the exchange of two axes leaves unchanged.

Eleven embeddings have two classes of maps and one setting:

  1. 𝖣𝟤\mathsf{D_{2}} in 𝖳\mathsf{T}, 𝖳𝗁\mathsf{T_{h}}, 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}.
  2. 𝖢𝟤𝗏\mathsf{C_{2v}} and 𝖣𝟤𝗁\mathsf{D_{2h}} in 𝖳𝗁\mathsf{T_{h}} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}.
  3. 𝖳\mathsf{T} in 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}.
  4. 𝖳𝗁\mathsf{T_{h}} in 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}.

Every other embedding has as many classes of maps as settings.

Each embedding has these fields:

  1. id.
  2. elements: the representative subgroup (ascending indices of elements.items).
  3. class_size: the number of 𝖦\mathsf{G}-conjugates of the representative.
  4. normal: whether class_size is 1.
  5. normalizer_order: ∣𝖭𝖦(𝖧′)∣\lvert \mathsf{N}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mathsf{H}')\rvert, 𝖧′\mathsf{H}' the representative.
  6. transversal: the left cosets 𝘨𝖧′\mathsfit{g} \mathsf{H}', each by its least element index, ascending: [𝖦:𝖧][\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{H}] indices, the first 0, the identity (section 12.4).

  7. subgroup: 𝖧\mathsf{H}, with these fields:

    1. o3_class: the name of the record whose group is 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}-conjugate to 𝖧\mathsf{H} (never C1h, D1, D1d or D1h: 𝖢𝗌\mathsf{C_{s}} is C1v, 𝖢𝟤\mathsf{C_{2}} is C2, 𝖢𝟤𝗁\mathsf{C_{2h}} is C2h, 𝖢𝟤𝗏\mathsf{C_{2v}} is C2v).
    2. schoenflies: that record's group.schoenflies.
    3. order.
    4. index =[𝖦:𝖧]= [\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{H}].

  8. monomorphism: the admissible monomorphisms, with these fields:

    1. count: their number.
    2. distinct_subductions: the number of their distinct subduction tables.
    3. determined: whether distinct_subductions is 1.
    4. map: the stored map, the admissible one whose list is lexicographically least. Its entry map[h] is the index in this record of the image of element 𝘩\mathsfit{h} of the subgroup's record.
    5. setting: the images of the generators of the subgroup's record (its elements in index order not generated by the earlier ones). Each has h_index, h_symbol (in the subgroup's record), g_index, g_symbol and g_class (in this record).
  9. subduction: the table of the stored map (section 12.2).
  10. alternative_settings (only when determined is false): one entry for each other table, with the least map that gives it, its setting and its subduction, sorted by map.

Beside subduction_embeddings.embeddings[] are these counts:

  1. count: the number of embeddings, that is, of 𝖦\mathsf{G}-conjugacy classes of subgroups.
  2. embeddings_with_alternative_settings: the number with determined false.
  3. alternative_table_count: the number of alternative tables (the entries of all alternative_settings).

12.2 Subduction tables ¶

For each irrep Γ\Gamma of 𝖦\mathsf{G}, in irrep order, the field subduction[] has an entry with irrep (its id), dim and terms, the irreps Λ\Lambda of the subgroup's record (their ids) with nonzero multiplicity ∣𝖧∣−1∑𝘩χΓ(map[𝘩]) χΛ(𝘩)∗\lvert \mathsf{H}\rvert^{-1} \sum_{\mathsfit{h}} \chi_\Gamma(\texttt{map}[\mathsfit{h}])\, \chi_\Lambda(\mathsfit{h})^*. The entry's blocks are the intertwiner of the restriction Γ ⁣↓𝖧\Gamma\!\downarrow \mathsf{H}, one for each copy κ=1,2,…,bΛ\kappa = 1, 2, \dots, b_\Lambda of each Λ\Lambda in the order of terms. A block is the dΓ×dΛd_\Gamma \times d_\Lambda matrix BB with

(12.1)DΓ(map[𝘩]) B=B DΛ(𝘩)for every 𝘩∈𝖧,D^\Gamma(\texttt{map}[\mathsfit{h}])\, B = B\, D^\Lambda(\mathsfit{h}) \quad \text{for every } \mathsfit{h} \in \mathsf{H},

with the matrices of section 9 of the two records. As kets, ∣ μΛ;κ⟩=∑ν∣ νΓ⟩ B[ν,μ]\bigl\lvert{}^{\Lambda}_{\,\mu};\kappa\bigr\rangle = \sum_\nu \bigl\lvert{}^{\Gamma}_{\,\nu}\bigr\rangle\, B[\nu, \mu]. Side by side, the blocks of an entry form a unitary matrix, which carries DΓ(map[𝘩])D^\Gamma(\texttt{map}[\mathsfit{h}]) into the direct sum of the DΛ(𝘩)D^\Lambda(\mathsfit{h}) in that order. A block has irrep (Λ\Lambda), kappa, N and partners as in section 10.3, and separation, "unique" when bΛ=1b_\Lambda = 1 and "projection" otherwise. For every block, B=ℰ/𝒩B = \mathcal{E}/\sqrt{\mathcal{N}} with the phase and scale of section 10.3, and a partner's term has i =ν= \nu and e. The copies are fixed by projection:

  1. The matrix P1,1Λ=∑𝘩D1,1Λ(𝘩)∗ DΓ(map[𝘩])P^\Lambda_{1,1} = \sum_{\mathsfit{h}} D^\Lambda_{1,1}(\mathsfit{h})^*\, D^\Gamma(\texttt{map}[\mathsfit{h}]) is a multiple of the projector on the first partners of the copies of Λ\Lambda.
  2. The columns P1,1Λe1,P1,1Λe2,…P^\Lambda_{1,1} e_1, P^\Lambda_{1,1} e_2, \dots are taken in order. Each column independent of those kept before it is kept, replaced by its part orthogonal to them. The κ\kappa-th column kept is the seed of copy κ\kappa.
  3. For every μ=1,…,dΛ\mu = 1, \dots, d_\Lambda, column μ\mu of the block of copy κ\kappa is ∑𝘩Dμ,1Λ(𝘩)∗ DΓ(map[𝘩])\sum_{\mathsfit{h}} D^\Lambda_{\mu,1}(\mathsfit{h})^*\, D^\Gamma(\texttt{map}[\mathsfit{h}]) times its seed (column 1 is ∣𝖧∣/dΛ\lvert \mathsf{H}\rvert / d_\Lambda times the seed), before the scale of section 10.3.

When the restricted matrices are already the direct sum of the DΛ(𝘩)D^\Lambda(\mathsfit{h}) in the order of terms, the unitary matrix of the blocks is the identity. An alternative setting has its own blocks, from its own map.

12.3 Product forms ¶

The field group.product_form lists every way 𝖦\mathsf{G} factors into two of its nontrivial subgroups. For a finite 𝖦\mathsf{G} it has these fields, from the classes of subgroups of section 12.1:

  1. direct: every unordered pair of distinct normal subgroups whose orders lie strictly between 1 and ∣𝖦∣\lvert \mathsf{G}\rvert and multiply to ∣𝖦∣\lvert \mathsf{G}\rvert, and whose intersection is {𝘌}\{\mathsfit{E}\}. The first factor is the one of larger order (on equal order, the one with the smaller node index).
  2. semidirect: every pair of a normal first factor and a second factor that is not normal (up to 𝖦\mathsf{G}-conjugacy, one entry for each node), with the same conditions on orders and intersection.
  3. count: the two list lengths.
  4. decomposable: whether either list is nonempty.

For a finite 𝖦\mathsf{G}, both lists are sorted by the order of the first factor, descending, then by its node index, then by the node index of the second factor. A factor of these lists is {node, o3_class, schoenflies, order, class_size, class_ids}, where o3_class is as in section 12.1 and class_ids are the sorted ids of the classes of 𝖦\mathsf{G} that contain its elements. The factor's schoenflies is its Schoenflies symbol, with C_s for 𝖢𝟣𝗏\mathsf{C_{1v}} and C_i for 𝖲𝟤\mathsf{S_{2}}, and a form's symbol is "H x K" (direct) or "H : K" (semidirect) with those names. The documents set x and : as ×\times and ⋊\rtimes, the semidirect product with its normal factor first ("C_3 : C_s" is 𝖢𝟥⋊𝖢𝗌\mathsf{C_{3}} \rtimes \mathsf{C_{s}}).

For a finite 𝖦\mathsf{G}, the rotation_subgroup of group.product_form is the node whose elements are 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}, with node, o3_class, schoenflies and index =[𝖦:𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)]= [\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}]. Its improper_complements lists these factors, each {node, o3_class, schoenflies, kind} (kind "direct" or "semidirect"), in this order:

  1. the second factor of every direct form whose first factor is that node.
  2. the first factor of every direct form whose second factor is that node.
  3. the second factor of every semidirect form whose first factor is that node.

Continuous groups. For a continuous 𝖦\mathsf{G} the forms are into closed subgroups, up to 𝖦\mathsf{G}-conjugacy of the pair. The first factor always contains 𝖲𝖮(𝟤)\mathsf{SO(2)}, is normal and has index, its index in 𝖦\mathsf{G}, and no node. A factor has these values:

  1. order: null continuous-group for an infinite factor.
  2. class_size: 1 for a normal factor and null continuous-group for a second factor with a continuum of conjugates.
  3. class_ids: the classes of 𝖦\mathsf{G} that a factor meets, an atom by its component id and a class on an arc by its distinguished-point id (section 16). Every factor has class_ids, no factor and no rotation_subgroup has a node, and the improper complements have class_ids in place of node.

For a continuous 𝖦\mathsf{G}, the lists are sorted by the first factor's index, then its name, then the second factor's name, then its class_ids.

12.4 Induction ¶

The transversal of an embedding gives the left cosets of its representative 𝖧′\mathsf{H}': 𝖦=⋃j𝘨j𝖧′\mathsf{G} = \bigcup_j \mathsfit{g}_j \mathsf{H}' with 𝘨1=𝘌,𝘨2,…,𝘨[𝖦:𝖧]\mathsfit{g}_1 = \mathsfit{E}, \mathsfit{g}_2, \dots, \mathsfit{g}_{[\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{H}]}. For an irrep Λ\Lambda of the subgroup's record and the map ϕ\phi of a setting, Ind⁡𝖧𝖦Λ\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathsf{H}}^{\mathsf{G}} \Lambda has a basis indexed by (j,ν)(j, \nu) (j=1,2,…,[𝖦:𝖧]j = 1, 2, \dots, [\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{H}], ν=1,2,…,dΛ\nu = 1, 2, \dots, d_\Lambda) and the matrices

(12.2)D(j,ν),(j′,ν′)Ind(𝘨)=[𝘨j−1𝘨 𝘨j′∈𝖧′]  Dν,ν′Λ(ϕ−1(𝘨j−1𝘨 𝘨j′)),D^{\mathrm{Ind}}_{(j,\nu),(j',\nu')}(\mathsfit{g}) = [\mathsfit{g}_j^{-1} \mathsfit{g}\, \mathsfit{g}_{j'} \in \mathsf{H}']\; D^\Lambda_{\nu,\nu'}\bigl(\phi^{-1}(\mathsfit{g}_j^{-1} \mathsfit{g}\, \mathsfit{g}_{j'})\bigr),

the products read from the multiplication table, so that DInd(𝘨) DInd(𝘨′)=DInd(𝘨𝘨′)D^{\mathrm{Ind}}(\mathsfit{g})\, D^{\mathrm{Ind}}(\mathsfit{g}') = D^{\mathrm{Ind}}(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{g}'). By Frobenius reciprocity Γ\Gamma occurs in Ind⁡𝖧𝖦Λ\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathsf{H}}^{\mathsf{G}} \Lambda as often as Λ\Lambda occurs in Γ ⁣↓𝖧\Gamma\!\downarrow \mathsf{H}: the subduction table of section 12.2 read down a column. For the block BB of copy κ\kappa of Λ\Lambda in Γ ⁣↓𝖧\Gamma\!\downarrow \mathsf{H}, the dΓd_\Gamma columns

(12.3)W(j,ν),μ=dΓ[𝖦:𝖧] dΛ  (DΓ(𝘨j) B)μ,ν∗W_{(j,\nu),\mu} = \sqrt{\frac{d_\Gamma}{[\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{H}]\, d_\Lambda}}\; \bigl(D^\Gamma(\mathsfit{g}_j)\, B\bigr)_{\mu,\nu}^*

span a copy of Γ\Gamma: DInd(𝘨) W=W DΓ(𝘨)D^{\mathrm{Ind}}(\mathsfit{g})\, W = W\, D^\Gamma(\mathsfit{g}) and W†W=1W^\dagger W = 1. Over all Γ\Gamma and κ\kappa the WW are orthonormal and fill Ind⁡𝖧𝖦Λ\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathsf{H}}^{\mathsf{G}} \Lambda. The permutation representation of 𝖦\mathsf{G} on the cosets of 𝖧′\mathsf{H}' is Ind⁡𝖧𝖦\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathsf{H}}^{\mathsf{G}} of the trivial irrep.

12.5 Permutation representations ¶

The field actions of permutation_representations lists the orbits of 𝖦\mathsf{G} on the points of space. It has one for each class of subgroups 𝖧\mathsf{H} (a node of subgroup_lattice, section 15.1, in node order) that is the stabilizer of a point 𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0, the subgroup of the elements 𝘩\mathsfit{h} with 𝐫0 P(𝘩)=𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0\, P(\mathsfit{h}) = \mathbf{r}_0. The subgroup 𝖧\mathsf{H} is a stabilizer when every element that fixes all the fixed points of 𝖧\mathsf{H} lies in 𝖧\mathsf{H}. These fixed points form a space of dimension fixed_dimension (0 the origin, 1 an axis, 2 a plane, 3 all of space), and the points whose stabilizer is 𝖧\mathsf{H} are then dense in it. Every point that another subgroup fixes has a larger stabilizer, so every orbit is one of the list, up to the choice of its point. Thus a finite set of points that 𝖦\mathsf{G} maps to itself is a union of such orbits. The orbit has N=[𝖦:𝖧]N = [\mathsf{G} : \mathsf{H}] (degree) points

(12.4)𝐫j=𝐫0 P(ξj),j=0,1,…,N−1,\mathbf{r}_j = \mathbf{r}_0\, P(\mathsfit{\xi}_j), \qquad j = 0, 1, \dots, N - 1,

where ξj\mathsfit{\xi}_j is the element of least index of the right coset 𝖧ξj\mathsf{H}\mathsfit{\xi}_j, in the order of these indices (ξ0=𝘌\mathsfit{\xi}_0 = \mathsfit{E}), as the cosets of a perm-dft irrep (section 9). The element 𝘨\mathsfit{g} moves 𝐫j\mathbf{r}_j to 𝐫j′\mathbf{r}_{j'}, where 𝖧ξj𝘨=𝖧ξj′\mathsf{H}\mathsfit{\xi}_j \mathsfit{g} = \mathsf{H}\mathsfit{\xi}_{j'}. Its permutation matrix D(𝘨)D(\mathsfit{g}), with 1 in row jj and column j′j' and 0 elsewhere,

(12.5)𝐫j P(𝘨)=𝐫j′,D(𝘨)j,j′=1,D(𝘨) D(𝘩)=D(𝘨𝘩),\mathbf{r}_j\, P(\mathsfit{g}) = \mathbf{r}_{j'}, \qquad D(\mathsfit{g})_{j,j'} = 1, \qquad D(\mathsfit{g})\, D(\mathsfit{h}) = D(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{h}),

is the matrix of 𝘨\mathsfit{g} on the vectors ∣ej⟩\lvert e_j\rangle of the points, on which an element acts as on functions (section 4.1): 𝘨 ∣ej′⟩=∣ej⟩\mathsfit{g}\, \lvert e_{j'}\rangle = \lvert e_j\rangle. The representation DD is Ind⁡𝖧𝖦\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathsf{H}}^{\mathsf{G}} of the trivial irrep (section 12.4) when the point 𝐫j\mathbf{r}_j stands for the left coset of the inverse of ξj\mathsfit{\xi}_j. Each orbit has these fields:

  1. subgroup_node: the node of subgroup_lattice, and the entry of subduction_embeddings (section 12.1), whose representative is 𝖧\mathsf{H}.
  2. degree (NN) and fixed_dimension.
  3. seed: the point 𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0 (exact values, section 8), the origin when fixed_dimension is 0.
  4. cosets: the NN indices of the ξj\mathsfit{\xi}_j.
  5. images: for each element, in the order of elements.items, the NN indices j′j' of the points to which it moves 𝐫0,𝐫1,…\mathbf{r}_0, \mathbf{r}_1, \dots. The images of 𝘨𝘩\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{h} are those of 𝘩\mathsfit{h} taken at the images of 𝘨\mathsfit{g}, as 𝘨𝘩\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{h} applies 𝘨\mathsfit{g} first. Thus GAP's PermList(List(images, j -> j + 1)), SymPy's Permutation(images) and Wolfram's PermutationCycles[images + 1] multiply as the multiplication table.
  6. class_cycle_types: for each class (section 5), the cycle type of j↦j′j \mapsto j' for its elements, [length, count] pairs by increasing length, including the fixed points (cycles of length 1). The documents write ℓc\ell^{c} for cc cycles of length ℓ\ell.

  7. shape: what the NN points form when every point with stabilizer 𝖧\mathsf{H} forms the same, up to its size and, for a rectangle, a prism or a cuboid, its proportions. Otherwise it is null depends-on-the-point (section 8).

    The orbits on an axis or at the origin are a point, a segment, a regular polygon, or a tetrahedron, octahedron, cube, cuboctahedron, icosahedron, dodecahedron or icosidodecahedron, regular or uniform. Of the others, in an axial group (section 3):

    1. an orbit with as many points as the group has rotations about zz, at least 3, is a regular polygon.
    2. in a group with σ𝘩\mathsfit{\sigma_{h}}, an orbit with twice as many, at least 6, whose stabilizer does not contain σ𝘩\mathsfit{\sigma_{h}}, is a prism over a regular polygon.
    3. an orbit of four points whose two diagonals have one midpoint and one length for every point with stabilizer 𝖧\mathsf{H} is a rectangle.
    4. in a group with the mirrors x=0x = 0, y=0y = 0 and z=0z = 0, none in 𝖧\mathsf{H}, an orbit of eight points, (±x,±y,±z)(\pm x, \pm y, \pm z), is a cuboid.

  8. uniform_shapes: the uniform polyhedra (with regular faces and all vertices alike) and the regular polygons that the orbit of some point with stabilizer 𝖧\mathsf{H} forms, up to its size, in this order:

    1. regular polygon (of NN sides).
    2. the Platonic solids tetrahedron, octahedron, cube, icosahedron, dodecahedron.
    3. prism and antiprism, over a regular polygon of N/2N/2 sides, with square faces and with equilateral triangles.
    4. the Archimedean solids truncated tetrahedron, cuboctahedron, truncated cube, truncated octahedron, rhombicuboctahedron, truncated cuboctahedron, snub cube, icosidodecahedron, truncated dodecahedron, truncated icosahedron, rhombicosidodecahedron, truncated icosidodecahedron and snub dodecahedron.

    An orbit whose shape is uniform has it as its one uniform shape. A rectangle has a square among its uniform shapes, a prism has a uniform prism and a cuboid has a cube. In 𝖳\mathsf{T}, 𝖳𝗁\mathsf{T_{h}}, 𝖳𝖽\mathsf{T_{d}}, 𝖮\mathsf{O}, 𝖮𝗁\mathsf{O_{h}}, 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}} a solid is an orbit when its symmetry group, one of these groups in the frame of section 3, contains 𝖦\mathsf{G} and 𝖦\mathsf{G} moves a vertex to every vertex. The vertex then has a stabilizer of class 𝖧\mathsf{H}.

    In an axial group the orbit of a point off the axis zz lies on one plane perpendicular to zz or on two, at opposite heights. It is uniform in these cases:

    1. a regular polygon when its points lie on one plane at evenly spaced azimuths.
    2. a uniform prism (a cube) or antiprism (an octahedron), for one height, when the two planes contain regular polygons of at least three points, with the same azimuths or turned by half a side.
    3. a tetrahedron, with two points on each plane, when each pair is a diameter and the two are perpendicular.
    4. a square, with two points on each plane, when the two pairs have the same azimuths, or opposite ones with the two azimuths of a pair more than a quarter turn apart.

The character χ(𝘨)\chi(\mathsfit{g}) of DD is the number of points fixed by 𝘨\mathsfit{g}. The multiplicity of Γ\Gamma in DD is 1∣𝖦∣∑𝘨χ(𝘨) χΓ(𝘨)∗\frac{1}{\lvert\mathsf{G}\rvert} \sum_{\mathsfit{g}} \chi(\mathsfit{g})\, \chi_\Gamma(\mathsfit{g})^*, equal to that of the trivial irrep of 𝖧\mathsf{H} in Γ ⁣↓𝖧\Gamma\!\downarrow \mathsf{H} (section 12.4). This multiplicity is the dimension of the space of the vectors of Γ\Gamma that 𝖧\mathsf{H} fixes. Thus some nonzero vector of Γ\Gamma has the stabilizer 𝖧\mathsf{H} exactly when Γ\Gamma occurs in the permutation representation of 𝖧\mathsf{H}, and more often than in that of every class of subgroups that contain 𝖧\mathsf{H} properly. The permutation representation of 𝖧\mathsf{H} is that of its orbit, or of its figure of section 12.6. For a nontrivial Γ\Gamma these stabilizers are its kernel and its epikernels [CV89], the symmetries that a distortion of symmetry Γ\Gamma can leave. The number of cycles of 𝘨\mathsfit{g} is c(𝘨)c(\mathsfit{g}), and the qNq^N products of one of qq states of each point (one nuclear spin state of each atom, for instance) carry the character qc(𝘨)q^{c(\mathsfit{g})}. In this character Γ\Gamma has the multiplicity 1∣𝖦∣∑𝘨qc(𝘨) χΓ(𝘨)∗\frac{1}{\lvert\mathsf{G}\rvert} \sum_{\mathsfit{g}} q^{c(\mathsfit{g})}\, \chi_\Gamma(\mathsfit{g})^*, which for the trivial irrep is the number of colorings of the points with qq colors up to 𝖦\mathsf{G}. The displacements of the points, a vector at each, carry the character χ(𝘨) tr⁡P(𝘨)\chi(\mathsfit{g})\, \operatorname{tr} P(\mathsfit{g}).

The atoms of a molecule lie on orbits of its point group, and the orbit of a point of stabilizer 𝖧\mathsf{H} carries the representation DD of the entry of 𝖧\mathsf{H} in actions. The displacements of all the atoms carry the sum of the characters χ(𝘨) tr⁡P(𝘨)\chi(\mathsfit{g})\, \operatorname{tr} P(\mathsfit{g}) of their orbits. The vibrations carry this sum minus the characters tr⁡P(𝘨)\operatorname{tr} P(\mathsfit{g}) of the translations and (det⁡𝘨) tr⁡P(𝘨)(\det \mathsfit{g})\, \operatorname{tr} P(\mathsfit{g}) of the rotations, whose irreps are those of p_x, p_y, p_z and of Rx\mathrm{R}_x, Ry\mathrm{R}_y, Rz\mathrm{R}_z (section 13). A linear molecule, whose atoms lie on its axis, has the rotations Rx\mathrm{R}_x and Ry\mathrm{R}_y only. The stretches of the bonds from an atom at the origin to the points of one orbit carry the representation DD of that orbit.

A function ff evaluated at the points of an orbit gives the vector ∑jf(𝐫j) ∣ej⟩\sum_j f(\mathbf{r}_j)\, \lvert e_j\rangle, on which an element acts as on ff: 𝘨\mathsfit{g} carries it to the vector of 𝘨f\mathsfit{g} f (section 4.1). Thus functions that transform as the partners of an irrep Γ\Gamma, such as a block of section 14, give the partners of a copy of Γ\Gamma in DD unless all their values are zero. These vectors are the symmetry-adapted combinations of the points. Hybrid orbitals directed at the points are built from an orthonormal basis of DD made of such combinations and from orthonormal orbitals that transform as these combinations do, one orbital for each. The polynomials of section 13 are not normalized: on the sphere, d_z2 has 3\sqrt{3} times the norm of d_x2-y2. The hybrid of the point 𝐫j\mathbf{r}_j is the sum of these orbitals, each times the conjugate of the coefficient of ∣ej⟩\lvert e_j\rangle in its combination. The NN hybrids are orthonormal, and 𝘨\mathsfit{g} carries the hybrid of 𝐫j′\mathbf{r}_{j'} to the hybrid of 𝐫j\mathbf{r}_j as it carries ∣ej′⟩\lvert e_{j'}\rangle to ∣ej⟩\lvert e_j\rangle. In this way the orbitals s, p_x, p_y, p_z give the hybrids of the tetrahedron of 𝖳𝖽\mathsf{T_{d}} (A1⊕T2\mathrm{\scriptsize A}_1 \oplus \mathrm{\scriptsize T}_2), and the orbitals s, p_x, p_y, p_z, d_z2, d_x2-y2 the hybrids of the octahedron of 𝖮𝗁\mathsf{O_{h}} (A1g⊕Eg⊕T1u\mathrm{\scriptsize A}_{1\mathrm{g}} \oplus \mathrm{\scriptsize E}_{\mathrm{g}} \oplus \mathrm{\scriptsize T}_{1\mathrm{u}}).

This stabilizer is the subgroup of the elements carrying the figure into itself. The copy jj is the figure moved by ξj\mathsfit{\xi}_j. The first five kinds, the directions, are the objects whose own symmetry is 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}}, 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}}, 𝖢∞𝗁\mathsf{C_{\infty h}}, 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}} and 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}}. The subgroup 𝖧\mathsf{H} is the stabilizer of one exactly when it is the intersection of 𝖦\mathsf{G} with a conjugate of that group. The spinning arrow and the helix are chiral and tried only when 𝖧\mathsf{H} has no improper element and 𝖦\mathsf{G} has one. A direction is sought in the space of the vectors that 𝖧\mathsf{H} fixes in the way of its kind (for a line or a helix, one space for each homomorphism of 𝖧\mathsf{H} onto the signs). It is the sum of the basis vectors with the coefficients 1,t,t21, t, t^2, for the least integer t≥2t \ge 2 that gives the stabilizer 𝖧\mathsf{H}. A space of dimension one gives its basis vector, in coprime integers when it is rational.

The last kind is the least orbit of a point under 𝖧\mathsf{H} whose stabilizer is 𝖧\mathsf{H}. A point in general position gives one, so every class has a figure. The point of an orbit is sought on the rotation axes of 𝖦\mathsf{G}, then in the planes of its mirrors and those perpendicular to its 2-fold axes, then in general position. The search keeps the least orbit, the first among orbits of one size.

Each entry has these fields:

  1. subgroup_node, degree, cosets, images and class_cycle_types: as in section 12.5.
  2. figure: kind, vector (exact values), points and shape for an orbit, and orbit (an index of actions) for a point.
  3. site_node: the class of the stabilizer of the point 𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0, which tells the symmetry elements of 𝖦\mathsf{G} that the figure lies on.
  4. vertices: the points of all the copies, exact. These are the points, the tips of the spins and the spinning arrows (that of a spin (det⁡ξj) 𝐫0 P(ξj)(\det \mathsfit{\xi}_j)\, \mathbf{r}_0\, P(\mathsfit{\xi}_j)), both ends of the lines and the helices, and the points of the orbits.
  5. copies: for each copy, the indices of its vertices.
  6. copies_per_vertex.
  7. vertex_shape: the shape of the vertices.
  8. rank, pair_classes: the orbits of 𝖧\mathsf{H} on the copies, which are the classes of pairs of copies under 𝖦\mathsf{G} (the copy 0 with the copy copy). Each has its size, shared_vertices for an orbit, cos for a direction and same_hand for the chiral kinds. The cosine cos of the angle between the two is 𝐫0⋅𝐫0 P(ξj)/𝐫0⋅𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0 \cdot \mathbf{r}_0\, P(\mathsfit{\xi}_j) / \mathbf{r}_0 \cdot \mathbf{r}_0, with the factor det⁡ξj\det \mathsfit{\xi}_j for a spin. For a line or a helix, cos is the absolute value of this quotient.
  9. hands: the copies whose ξj\mathsfit{\xi}_j is a rotation, then the others, when 𝖧\mathsf{H} has no improper element and 𝖦\mathsf{G} has one. The rotations of 𝖦\mathsf{G} leave the copies in these two classes, mirror images of each other, and every improper element exchanges them. Otherwise hands is null one-class-under-the-rotations.
  10. compound: the regular compound that the copies form, with name and uc, its number in the complete list of the uniform compounds [Ski76]. It is compound of two tetrahedra (UC04), compound of five tetrahedra (UC05), compound of ten tetrahedra (UC06), compound of five cubes (UC09) or compound of five octahedra (UC17), otherwise null not-a-regular-compound.

A shape and a vertex_shape are a segment, a disphenoid (four points whose opposite edges are equal in pairs) or a name of section 12.5 that the points form exactly. Such a name is a regular polygon, a rectangle, a regular polyhedron, a cuboctahedron or an icosidodecahedron with regular faces of one side, or a cuboid. Otherwise each is null not-a-named-shape.

The field marks is the table of marks. Its entry rows[i][k] is the number of copies of the figure of the class columns[i] that the representative of the class columns[k] leaves in place, each carried into itself by all its elements. It is nonzero only when that subgroup lies in a conjugate of the first. The mark of a class at itself is the index of 𝖧\mathsf{H} in its normalizer, and the mark at a cyclic subgroup is the number of copies fixed by its generator.
