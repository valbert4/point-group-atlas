14 Symmetry-adapted bases ¶

The kets ∣ mJ⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle are the spherical harmonics, with the action of section 4: the inversion acts as (−1)J(-1)^J, and an element 𝘨\mathsfit{g} acts as (det⁡𝘨)J(\det \mathsfit{g})^J times its rotation part (det⁡𝘨) 𝘨(\det \mathsfit{g})\, \mathsfit{g}. A basis vector is written by its coefficients eme_m:

(14.1)v=tot−1/2∑memFm ∣ mJ⟩,Fm=(J+m)! (J−m)!,tot=∑m∣em∣2Fm,v = \mathrm{tot}^{-1/2} \sum_m e_m \sqrt{F_m}\, \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle, \qquad F_m = (J + m)!\,(J - m)!, \qquad \mathrm{tot} = \sum_m \lvert e_m\rvert^2 F_m,

so that the amplitude of ∣ mJ⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle is emFm/tote_m \sqrt{F_m/\mathrm{tot}} and ∑m∣em∣2Fm/tot=1\sum_m \lvert e_m\rvert^2 F_m/\mathrm{tot} = 1. A positive rational factor of the eme_m of a vector leaves it unchanged. A block is the dd partners v1,…,vdv_1, \dots, v_d (μ=\mu = mu =1,2,…,d= 1, 2, \dots, d) of one copy of an irrep Γ\Gamma of dimension dd, and obeys the partner law

(14.2)𝘨 vμ=∑νvν Dν,μΓ(𝘨)for every element 𝘨,\mathsfit{g}\, v_\mu = \sum_\nu v_\nu\, D^\Gamma_{\nu,\mu}(\mathsfit{g}) \qquad \text{for every element } \mathsfit{g},

where DΓD^\Gamma are the matrices of section 9. The index κ\kappa (kappa, or copy in the bases files) numbers the copies of Γ\Gamma at one JJ. In 𝖳𝗁\mathsf{T_{h}}, 𝖮𝗁\mathsf{O_{h}}, 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}} and the other groups containing 𝘪\mathsfit{i}, the irreps are g\mathrm{g} at even JJ and u\mathrm{u} at odd JJ.

Time reversal Θ\varTheta acts on a function by complex conjugation (section 31.1), and on the kets as Θ∣ mJ⟩=(−1)m∣ −mJ⟩\varTheta \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle = (-1)^m \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,-m}\bigr\rangle. It replaces each coefficient eme_m of a vector by (−1)m(-1)^m times the conjugate of e−me_{-m} and keeps its tot\mathrm{tot}. Every block of the bases of this catalogue, in its records and in its files, is adapted to time reversal in one of two ways:

  1. A block of a real irrep Γ\Gamma (section 7) has Θvμ=∑νvνNν,μ\varTheta v_\mu = \sum_\nu v_\nu N_{\nu,\mu}, with the matrix NN of section 32.2. In a finite group it is d\sqrt{d} times the block of Γ⊗Γ\Gamma \otimes \Gamma into the trivial irrep (section 10.3), with its row (μ1,μ2)(\mu_1, \mu_2) as row μ1\mu_1 and column μ2\mu_2. In a continuous group it is that of section 37.2, on the kets of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} the matrix of Θ\varTheta itself.
  2. The block of the second irrep of a complex pair (section 6.4) at JJ and κ\kappa is Θ\varTheta applied to the block of the first irrep at the same JJ and κ\kappa, partner by partner.

The first nonzero coefficient of partner 1, in the order of its components, has a positive real part or is i\mathrm{i} times a positive number (the phase form of section 38.1). This form holds in the blocks of the real irreps and of the first irreps of the complex pairs.

Every subduction_basis holds two summaries of the multiplicities bΓ(J)=bΓ(J,(−1)J)b_\Gamma(J) = b_\Gamma(J, (-1)^J) (section 11), the number of blocks of Γ\Gamma at JJ:

  1. generating_function: for a finite group, HΓ(t)=∑J≥0bΓ(J) tJ=PΓ(t)/((1−ta1)(1−ta2))H_\Gamma(t) = \sum_{J\ge0} b_\Gamma(J)\, t^J = P_\Gamma(t)/((1 - t^{a_1})(1 - t^{a_2})), with denominator [a1,a2][a_1, a_2] and numerators, one entry for each irrep (irrep its label, irrep_id its id, in irrep order) whose coefficients are the coefficients of PΓP_\Gamma from t0t^0. Here a1≤a2a_1 \le a_2 is the pair of divisors of 2L2L, L=lcm⁡(period,2)L = \operatorname{lcm}(\texttt{period}, 2), with the least a1a2a_1 a_2, then the least a1a_1, for which every PΓP_\Gamma is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients.

    For a continuous group, families has one entry for each irrep family (family, label_template). Each entry has numerator, a formula in tt and the family index nn (t^n, t^(2*ceil(n/2)), t^(2*floor(n/2)+1), 1, t or 0), and denominator, the exponents dd of the factors (1−td)(1 - t^d). In 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}, whose family index is JJ, numerator is t^J, t^J [J even] or t^J [J odd] ([...] is 1 when the condition is true and 0 otherwise). Their denominator is []: the instance JJ occurs only at angular momentum JJ.

  2. allowed_m: for a finite group, the elements that map the zz axis to itself and act on the xyxy plane as a rotation form the subgroup 𝖠=𝖦∩𝖢∞𝗁\mathsf{A} = \mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{C_{\infty h}}. With w=P11+iP12w = P_{11} + \mathrm{i}P_{12} and sz=P33s_z = P_{33}, 𝘨∈𝖠\mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{A} acts on ∣ mJ⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle as wmw^m (sz=+1s_z = +1) or (−1)J+mwm(-1)^{J+m} w^m (sz=−1s_z = -1). The modulus KK is the least modulus for which these eigenvalues depend on mm only through m mod Km \bmod K. For each irrep (irrep, irrep_id), residues gives the residues q mod Kq \bmod K at even and at odd JJ whose character of 𝖠\mathsf{A} occurs in Γ\Gamma restricted to 𝖠\mathsf{A}. Every nonzero component of a vector of Γ\Gamma has m mod Km \bmod K in this list, and every listed residue occurs in some vector of Γ\Gamma.

    For a continuous group, families has one entry for each family with m, the mm of its kets: 0, n, -n or n, -n for ±n\pm n, and J, J-1, ..., -J in 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}.

14.1 Axial and continuous groups ¶

The field subduction_basis.status of an axial or continuous record is "closed-form". Each vector is one ket ∣ mJ⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle or a pair ∣ mJ⟩±∣ −mJ⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle \pm \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,-m}\bigr\rangle, by these rules, with η=(−1)J\eta = (-1)^J (they also apply at η=−(−1)J\eta = -(-1)^J, rule 6):

  1. Action. With w=P11+iP12w = P_{11} + \mathrm{i}P_{12} and sz=P33s_z = P_{33}, an element whose planar block A(𝘨)A(\mathsfit{g}) is a rotation acts by 𝘨∣ mJ⟩=wm∣ mJ⟩\mathsfit{g} \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle = w^m \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle (sz=+1s_z = +1) or η(−1)mwm∣ mJ⟩\eta (-1)^m w^m \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle (sz=−1s_z = -1). An element whose planar block is a reflection acts by 𝘨∣ mJ⟩=(−1)Jwm∣ −mJ⟩\mathsfit{g} \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle = (-1)^J w^m \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,-m}\bigr\rangle (sz=−1s_z = -1) or η(−1)J+mwm∣ −mJ⟩\eta (-1)^{J+m} w^m \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,-m}\bigr\rangle (sz=+1s_z = +1). Here wm=(w∗)∣m∣w^m = (w^*)^{\lvert m\rvert} for m<0m < 0. For the named operations (ψ\psi the azimuth of the 𝘊𝟤′\mathsfit{C_{2}'} axis, or of the horizontal direction in the mirror plane):

    (14.3)𝘙𝘻(ϕ) ∣ mJ⟩=e+imϕ ∣ mJ⟩σ𝘩 ∣ mJ⟩=η (−1)m ∣ mJ⟩𝘚𝟤N ∣ mJ⟩=η (−1)me+imπ/N ∣ mJ⟩𝘪 ∣ mJ⟩=η ∣ mJ⟩𝘊𝟤′(ψ) ∣ mJ⟩=(−1)Je2imψ ∣ −mJ⟩σ𝘷(ψ) ∣ mJ⟩=η (−1)J+me2imψ ∣ −mJ⟩.\begin{aligned} \mathsfit{R_{z}}(\phi)\, \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle &= \mathrm{e}^{+\mathrm{i}m\phi}\, \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle\\ \mathsfit{\sigma_{h}}\, \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle &= \eta\, (-1)^m\, \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle\\ \mathsfit{S_{2\mathnormal{N}}}\, \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle &= \eta\, (-1)^m \mathrm{e}^{+\mathrm{i}m\pi/N}\, \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle\\ \mathsfit{i}\, \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle &= \eta\, \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle\\ \mathsfit{C_{2}'}(\psi)\, \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle &= (-1)^J \mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}m\psi}\, \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,-m}\bigr\rangle\\ \mathsfit{\sigma_{v}}(\psi)\, \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle &= \eta\, (-1)^{J+m} \mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}m\psi}\, \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,-m}\bigr\rangle. \end{aligned}

    With η=(−1)J\eta = (-1)^J this action is that of section 4 on the spherical harmonics.

  2. Labels are read from the characters of this action: ∣ mJ⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle has χ(𝘊N)=ζN+m\chi(\mathsfit{C_{\mathnormal{N}}}) = \zeta_N^{+m}, so in 𝖢N\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}}} the ket ∣ +qJ⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,+q}\bigr\rangle carries 1Eq{}^1\mathrm{\scriptsize E}_q.
  3. Partner law. The blocks obey the partner law with the matrices character_table.irreps[].matrices.by_element (for a continuous group, the matrices of section 9 on each component). A one-dimensional block obeys its character.

  4. Partner order and phase. A two-dimensional block sits on {∣ mJ⟩,∣ −mJ⟩}\{\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle, \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,-m}\bigr\rangle\}, m≠0m \ne 0, and its irrep has the axial-monomial parameters (ℓ,δ)(\ell, \delta) of section 9. Its partner v1=∣ mJ⟩v_1 = \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle is the ket on which 𝖦∩𝖢∞𝗁\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{C_{\infty h}} acts as on (x−iy)ℓzδ(x - \mathrm{i}y)^\ell z^\delta:

    1. m≡−ℓ(modN)m \equiv -\ell \pmod{N} for 𝖢N𝗏\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}v}}, 𝖣N\mathsf{D_{\mathnormal{N}}}, 𝖣N𝗁\mathsf{D_{\mathnormal{N}h}}.
    2. N[η=−1]+(N+1)m≡Nδ−ℓ(mod2N)N[\eta = -1] + (N + 1) m \equiv N\delta - \ell \pmod{2N} for 𝖣N𝖽\mathsf{D_{\mathnormal{N}d}}.
    3. m=−nm = -n for En\mathrm{\scriptsize E}_n of 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}}, 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}}, 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}}.

    Here v1v_1 has e=+1e = +1, and the partner law forces v2=c∣ −mJ⟩v_2 = c \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,-m}\bigr\rangle, where c=(−1)J+δc = (-1)^{J+\delta} in 𝖣N\mathsf{D_{\mathnormal{N}}}, 𝖣N𝗁\mathsf{D_{\mathnormal{N}h}}, 𝖣N𝖽\mathsf{D_{\mathnormal{N}d}}, 𝖣∞\mathsf{D_{\infty}}, 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}} (flip 𝘊𝟤𝘹\mathsfit{C_{2x}}) and c=η(−1)J+mc = \eta (-1)^{J+m} in 𝖢N𝗏\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}v}}, 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}} (flip σ𝘷(0)\mathsfit{\sigma_{v}}(0)). A pair is ∣ mJ⟩+s∣ −mJ⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle + s \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,-m}\bigr\rangle with m>0m > 0 (em=1e_m = 1, e−m=se_{-m} = s), and every other single ket has e=+1e = +1. Both partners of a two-dimensional block are then multiplied by i\mathrm{i} when c=−(−1)mc = -(-1)^m, and a pair when s=−(−1)ms = -(-1)^m, so that time reversal acts on the block as in section 14. Every ee is therefore ±1\pm1 or ±i\pm\mathrm{i}. As the conjugate of ⟨𝐫∣ mJ⟩=rJYJm\bigl\langle\mathbf{r}\bigm\vert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle = r^J Y_J^m is (−1)m⟨𝐫∣ −mJ⟩(-1)^m \bigl\langle\mathbf{r}\bigm\vert{}^{J}_{\,-m}\bigr\rangle, a pair is 2Re⁡(rJYJm)2 \operatorname{Re}(r^J Y_J^m) when s=(−1)ms = (-1)^m and 2iIm⁡(rJYJm)2\mathrm{i} \operatorname{Im}(r^J Y_J^m) when s=−(−1)ms = -(-1)^m, so with its factor i\mathrm{i} it is −2Im⁡(rJYJm)-2 \operatorname{Im}(r^J Y_J^m). Every pair is thus a real solid harmonic. Up to constant factors, the functions of section 13 are such pairs and kets with m=0m = 0. In 𝖦=𝖠\mathsf{G} = \mathsf{A} the kets ∣ mJ⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle and ∣ −mJ⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,-m}\bigr\rangle, m>0m > 0, of one character of 𝖠\mathsf{A} give two pairs. Otherwise they carry the two irreps of a complex pair, whose blocks are the ket of the first irrep with e=1e = 1 and the ket of its partner with e=(−1)me = (-1)^m.

  5. Block order: by JJ, then by irrep in the record's order, then by κ\kappa. The index κ\kappa counts the blocks of one irrep at one JJ by ∣m∣\lvert m\rvert ascending, with +m+m before −m-m when 𝖦\mathsf{G} has no flip (below), and at one ∣m∣\lvert m\rvert the pair with s=+1s = +1 before s=−1s = -1. For a continuous group the irreps are its instances, by their index (section 16), null first, and then by family.
  6. Parity. The blocks are those of η=(−1)J\eta = (-1)^J. At η=−(−1)J\eta = -(-1)^J a block keeps its kets, and its label becomes Γ⊗det⁡𝖦\Gamma \otimes \det_{\mathsf{G}} (parity_label_map), where det⁡𝖦\det_{\mathsf{G}} is the determinant character, 𝘨↦det⁡𝘨\mathsfit{g} \mapsto \det \mathsfit{g}. Rule 4 is then applied again at that η\eta and can change the partner order, the sign of v2v_2 and the factor i\mathrm{i}.

In 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} the restriction of D(J,(−1)J)D^{(J,(-1)^J)} is irreducible, so the 2J+12J + 1 kets of one JJ form one block, of kind "all_kets": vμ=∣ mJ⟩v_\mu = \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle with μ=J−m+1\mu = J - m + 1 (the order of section 9) and every e=+1e = +1. Its irrep is D(J)D^{(J)} in 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)}, and D(J,+)D^{(J,+)} for even and D(J,−)D^{(J,-)} for odd JJ in 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}. At the other parity (rule 6) the block keeps its kets and its partner order, and its label is D(J)D^{(J)} again in 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and D(J,∓)D^{(J,\mp)} in 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}.

The field closed_form gives the basis for every JJ and NN of the family:

  3. cases: the kinds of block, {case, kind, dim, vectors}:

    1. single_ket: one ket, ∣ 0J⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,0}\bigr\rangle or, in 𝖦=𝖠\mathsf{G} = \mathsf{A}, the ket of an irrep of a complex pair.

    3. pm_pair: a pair ∣ mJ⟩±∣ −mJ⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle \pm \bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,-m}\bigr\rangle (rule 4), one block for each sign.
    4. all_kets: the 2J+12J + 1 kets of JJ, in 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}.

  4. multiplicity: bΓ(J)b_\Gamma(J) as a formula in JJ for even and odd JJ, by irrep (irrep its Mulliken label and irrep_id its id, in irrep order) or, for a continuous group, by family (family and label_template). 14.2 The files of 𝖳\mathsf{T}, 𝖮\mathsf{O} and 𝖨\mathsf{I} ¶

The file data/symmetry_adapted_bases/<G>/J<JJJ>.json.gz (J=0,1,…,60J = 0, 1, \dots, 60, <JJJ> three digits) holds the bases of D(J)D^{(J)} restricted to 𝖦\mathsf{G} with the action of section 4: {J, blocks, group, schema}. The repository holds the files J=0,1,…,10J = 0, 1, \dots, 10, and the files J=11,12,…,60J = 11, 12, \dots, 60 of every group are distributed separately (README.md) or computed exactly by the generator.

  1. blocks follow the record's irrep order, then copy κ=1,2,…\kappa = 1, 2, \dots. Each block has J, copy, dim, irrep (the record's irrep id), label (its label) and vectors, ordered by μ\mu (mu) =1,2,…,d= 1, 2, \dots, d.
  2. components are ordered by ascending mm, without those with em=0e_m = 0. The amplitude of ∣ mJ⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{J}_{\,m}\bigr\rangle is emrme_m \sqrt{r_m} with rm=Fm/totr_m = F_m/\mathrm{tot}, Fm=(J+m)! (J−m)!F_m = (J + m)!\,(J - m)!, tot=∑m∣em∣2Fm\mathrm{tot} = \sum_m \lvert e_m\rvert^2 F_m, so ∑m∣emrm∣2=1\sum_m \lvert e_m \sqrt{r_m}\rvert^2 = 1 exactly. All partners of one block have the same tot\mathrm{tot}. The scale of the eme_m of a vector is free: a positive rational factor cancels in rmr_m.
  3. eme_m and rmr_m are exact values not reduced to their minimal field. For 𝖳\mathsf{T}, 𝖳𝗁\mathsf{T_{h}}, 𝖳𝖽\mathsf{T_{d}}, 𝖮\mathsf{O} and 𝖮𝗁\mathsf{O_{h}} every eme_m is a cyc with ℳ=12\mathcal{M} = 12 (also when it lies in a smaller field), and every rmr_m is a q. For 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}, eme_m is a q or a cyc with ℳ∈{3,4,5,12,20,60}\mathcal{M} \in \{3, 4, 5, 12, 20, 60\}, and rmr_m is a q or a cyc with ℳ∈{5,12,20,60}\mathcal{M} \in \{5, 12, 20, 60\}. Every cyc is in the reduced power basis (len(c) =deg⁡Φℳ= \deg \Phi_{\mathcal{M}}).
  4. Partner law: D(J)(𝘨) vμ=∑νDν,μΓ(𝘨) vνD^{(J)}(\mathsfit{g})\, v_\mu = \sum_\nu D^\Gamma_{\nu,\mu}(\mathsfit{g})\, v_\nu for every element, with the record's matrices.
  5. Copies: vκ,1v_{\kappa,1} is vector κ\kappa of the reduced row echelon basis of the range of P1,1Γ=∑𝘨D1,1Γ(𝘨)∗ D(J)(𝘨)P^\Gamma_{1,1} = \sum_{\mathsfit{g}} D^\Gamma_{1,1}(\mathsfit{g})^*\, D^{(J)}(\mathsfit{g}), in the coordinates eme_m ordered from m=−Jm = -J. The index κ\kappa ascends with the pivot (the lowest mm at which the vector is nonzero), and vκ,μv_{\kappa,\mu} is Pμ,1ΓP^\Gamma_{\mu,1} applied to vκ,1v_{\kappa,1}. Up to a positive rational factor, the partners of the copies are the Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization of the vκ,μv_{\kappa,\mu} in κ\kappa order under ∑mem∗em′Fm\sum_m e_m^* e'_m F_m on the ee-coordinates eme_m, em′e'_m of two vectors. Its coefficients are computed on partner 1, and it leaves copy 1 unchanged. Partner μ\mu of copy κ\kappa is thus, up to that factor, ∑λ≤κaλvλ,μ\sum_{\lambda \le \kappa} a_\lambda v_{\lambda,\mu}, with the same aλa_\lambda for every partner and aκa_\kappa a positive rational. Partner 1 of copy κ\kappa has a positive rational coefficient at the pivot of vκ,1v_{\kappa,1}, where the copies before it are zero. The files are exact, while in floating point the echelon step loses about as many digits as the decimal logarithm of the condition number of its pivot rows. At J=60J = 60 this number is about a hundred million for 𝖮\mathsf{O}, 2500 for 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 3 for 𝖳\mathsf{T}.
  6. Time reversal: the files of this catalogue hold these copies adapted to time reversal (section 14). For a real irrep, the vectors of the range of P1,1ΓP^\Gamma_{1,1} that are partner 1 of an adapted block form a real vector space, the real form of the range. Its coordinates are Re⁡em\operatorname{Re} e_m and Im⁡em\operatorname{Im} e_m, ordered from m=−Jm = -J with the real part first. Its reduced row echelon basis over ℝ\mathbb{R} takes the place of the basis above, with the same Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization and real aλa_\lambda. A copy of a real irrep or of the first irrep of a complex pair is then multiplied by ±1\pm1, the sign of the phase form of the first nonzero coefficient of its partner 1. The copies of the second irrep of a complex pair are those of the first under time reversal.
  7. The blocks are orthonormal: each copy within itself, the copies of one irrep to one another, and the blocks of different irreps.

14.3 𝖳𝗁\mathsf{T_{h}}, 𝖮𝗁\mathsf{O_{h}}, 𝖳𝖽\mathsf{T_{d}} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}} ¶

The bases files of 𝖳𝗁\mathsf{T_{h}}, 𝖮𝗁\mathsf{O_{h}}, 𝖳𝖽\mathsf{T_{d}} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}} are those of 𝖳\mathsf{T}, 𝖮\mathsf{O}, 𝖮\mathsf{O} and 𝖨\mathsf{I}, relabeled by subduction_basis.computed_from of the derived record. This field has group (the parent record), l_even and l_odd (maps from the parent record's irrep labels to this record's, for even and odd JJ) and intertwiner. A parent block of irrep Γ′\Gamma' at JJ carries the matrices (det⁡𝘨)JDΓ′((det⁡𝘨) 𝘨)(\det \mathsfit{g})^J D^{\Gamma'}((\det \mathsfit{g})\, \mathsfit{g}). It becomes a block of the irrep Γ\Gamma to which l_even or l_odd maps Γ′\Gamma', the irrep with the character of these matrices. Where they equal the record's DΓ(𝘨)D^\Gamma(\mathsfit{g}) for every 𝘨\mathsfit{g}, the vectors are the parent record's. Otherwise the entry of Γ\Gamma in intertwiner.l_even or intertwiner.l_odd is the exact matrix SS with (det⁡𝘨)JDΓ′((det⁡𝘨) 𝘨) S=S DΓ(𝘨)(\det \mathsfit{g})^J D^{\Gamma'}((\det \mathsfit{g})\, \mathsfit{g})\, S = S\, D^\Gamma(\mathsfit{g}) for every 𝘨\mathsfit{g}, and partner μ\mu becomes wμ=∑νSν,μvνw_\mu = \sum_\nu S_{\nu,\mu} v_\nu. 14.4 The records' blocks, J≤10J \le 10 ¶

The field subduction_basis.enumerated of an exceptional record (J_min 0, J_max 10, block_count, vector_count) holds the vectors of the files for J≤10J \le 10. Its blocks of one JJ follow the record's irrep order, then κ\kappa. Each block has J, irrep (the irrep's label), display (the irrep's display, section 6.2), kappa, dim and vectors. The values are in their minimal field, and each vector is the file's vector divided by a positive rational, so that the coordinates of its coefficients are coprime integers. The amplitudes are unchanged (tot\mathrm{tot} is computed from the vector's own eme_m). A component is {m, e}. However, when a vector has two or more components and every coefficient is a rational multiple of one value that is not rational, that value is the vector's factor. The vector's components are then {m, q} with em=factor⋅qme_m = \texttt{factor} \cdot q_m (qm=[n,d]q_m = [n, d], here always an integer).

14.5 The crystal field ¶

The crystal field of a site of symmetry 𝖦\mathsf{G} [Bet29] is the operator ∑k,qBqk Cq(k)\sum_{k,q} B^k_q\, C^{(k)}_q, summed over the electrons. In the normalization of Wybourne [Wyb65] its tensors are Cq(k)=4π/(2k+1) YkqC^{(k)}_q = \sqrt{4\pi/(2k + 1)}\, Y_k^q [Jud63, eq. 2-25], and it is hermitian when B−qk=(−1)q (Bqk)∗B^k_{-q} = (-1)^q\, (B^k_q)^*. These tensors transform as the kets ∣ qk⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{k}_{\,q}\bigr\rangle (section 10.8), so the field is invariant when at each rank kk the vector ∑qBqk ∣ qk⟩\sum_q B^k_q\, \bigl\lvert{}^{k}_{\,q}\bigr\rangle is a combination of the blocks of the trivial irrep Γ\Gamma at J=kJ = k. These blocks are thus the crystal-field operators of the site. They number bΓ(k,(−1)k)b_\Gamma(k, (-1)^k) (section 11), and the field has as many real parameters at rank kk. A field of one block has as its BqkB^k_q the amplitudes emFm/tote_m \sqrt{F_m/\mathrm{tot}} of the block (section 14) at m=qm = q, times one factor. The block is invariant under time reversal (section 14), so this field is hermitian when the factor is real. For example, the blocks of 𝖮𝗁\mathsf{O_{h}} at k=4k = 4 and 6, of coefficients (1,14,1)(1, 14, 1) and (1,−2,1)(1, -2, 1) on m=−4,0,4m = -4, 0, 4, give B±44=5/14 B04B^4_{\pm 4} = \sqrt{5/14}\, B^4_0 and B±46=−7/2 B06B^6_{\pm 4} = -\sqrt{7/2}\, B^6_0.

Within one shell, beside the constant at k=0k = 0, the d electrons take the ranks k=2,4k = 2, 4 and the f electrons k=2,4,6k = 2, 4, 6 [Jud63, section 2-7]. Odd ranks occur only in a group without 𝘪\mathsfit{i}, and they join configurations of opposite parity. Thus the field of one shell has 1 parameter for d electrons and 2 for f electrons in 𝖮𝗁\mathsf{O_{h}}, 3 and 6 in 𝖣𝟥\mathsf{D_{3}}, and 2 and 3 in 𝖣𝟦𝖽\mathsf{D_{4d}}. These counts hold in the frame of section 3. A rotation 𝘙𝘻(ϕ)\mathsfit{R_{z}}(\phi) maps 𝖢N\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}}}, 𝖢N𝗁\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}h}} and 𝖲𝟤N\mathsf{S_{2\mathnormal{N}}} onto themselves and multiplies BqkB^k_q by eiqϕ\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}q\phi}, so a choice of the azimuth of xx can make one complex BqkB^k_q real. In 𝖢𝟣\mathsf{C_{1}} and 𝖢𝗂\mathsf{C_{i}} the rotations of the frame remove three parameters.

The binary rotation 𝘊𝟤𝘹\mathsfit{C_{2x}} carries ∣ qk⟩\bigl\lvert{}^{k}_{\,q}\bigr\rangle to (−1)k∣ −qk⟩(-1)^k \bigl\lvert{}^{k}_{\,-q}\bigr\rangle (section 14.1). With the hermiticity it makes BqkB^k_q real when k+qk + q is even and imaginary when k+qk + q is odd. Thus in the frame of section 3, with 𝘊𝟤′\mathsfit{C_{2}'} along xx, the fields of 𝖣𝟥\mathsf{D_{3}} and 𝖣𝟥𝖽\mathsf{D_{3d}} have imaginary B34B^4_3 and B36B^6_3, which the binary axes along yy of section 6.5 make real. The mirror σ𝘷(0)\mathsfit{\sigma_{v}}(0) of 𝖢N𝗏\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{N}v}} makes every BqkB^k_q real.
