10 Fusion and Clebsch–Gordan coefficients ¶

10.1 Fusion, finite groups ¶

fusion (kind "table"): irreps (the ids in irrep order), n_irreps, identity (the trivial irrep) and products, one per pair Γ1≤Γ2\Gamma_1 \le \Gamma_2 in irrep order (Γ2⊗Γ1=Γ1⊗Γ2\Gamma_2 \otimes \Gamma_1 = \Gamma_1 \otimes \Gamma_2 is not repeated). Each has a =Γ1= \Gamma_1, b =Γ2= \Gamma_2, diagonal (Γ1=Γ2\Gamma_1 = \Gamma_2) and terms, the decomposition Γ1⊗Γ2=⨁mult Γ\Gamma_1 \otimes \Gamma_2 = \bigoplus \texttt{mult}\,\Gamma in irrep order. A diagonal entry also has symmetric and antisymmetric, the decompositions of the two squares, of characters (χ(𝘨)2±χ(𝘨2))/2(\chi(\mathsfit{g})^2 \pm \chi(\mathsfit{g}^2))/2. display writes terms with the irreps' labels joined by + in irrep order, a multiplicity above 1 as a prefix, and on the diagonal the antisymmetric part of each term in braces after its symmetric part ("A_1 + {A_2} + B_1 + B_2" for E⊗E\mathrm{\scriptsize E} \otimes \mathrm{\scriptsize E} of 𝖢𝟦𝗏\mathsf{C_{4v}}).

10.2 Fusion, continuous groups ¶

fusion (kind "family-law"): families (the family ids), family_labels (their label templates), identity (the trivial irrep: a family id, or in 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} the instance reference of section 2.2, {"family": "D", "index": 0} and {"family": "Dg", "index": 0}), and rules, one per unordered pair of families and case: a and b (family, with index for a family with an index), condition, terms and display. A term has family, mult and either index (one irrep; no index for a one-member family) or index_range (symbol, from, to, step: the direct sum of the irreps at symbol == from, from + step, …, to, empty when to << from, as in the antisymmetric square of D(0)D^{(0)}). index_symbols names the indices of a, of b and of a square; every index, from and to is one of the record's index_grammar, and every condition one of its condition_grammar. In 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} the index symbols are J1, J2 and J, the indices J1, J2, J, |J1-J2|, J1+J2, 0, 1, 2J, 2J-1 and the one condition "always"; in the other five they are m, n and n, the indices m, n, m+n, m-n, n-m, |m-n|, 2n and the conditions "always", "m == n", "m != n", "m > n", "m < n". squares gives the symmetric and antisymmetric squares of each family, as in 10.1; a square's display writes the symmetric part, then the antisymmetric part in braces.

10.3 Clebsch–Gordan coefficients, finite groups ¶

clebsch_gordan (kind "table") has irreps and products, in the order of fusion.products. Each product Γ1⊗Γ2\Gamma_1 \otimes \Gamma_2 has a =Γ1= \Gamma_1, b =Γ2= \Gamma_2 and couplings, one per (Γ,κ)(\Gamma, \kappa) in the order of its terms: c (the id of the irrep Γ\Gamma), kappa (κ=1,2,…\kappa = 1, 2, \dots numbering the copies of Γ\Gamma), N, partners and separation (below). A coupling stores the block CC of dΓ1dΓ2d_{\Gamma_1} d_{\Gamma_2} rows and dΓd_\Gamma columns with

(10.1)(DΓ1(𝘨)⊗DΓ2(𝘨)) C=C DΓ(𝘨)for every 𝘨,C[(μ1,μ2),μ]=⟨ μ1Γ1  μ2Γ2∣ μΓ;κ⟩,(D^{\Gamma_1}(\mathsfit{g}) \otimes D^{\Gamma_2}(\mathsfit{g}))\, C = C\, D^\Gamma(\mathsfit{g}) \quad \text{for every } \mathsfit{g}, \qquad C[(\mu_1,\mu_2), \mu] = \bigl\langle{}^{\Gamma_1}_{\,\mu_1}\,{}^{\Gamma_2}_{\,\mu_2}\bigm\vert{}^{\Gamma}_{\,\mu};\kappa\bigr\rangle,

with the matrices of section 9 and row (μ1,μ2)(\mu_1, \mu_2) at position (μ1−1)dΓ2+(μ2−1)(\mu_1 - 1) d_{\Gamma_2} + (\mu_2 - 1) (Kronecker order, Γ1\Gamma_1-major). As kets, ∣ μΓ;κ⟩=∑μ1,μ2∣ μ1Γ1⟩∣ μ2Γ2⟩ C[(μ1,μ2),μ]\bigl\lvert{}^{\Gamma}_{\,\mu};\kappa\bigr\rangle = \sum_{\mu_1,\mu_2} \bigl\lvert{}^{\Gamma_1}_{\,\mu_1}\bigr\rangle\bigl\lvert{}^{\Gamma_2}_{\,\mu_2}\bigr\rangle\, C[(\mu_1,\mu_2), \mu] and 𝘨∣ μΓ⟩=∑ν∣ νΓ⟩ Dν,μΓ(𝘨)\mathsfit{g}\bigl\lvert{}^{\Gamma}_{\,\mu}\bigr\rangle = \sum_\nu \bigl\lvert{}^{\Gamma}_{\,\nu}\bigr\rangle\, D^\Gamma_{\nu,\mu}(\mathsfit{g}). Over all (Γ,κ)(\Gamma, \kappa) of one product the blocks form a unitary matrix.

  1. partners: per μ\mu (mu), the nonzero entries of column μ\mu of ℰ\mathcal{E} as terms (i =μ1= \mu_1, k =μ2= \mu_2, e), in row order; an entry that is not listed is 0.
  2. N: C=ℰ/𝒩C = \mathcal{E}/\sqrt{\mathcal{N}} with ℰ\mathcal{E} exact and 𝒩\mathcal{N} a positive element of the real subfield, one per block, and ℰ†ℰ=𝒩 1\mathcal{E}^\dagger \mathcal{E} = \mathcal{N}\,1; the square root stays implicit.
  3. Phase: the first nonzero entry of column μ=1\mu = 1, in row order, is real and positive.
  4. Scale: the power-basis coordinates of all the entries of ℰ\mathcal{E} are coprime integers.

When Γ1\Gamma_1 and Γ2\Gamma_2 are one-dimensional, the product has one block, C=(1)C = (1) with 𝒩=1\mathcal{N} = 1: its coefficient is ⟨Γ1 Γ2  ∣  Γ⟩=1\langle \Gamma_1\,\Gamma_2\;\vert\;\Gamma\rangle = 1, and ∣Γ⟩=∣Γ1⟩∣Γ2⟩\lvert\Gamma\rangle = \lvert\Gamma_1\rangle\lvert\Gamma_2\rangle.

10.4 Multiplicities ¶

Only 𝖳\mathsf{T}, 𝖳𝗁\mathsf{T_{h}}, 𝖨\mathsf{I} and 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}} have an irrep Γ\Gamma with multiplicity 2 in Γ1⊗Γ2\Gamma_1 \otimes \Gamma_2. The two copies are separated by the first of these rules that separates them; when 𝘪∈𝖦\mathsfit{i} \in \mathsf{G} the rules are applied on 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}, where Γ1=Γ2\Gamma_1 = \Gamma_2 means that the two irreps have equal matrices on 𝖦∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{G} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}.

  1. exchange (Γ1=Γ2\Gamma_1 = \Gamma_2): C[(μ1,μ2),μ]=±C[(μ2,μ1),μ]C[(\mu_1,\mu_2), \mu] = \pm C[(\mu_2,\mu_1), \mu];
  2. crossing (Γ2=Γ\Gamma_2 = \Gamma, else Γ1=Γ\Gamma_1 = \Gamma): with 𝒥≠0\mathcal{J} \ne 0 the matrix with DΓ(𝘨) 𝒥=𝒥 DΓ(𝘨)∗D^\Gamma(\mathsfit{g})\, \mathcal{J} = \mathcal{J}\, D^\Gamma(\mathsfit{g})^* for every 𝘨\mathsfit{g}, the tensor Tμ1,μ2,ν=∑μC[(μ1,μ2),μ] 𝒥ν,μT_{\mu_1,\mu_2,\nu} = \sum_\mu C[(\mu_1,\mu_2),\mu]\, \mathcal{J}_{\nu,\mu} is symmetric or antisymmetric under the exchange of the two equal slots (μ2↔ν\mu_2 \leftrightarrow \nu when Γ2=Γ\Gamma_2 = \Gamma, μ1↔ν\mu_1 \leftrightarrow \nu when Γ1=Γ\Gamma_1 = \Gamma);
  3. 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} parentage (Γ1=Γ2=Γ\Gamma_1 = \Gamma_2 = \Gamma with both copies exchange-symmetric: H⊗H→2 H\mathrm{\scriptsize H} \otimes \mathrm{\scriptsize H} \to 2\,\mathrm{\scriptsize H} of 𝖨\mathsf{I} and its images in 𝖨𝗁\mathsf{I_{h}}): with VV the 3-dimensional irrep whose character is the trace of the rotation part, and JxJ_x, JyJ_y, JzJ_z the operators on Γ\Gamma fixed up to one common scalar by the one-dimensional Hom⁡(V⊗Γ,Γ)\operatorname{Hom}(V \otimes \Gamma, \Gamma), the first copy satisfies (Jx⊗1+1⊗Jx) C=C Jx(J_x \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes J_x)\, C = C\, J_x, likewise for JyJ_y and JzJ_z, and the second is its orthogonal complement.

The symmetric copy, or the lowest parent, is κ=1\kappa = 1. separation names the rule and the outcome: "unique" (Γ\Gamma occurs once), "exchange-symmetric", "exchange-antisymmetric", "crossing-symmetric", "crossing-antisymmetric", "so3-parent-lowest", "so3-parent-complement".

clebsch_gordan of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} and 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} (kind "table", group "SO(3)" or "O(3)") has rules, the couplings in words (the decomposition, the convention, the layout below, the closed form of van der Waerden and Racah, the symmetries and, in 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}, the parity rule), and the tables of D(J1)⊗D(J2)D^{(J_1)} \otimes D^{(J_2)} for J1≤J2≤J_1 \le J_2 \le J_max_tabulated (10), in the layout of section 10.3 with these differences:

10.7 Power spectrum and bispectrum ¶

The records hold no spectra; they follow from the couplings of 10.6. A function on the unit sphere, f=∑J,mfmJ YJmf = \sum_{J,m} f^J_m\, Y_J^m, with fJf^J the vector of its fmJf^J_m in the order of section 9, has the power spectrum ∥fJ∥2\lVert f^J\rVert^2 and the bispectrum [BKC13, eq. 22]

(10.4)BJ1,J2,J(f)=∑m1,m2,M⟨ m1J1  m2J2∣ MJ⟩ fm1J1 fm2J2 (fMJ)∗,B_{J_1,J_2,J}(f) = \sum_{m_1,m_2,M} \bigl\langle{}^{J_1}_{\,m_1}\,{}^{J_2}_{\,m_2}\bigm\vert{}^{J}_{\,M}\bigr\rangle\, f^{J_1}_{m_1}\, f^{J_2}_{m_2}\, (f^J_M)^*,

the coefficients those of 10.6 (stored for J1≤J2≤10J_1 \le J_2 \le 10; BJ1,J2,J=(−1)J1+J2−JBJ2,J1,JB_{J_1,J_2,J} = (-1)^{J_1+J_2-J} B_{J_2,J_1,J}). An element 𝘨\mathsfit{g} acts on fJf^J as D(J,(−1)J)(𝘨)D^{(J,(-1)^J)}(\mathsfit{g}), so BJ1,J2,J(𝘨f)=(det⁡𝘨)J1+J2+JBJ1,J2,J(f)B_{J_1,J_2,J}(\mathsfit{g} f) = (\det \mathsfit{g})^{J_1+J_2+J} B_{J_1,J_2,J}(f); without the conjugate the sum is not invariant. If 𝘨f=f\mathsfit{g} f = f for every 𝘨\mathsfit{g} of a group 𝖦\mathsf{G}, then fJ=0f^J = 0 when bΓ(J,(−1)J)=0b_\Gamma(J, (-1)^J) = 0 for the trivial irrep Γ\Gamma (section 11), and if 𝖦\mathsf{G} has an element of determinant −1-1, BJ1,J2,J(f)=0B_{J_1,J_2,J}(f) = 0 whenever J1+J2+JJ_1 + J_2 + J is odd.
