7 Reality of irreps ¶

frobenius_schur is the Frobenius–Schur indicator νFS=∣𝖦∣−1∑𝘨χ(𝘨2)\nu_{\mathrm{FS}} = \lvert \mathsf{G}\rvert^{-1} \sum_{\mathsfit{g}} \chi(\mathsfit{g}^2), computed with power_map and the class sizes; the continuous families carry the same field. The irreps are irreducible over ℂ\mathbb{C} (section 1), and νFS\nu_{\mathrm{FS}} gives the reality, or type, of an irrep Γ\Gamma of dimension dd, which says what Γ\Gamma is over ℝ\mathbb{R}:

  1. νFS=+1\nu_{\mathrm{FS}} = +1, real: Γ\Gamma has a basis in which every matrix is real (the matrices of section 9 need not be in it), and in it Γ\Gamma is an irrep over ℝ\mathbb{R} of dimension dd;
  2. νFS=0\nu_{\mathrm{FS}} = 0, complex: the character is not real, and Γ\Gamma and its conjugate are two irreps (section 6.4); together they form one irrep over ℝ\mathbb{R} of dimension 2d2d and character 2Re⁡χΓ2\operatorname{Re}\chi_\Gamma;
  3. νFS=−1\nu_{\mathrm{FS}} = -1, pseudoreal (quaternionic): the character is real, but no basis makes every matrix real; Γ\Gamma read as a real space of dimension 2d2d is one irrep over ℝ\mathbb{R}, of character 2χΓ2\chi_\Gamma.

So every irrep over ℂ\mathbb{C} lies in exactly one irrep over ℝ\mathbb{R} (it is a constituent of that irrep's complexification), and every irrep over ℝ\mathbb{R} comes from one real irrep, one complex pair or one pseudoreal irrep. The type does not change over ℝ\mathbb{R}: the real matrices that commute with every matrix of that irrep over ℝ\mathbb{R} form a copy of the real numbers, of the complex numbers or of the quaternions as Γ\Gamma is real, complex or pseudoreal. No irrep of the 98 groups is pseudoreal. The representations are vector representations, for which time reversal Θ\Theta has Θ2=+1\Theta^2 = +1: a real irrep keeps its degeneracy, a pseudoreal one doubles it, and a complex one pairs with its conjugate, so that the degeneracy is the dimension of the irrep over ℝ\mathbb{R}.

The table of irreps of a finite document also gives each irrep's kernel ker⁡Γ\ker\Gamma, the normal subgroup of the elements whose character is dd, those that Γ\Gamma maps to the identity, by its name in the subgroup lattice (section 15.1), and its image, the group that the matrices of Γ\Gamma form, isomorphic to 𝖦/ker⁡Γ\mathsf{G}/\ker\Gamma, by the name of a point group: for d=1d = 1 the cyclic group of the characters, roots of unity that turn the complex plane; for d=2d = 2 and d=3d = 3 the group of the matrices in a real basis, in 𝖮(𝟤)\mathsf{O(2)} acting on the plane or in 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}, the image of the level (section 19); for d=5d = 5 the subgroup of the 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} or 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} through which the chain of the level passes; for d=4d = 4 the point group isomorphic to it, the matrices lying in 𝖲𝖮(𝟦)\mathsf{SO(4)}. Isomorphic groups may have different names: an improper group without 𝘪\mathsfit{i} is isomorphic to the proper group of its rotations and of 𝘪\mathsfit{i} times its improper elements [Lax74, section 2.10], as 𝖢𝟥𝗏\mathsf{C_{3v}} is to 𝖣𝟥\mathsf{D_{3}}, and an image has the name of the group that its matrices form. The table of irrep families of a continuous document gives both by the index: the kernel of En,g\mathrm{\scriptsize E}_{n,\mathrm{g}} of 𝖢∞𝗁\mathsf{C_{\infty h}} and 𝖣∞𝗁\mathsf{D_{\infty h}} is 𝖢n𝗁\mathsf{C_{\mathnormal{n}h}} for even nn and 𝖲𝟤n\mathsf{S_{2\mathnormal{n}}} for odd nn, that of En,u\mathrm{\scriptsize E}_{n,\mathrm{u}} the other of the two; the image of a family of dimension 2 is 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}}, 𝖮(𝟤)\mathsf{O(2)} acting on the plane, and the kernel of D(J)D^{(J)}, J≥1J \ge 1, is 𝖢𝟣\mathsf{C_{1}} (𝖢𝗂\mathsf{C_{i}} for D(J,+)D^{(J,+)} of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)}), its image 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} (𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} for D(J,−)D^{(J,-)}).
