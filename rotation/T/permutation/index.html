4.1 Figures ¶

Data: permutation_representations, subgroup_lattice in JSON · GAP · Wolfram Language · SymPy

Conventions: sections 12.4 to 12.6. Every class of subgroups 𝖧\mathsf{H} is the stabilizer of a figure in space, the subgroup of the elements that carry it into itself. The group 𝖳\mathsf{T} permutes the N=[𝖳:𝖧]N = [\mathsf{T} : \mathsf{H}] copies of the figure as it permutes the right cosets 𝖧ξj\mathsf{H}\mathsfit{\xi}_j, j=0,1,…,N−1j = 0, 1, \dots, N - 1, ordered by the indices of their least elements ξj\mathsfit{\xi}_j (ξ0=𝘌\mathsfit{\xi}_0 = \mathsfit{E}). The copy jj is the figure moved by ξj\mathsfit{\xi}_j. The matrices P(𝘨)P(\mathsfit{g}) of the defining representation of 𝖳\mathsf{T} (section 1.2) generate the copies, and the root point 𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0 of the figure moves to the point 𝐫j=𝐫0 P(ξj)\mathbf{r}_j = \mathbf{r}_0\, P(\mathsfit{\xi}_j) of the copy jj. The element 𝘨\mathsfit{g} moves the copy jj to the copy j′j', where 𝖧ξj𝘨=𝖧ξj′\mathsf{H}\mathsfit{\xi}_j \mathsfit{g} = \mathsf{H}\mathsfit{\xi}_{j'}. Its permutation matrix D(𝘨)D(\mathsfit{g}) has the one nonzero entry of row jj in column j′j',

The record holds the coordinates of the points of every copy, the images j′j' of every copy under every element and the table of marks (Conventions, sections 12.5 and 12.6). The figure of a class is the first of these whose stabilizer is 𝖧\mathsf{H}: the point 𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0, the line through the origin along 𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0, a spin, a spinning arrow and a helix. Otherwise the figure is the least orbit of 𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0 under 𝖧\mathsf{H} whose stabilizer is 𝖧\mathsf{H}, a segment, a polygon or a polyhedron. A spin is the axial vector 𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0, a sense of rotation about its line. A spinning arrow is the arrow 𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0 with a sense of rotation about itself, and a helix is the line along 𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0 with a handedness. These two figures are tried only when 𝖧\mathsf{H} has no improper element and 𝖳\mathsf{T} has one. The table lists the classes of subgroups in the order and with the names of the table of section 3.1. A row gives NN, the figure by its shape or number of points, the stabilizer of the point 𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0 (site), which tells the symmetry elements of 𝖳\mathsf{T} that the figure lies on, and 𝐫0\mathbf{r}_0. The column vertices gives what the points of all the copies form (the tips of the arrows and spins, both ends of the lines and helices), with ×k{\times}k when k>1k > 1 copies pass through each. The rank counts the classes of pairs of copies under 𝖳\mathsf{T}, including that of a copy with itself. For a point, line or spin of rank at most 5, the column cos gives the cosine of the angle between two copies (of the acute angle, for lines) for each remaining class of pairs. The column hand gives 2 when 𝖧\mathsf{H} has no improper element and 𝖳\mathsf{T} has one, so that the rotations of 𝖳\mathsf{T} leave the copies in two classes, mirror images of each other. These classes are the left- and the right-handed copies of a chiral figure.
