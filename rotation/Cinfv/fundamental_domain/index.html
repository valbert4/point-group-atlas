Conventions: sections 4.2 and 17. A molecule with symmetry 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}} placed by 𝘘∈𝖮(𝟥)\mathsfit{Q} \in \mathsf{O(3)} is the same set of atoms when placed by 𝘨𝘘\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q} with 𝘨∈𝖢∞𝗏\mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{C_{\infty v}}, since P(𝘨𝘘)=P(𝘨) P(𝘘)P(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q}) = P(\mathsfit{g})\,P(\mathsfit{Q}). Thus its orientations are the cosets 𝖢∞𝗏𝘘={𝘨𝘘:𝘨∈𝖢∞𝗏}\mathsf{C_{\infty v}}\mathsfit{Q} = \{\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q} : \mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{C_{\infty v}}\}, the points of the space 𝖢∞𝗏\𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{C_{\infty v}}\backslash\mathsf{O(3)}. On column vectors a coset is the left coset of the transposes P(𝘘)⊤P(𝘨)⊤P(\mathsfit{Q})^{\top}P(\mathsfit{g})^{\top}, 𝘨∈𝖢∞𝗏\mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{C_{\infty v}}, a point of 𝖮(𝟥)/𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{O(3)}/\mathsf{C_{\infty v}}. Volumes are those of the invariant metric of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} in which the distance from 𝘌\mathsfit{E} to a rotation by ω≤π\omega \le \pi is ω\omega, so that vol⁡𝖲𝖮(𝟥)=8π2\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{SO(3)} = 8\pi^2 and vol⁡𝖮(𝟥)=16π2\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{O(3)} = 16\pi^2. The group 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}} is the union of the circles 𝘊∞(ϕ)\mathsfit{C_{\infty}}(\phi) and σ𝘷(ψ)\mathsfit{\sigma_v}(\psi), ϕ∈[0,2π)\phi \in [0, 2\pi) and ψ∈[0,π)\psi \in [0, \pi), each of length 2π2\pi, so that vol⁡𝖢∞𝗏=4π\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{C_{\infty v}} = 4\pi.

The Euler angles of the rotation part (det⁡𝘘) 𝘘(\det \mathsfit{Q})\,\mathsfit{Q} are (α,β,γ)(\alpha,\beta,\gamma): by α\alpha about zz, then by β\beta about yy, then by γ\gamma about zz (fixed axes). In these angles the volume element of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} is sin⁡β dα dβ dγ\sin\beta\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}\beta\,\mathrm{d}\gamma. As 𝘊∞(ϕ) 𝘘\mathsfit{C_{\infty}}(\phi)\,\mathsfit{Q} has the angles (α+ϕ,β,γ)(\alpha + \phi, \beta, \gamma), a coset of 𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty}} is a circle of α\alpha, and (β,γ)(\beta, \gamma) are the polar and azimuthal angles of the axis (0,0,1)P((det⁡𝘘) 𝘘)(0, 0, 1)P((\det \mathsfit{Q})\,\mathsfit{Q}) of the molecule. Thus the cosets have the metric of the unit sphere of the axes, of area element sin⁡β dβ dγ\sin\beta\,\mathrm{d}\beta\,\mathrm{d}\gamma. The cell 𝒟\mathcal{D} of the rotations with α=0\alpha = 0, β∈[0,π]\beta \in [0, \pi] and γ∈(−π,π]\gamma \in (-\pi, \pi] meets every coset of 𝖢∞𝗏∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)=𝖢∞\mathsf{C_{\infty v}} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)} = \mathsf{C_{\infty}} once, except on its boundary (β=0\beta = 0 and β=π\beta = \pi), and, in the metric of the cosets, vol⁡𝒟=4π\operatorname{vol}\mathcal{D} = 4\pi.

As 𝖢∞𝗏\mathsf{C_{\infty v}} contains an improper element, every coset 𝖢∞𝗏𝘘\mathsf{C_{\infty v}}\mathsfit{Q} meets 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} in one coset of 𝖢∞𝗏∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{C_{\infty v}} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)}, so that ℱ=𝒟\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{D} is a fundamental domain. It contains one element of every coset, more only on its boundary. The space 𝖢∞𝗏\𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{C_{\infty v}}\backslash\mathsf{O(3)} is 𝖢∞\𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{C_{\infty}}\backslash\mathsf{SO(3)}, the sphere 𝖲2\mathsf{S}^{2}, and
