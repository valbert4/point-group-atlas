In this table, type is I when every element of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} is a rotation, II when 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} contains the inversion 𝘪\mathsfit{i} and III otherwise; polar is yes when 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} fixes a nonzero vector; crystallographers call a group chiral, or enantiomorphic, when its type is I, and centrosymmetric when it contains 𝘪\mathsfit{i}; laue_class is the group that 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} and 𝘪\mathsfit{i} generate. In product_form, after a semidirect product, ×∞{\times}\infty marks infinitely many subgroups conjugate to its second factor; the braces hold the classes of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} that the finite second factor meets: atoms of the class space (section 1.2) and distinguished points of its arcs (Conventions, section 16).
