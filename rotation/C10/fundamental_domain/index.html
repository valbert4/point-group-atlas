A molecule with symmetry 𝖢𝟣𝟢\mathsf{C_{10}} placed by 𝘘∈𝖮(𝟥)\mathsfit{Q} \in \mathsf{O(3)} is the same set of atoms when placed by 𝘨𝘘\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q} with 𝘨∈𝖢𝟣𝟢\mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{C_{10}}, since P(𝘨𝘘)=P(𝘨) P(𝘘)P(\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q}) = P(\mathsfit{g})\,P(\mathsfit{Q}). Thus its orientations are the cosets 𝖢𝟣𝟢𝘘={𝘨𝘘:𝘨∈𝖢𝟣𝟢}\mathsf{C_{10}}\mathsfit{Q} = \{\mathsfit{g}\mathsfit{Q} : \mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{C_{10}}\}, the points of the space 𝖢𝟣𝟢\𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{C_{10}}\backslash\mathsf{O(3)}. On column vectors a coset is the left coset of the transposes P(𝘘)⊤P(𝘨)⊤P(\mathsfit{Q})^{\top}P(\mathsfit{g})^{\top}, 𝘨∈𝖢𝟣𝟢\mathsfit{g} \in \mathsf{C_{10}}, a point of 𝖮(𝟥)/𝖢𝟣𝟢\mathsf{O(3)}/\mathsf{C_{10}}. Volumes are those of the invariant metric of 𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{O(3)} in which the distance from 𝘌\mathsfit{E} to a rotation by ω≤π\omega \le \pi is ω\omega, so that vol⁡𝖲𝖮(𝟥)=8π2\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{SO(3)} = 8\pi^2 and vol⁡𝖮(𝟥)=16π2\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{O(3)} = 16\pi^2. A finite group has the counting measure, vol⁡𝖢𝟣𝟢=∣𝖢𝟣𝟢∣=10\operatorname{vol}\mathsf{C_{10}} = |\mathsf{C_{10}}| = 10.

The rotations by π\pi lie at infinity, and the volume element of 𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{SO(3)} is 8 (1+∥𝝆∥2)−2 d3𝝆8\,(1 + \lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}\rVert^2)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}^3\boldsymbol{\rho}. The rotations nearer to 𝘌\mathsfit{E} than to any other element of 𝖢𝟣𝟢∩𝖲𝖮(𝟥)\mathsf{C_{10}} \cap \mathsf{SO(3)} form the cell
